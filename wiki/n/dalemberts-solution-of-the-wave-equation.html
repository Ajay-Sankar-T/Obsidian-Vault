D'Alembert's Solution of the Wave Equation

What it is

For the one-dimensional wave equation on the whole line

utt​=c2uxx​,x∈R, t>0,u(x,0)=f(x), ut​(x,0)=g(x),

d'Alembert's formula gives the solution in closed form:

u(x,t)=2f(x+ct)+f(x−ct)​+2c1​∫x−ctx+ct​g(s)ds​

Derivation

The characteristic equation dx2−c2dt2=0 gives ξ=x+ct, η=x−ct (Canonical Forms of Second Order PDEs). Then utt​−c2uxx​=−4c2uξη​=0, so

u=F(x+ct)+G(x−ct):

a wave moving left plus a wave moving right, each rigid, at speed c. The initial data give F+G=f and c(F′−G′)=g; integrating the second, F−G=c1​∫g. Solving for F and G and adding gives the formula.

What it says

  • Travelling waves. f(x−ct) is the profile f shifted right by ct.
  • Domain of dependence. u(x0​,t0​) depends only on f at the two points x0​±ct0​ and on g over the interval between them; changes elsewhere cannot reach (x0​,t0​) (finite speed c).
  • Range of influence. Data at x0​ influence only ∣x−x0​∣≤ct.
  • Discontinuities travel along characteristics and are not smoothed (contrast the heat equation).

Example. u(x,0)=sinx, ut​(x,0)=cosx: u=sinxcosct+c1​cosxsinct. With f=x, g=4cx: u=x(1+4ct). With f=x(1−x), g=sinπx: u=x(1−x)−c2t2+πc1​sinπxsinπct.

x>0: reflection">

Half-line x>0: reflection

For x>0 with a boundary condition at x=0, extend the data to the whole line and apply the formula:

Boundary condition at x=0 Extension of f,g Effect on a pulse
u(0,t)=0 (fixed end) odd: f(−x)=−f(x), g(−x)=−g(x) reflects with the sign flipped
ux​(0,t)=0 (free end) even: f(−x)=f(x), g(−x)=g(x) reflects with the same sign

Then for x>ct the plain formula holds; for 0<x<ct one of the arguments x−ct is negative and the extension is used. By symmetry u(0,t)=0 (odd) or ux​(0,t)=0 (even) holds automatically.

Compatibility. For a fixed end, f(0)=0 and g(0)=0 are needed for a continuous solution. If f(0)=0, the odd extension jumps at 0 and the solution has a jump along x=ct. For f=e−x, g=ex (so f(0)=g(0)=1), the solution is

u={e−xcoshct+c1​exsinhct,21​(e−x−ct−ex−ct)+2c1​(ex+ct−ect−x),​x≥ct0<x<ct​

with a jump of size 1 across x=ct (a weak solution).

Traps
  • The formula is for time-independent coefficient, infinite line. On a finite interval reflections never stop: use Wave Equation on a Finite Interval.
  • Forgetting the 2c1​, or integrating g over [x−t,x+t] instead of [x−ct,x+ct].
  • Odd vs even: fixed end ⇒ odd, free end ⇒ even.

Cross-domain links

  • The Universal Second-Order Linear System — a string is a chain of coupled oscillators; d'Alembert's solution is the continuum limit, in which the characteristic speed c=T/ρ​ replaces the natural frequency.
  • Transmission lines obey the same equation (vtt​=c2vxx​ with v the voltage). The odd/even rule is the reflection coefficient Γ=(ZL​−Z0​)/(ZL​+Z0​): a short circuit (ZL​=0, voltage forced to zero, like a fixed end) gives Γ=−1 (odd extension), an open circuit (ZL​→∞, like a free end) gives Γ=+1 (even extension). Compare Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance for impedance matching.
