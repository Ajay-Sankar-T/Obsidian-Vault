T6-Q5 — Difference Amplifier

EE1100op-amptutorial-solutiondifference-amplifier

Hub: Tutorial 6 on OP-AMP · Previous: T6-Q4 - Inverting Summer · Next: T6-Q6 - Integrator

Fig. 5: V1​ and V2​ each through 8 kΩ to the inverting and non-inverting inputs; feedback 120 kΩ; 120 kΩ from the + input to ground. Find Vo​ for (a) V1​=4 mV, V2​=0; (b) V1​=0, V2​=6 mV; (c) V1​=40 mV, V2​=30 mV; (d) V1​=25 mV, V2​=40 mV. [(a) −60 mV (b) 90 mV (c) −150 mV (d) 225 mV]

Tests: difference amplifier, superposition.

Given

R1​=R3​=8 kΩ, R2​=R4​=120 kΩ (the two ratios are equal: R2​/R1​=R4​/R3​=15).

Principle and why it applies

Superposition on an ideal op-amp. With V2​=0 it is an inverting stage (−R1​R2​​V1​). With V1​=0 it is a non-inverting stage fed through the divider R3​+R4​R4​​.

Solution

Vo​=(1+R1​R2​​)R3​+R4​R4​​V2​−R1​R2​​V1​=16⋅128120​V2​−15V1​=15(V2​−V1​).

V1​ V2​ Vo​=15(V2​−V1​)
(a) 4 mV 0 −60 mV
(b) 0 6 mV +90 mV
(c) 40 mV 30 mV −150 mV
(d) 25 mV 40 mV +225 mV

Result

Vo​=15(V2​−V1​): (a) −60 mV, (b) 90 mV, (c) −150 mV, (d) 225 mV.

Check

(c) and (d) show that only the difference matters: a common-mode shift of the two inputs changes nothing (the ratio-matching condition). With mismatched resistors a common-mode part leaks into the output (CMRR of the circuit).

Trap / alternative

Using the inverting gain −15 for both inputs: the V2​ path has a positive gain. Also the divider 8+120120​ and the factor (1+15) cancel to exactly 15 only when R2​/R1​=R4​/R3​.
