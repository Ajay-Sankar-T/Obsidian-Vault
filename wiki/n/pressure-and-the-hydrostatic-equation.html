Pressure and the Hydrostatic Equation

Pressure

P=AF​​,1 Pa=1 N/m2​

The hydrostatic equation

Balancing the pressure-gradient force against gravity on a static fluid element (derived from Static Equilibrium's force balance), with z measured positive upward:

dzdP​=−ρg​

For constant density (incompressible fluid):

P+ρgz=C​⟹P2​−P1​=ρg(z1​−z2​)​

If point 2 is a depth h below point 1:

P2​=P1​+ρgh​

Key idea: pressure increases with depth, and — critically — depends only on vertical depth, never on horizontal position or on the shape of the container (a direct consequence of ∂p/∂x=∂p/∂y=0 noted in Static Equilibrium). This is the "hydrostatic paradox": a thin tall tube and a wide tank of the same fluid at the same depth exert the same pressure on their base, regardless of total weight.

Pressure head

hp​=ρgP​​

The equivalent height of a column of the fluid whose weight-per-area produces the given pressure — used to report pressures as "mm Hg," "m of water," etc., and directly usable in Manometers.
