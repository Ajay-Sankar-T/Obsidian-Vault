T6-Q2 — Inverting Amplifier

EE1100op-amptutorial-solutioninverting-amplifier

Hub: Tutorial 6 on OP-AMP · Previous: T6-Q1 - Amplifier Gain and Design · Next: T6-Q3 - Non-inverting Amplifier

The op-amp of Fig. 2 has Vi​=1 mV with R1​=15 kΩ and R2​=1.2 MΩ. Find the voltage gain and the output voltage. [Gain −80, Vo​=−80 mV]

Tests: inverting amplifier, virtual ground.

Given

R1​=15 kΩ from Vi​ to the inverting input, R2​=1.2 MΩ in the feedback path, non-inverting input grounded, Vi​=1 mV.

Principle and why it applies

Negative feedback through R2​ and the grounded + input: virtual short v−​=v+​=0 and no input current, so the current through R1​ must all flow through R2​.

Solution

KCL at the inverting node: R1​Vi​−0​=R2​0−Vo​​ ⇒ Vo​=−R1​R2​​Vi​.

(a) Av​=−15×1031.2×106​=−80. (b) Vo​=−80×1 mV=−80 mV.

Result

Av​=−80,Vo​=−80 mV

Check

Current in R1​: 1 mV/15 kΩ=66.7 nA; across R2​: 66.7 nA×1.2 MΩ=80 mV, polarity such that Vo​ is below ground ✓. The input resistance seen by the source is R1​=15 kΩ (not infinite).

Trap / alternative

Quoting a positive gain: the minus sign is the 180° phase shift of the inverting configuration. Also 1+R2​/R1​=81 is the non-inverting formula, not this one.
