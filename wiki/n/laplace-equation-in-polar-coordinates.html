Laplace Equation in Polar Coordinates

What it is

For circular regions put x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ. By the chain rule the Laplacian becomes

∇2u=urr​+r1​ur​+r21​uθθ​=0.

(The ur​/r term comes from the second derivative of r=x2+y2​; the mixed terms cancel.)

u=R(r)Θ(θ)">

Solution by separation u=R(r)Θ(θ)

r2R′′+rR′−λR=0,Θ′′+λΘ=0.

The r-equation is a Cauchy–Euler equation: R=rm, m=±λ​. The angular equation depends on the region.

Boundedness at the centre. For the disk R=r−n (and lnr) blow up at r=0, so they are dropped.

Disk, u(a,θ)=f(θ): an​=π1​∫02π​fcosnθdθ, bn​=π1​∫02π​fsinnθdθ.

  • Mean value property: u(0,θ)=a0​/2 is the average of f over the circle.
  • Maximum principle: the extremes of u occur on the boundary circle.
  • Summing the series gives Poisson's formula u(r,θ)=2π1​∫02π​a2−2arcos(θ−ϕ)+r2a2−r2​f(ϕ)dϕ.
  • A single mode: u(a,θ)=cosnθ⇒u=(r/a)ncosnθ.

Annulus (zero on the outer radius, data on the inner): each mode is (a/b)m−(b/a)m(r/b)m−(b/r)m​, which equals 1 at r=a and 0 at r=b. For 1<r<2, u(1,θ)=sinθ, u(2,θ)=0 on 0<θ<π: u=3r4−r2​sinθ.

Figure (right): the disk with boundary value θ on the right half and 0 on the left. The solution is smooth inside even though the boundary data jump at θ=±π/2.

Method

  1. Write ∇2 in polar form; identify the angular condition from the shape (periodic for a full disk, Dirichlet for a sector).
  2. Solve Θ′′+λΘ=0, then the Euler equation for R.
  3. Keep only bounded radial solutions (r−n is out at the centre, rn out at infinity).
  4. Match the boundary circle: Fourier series in θ.
Traps
  • Forgetting that Θ must be 2π-periodic on a full disk, which forces integer n.
  • On a sector the exponent is nπ/α (not n); for a half disk it is an integer, for a quarter disk it is 2n.
  • Not matching the Fourier interval to the one-period description of the boundary data (e.g. θ∈[−π/2,3π/2) describes the same function as [−π,π)).
  • Harmonic functions are smooth in the interior, not necessarily up to the boundary: at the tip of a slit (interior angle 2π) the exponent is π/α=21​, so u∼r1/2 and ur​∼r−1/2 is unbounded.
