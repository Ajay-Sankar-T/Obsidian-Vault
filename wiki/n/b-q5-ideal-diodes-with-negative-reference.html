B-Q5 — Ideal Diodes with a −5 V and a 0 V Input

EE1100diodestutorial-solutionlogic

Hub: Tutorial 5 on Diode Circuits and Applications · Previous: B-Q4 - Diode AND Gate · Next: B-Q6 - Diode Clamper

What is V0​ in the circuit of Fig. 5 (inputs −5 V and 0 V through diodes, load RL​=1 kΩ to ground)? Assume ideal diodes.

1. Theory

Same structure as the AND gate (B-Q4 - Diode AND Gate): the diode cathodes are the inputs, the anodes meet at V0​, and V0​ is tied to ground through RL​ (here the pull-up level is 0 V). The diode with the lowest cathode voltage conducts and pulls the node down to that voltage (ideal: no drop).

2. Solution

Assume D1​ (cathode at −5 V) is ON: V0​=−5 V. Check D2​: anode =V0​=−5 V, cathode =0 V, so vD2​=−5 V <0: reverse biased, OFF ✓. Check D1​: current from ground through RL​ into the node and out through D1​ to the −5 V source: I=1 k0−(−5)​=5 mA>0 ✓.

(If instead D2​ were ON, V0​=0 and D1​ would have anode 0 V, cathode −5 V: forward biased with no resistance to limit it — inconsistent.)

V0​=−5 V​,IRL​​=ID1​=5 mA, ID2​=0.

A circuit simulation gives V0​=−4.999995 V with states (D1​ ON, D2​ OFF).
