Eigenvalues, Rotation and Diagonalization Everywhere

← up: Cross-Domain Synthesis MOC

Mohr's circle explorer

Three notes, one linear-algebra fact

  • Plane Stress Transformation: the stress matrix [σ]=(σx​τxy​​τxy​σy​​) is diagonalized by a rotation θp​, giving principal stresses σ1​,σ2​ as eigenvalues.
  • Phasors and Complex Algebra: multiplying by j is a 90° rotation in the complex plane (j=ej90°), and every phasor is literally a 2D vector (x,y)=(Acosθ,Asinθ).
  • Mohr's Circle for Stress: the transformation equations, parameterized by angle, trace out a circle in (σ,τ)-space — because a 2×2 symmetric matrix's transformation under rotation is, geometrically, exactly a circle in its own eigenbasis-centered coordinates.

These are not three separate techniques — they are the same fact about symmetric 2×2 real matrices under orthogonal similarity transformation ([σ′]=[Q]T[σ][Q], noted explicitly in Plane Stress Transformation's "eigenvalue/matrix interpretation" section), applied once to a stress tensor and once (informally) to a rotating vector in the electrical case.

The genuinely new observation

A phasor is not a tensor — it has no "principal value" problem, because a vector under rotation doesn't have eigenvalues the way a matrix does (a vector just rotates; it doesn't diagonalize into anything). So why does Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits's θI​=θV​−θZ​ feel so structurally similar to Plane Stress Transformation's tan2θp​=σx​−σy​2τxy​​?

Because impedance Z=R+jX is a 1×1 "complex-diagonalized" stand-in for what, in a fully general anisotropic material or a multi-port network, would be a matrix. The factor-of-2 in tan2θp​ (angle doubles for stress, boxed explicitly in Mohr's Circle for Stress's "critical angle doubling rule") comes precisely from the fact that a symmetric matrix's eigenvector rotation appears at twice the physical angle in the transformation formula (a consequence of cos2θ,sin2θ terms arising from squaring rotation matrix entries) — whereas a phasor's own rotation angle appears at 1×, not 2×, because a phasor is a first-order (vector) object, not a second-order (tensor) object. This is the reason Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy has to warn about the γ=2εxy​ engineering-vs-tensor-shear factor of 2 — it's the exact same order-of-tensor bookkeeping issue reappearing.

Rule of thumb this gives you across all three domains: whenever a physical transformation law involves cos2θ/sin2θ (stress, strain, Mohr's circle, moment of inertia principal axes — none of which currently has its own note but would obey the identical formula), you are diagonalizing a rank-2 tensor. Whenever it involves cosθ/sinθ at 1× (phasor rotation, impedance angle, coordinate rotation of a force vector), you are rotating a rank-1 vector. Confusing the two — using θ where you need 2θ — is the single most common transformation-formula error in both subjects.

Impedance angle as a degenerate Mohr's circle

Push the analogy one step further: AC Power and Power Factor's power triangle (P horizontal, Q vertical, S hypotenuse, tanϕ=Q/P) is geometrically identical to Mohr's circle collapsed to a single point-vector rather than a circle of transformation states — P plays σavg​'s role (a "real, dissipative" axis) and Q plays τmax​'s role (a "reactive, non-dissipative" axis). This is why both fields independently converge on a triangle-with-a-hypotenuse-magnitude-and-an-angle as the natural summary picture: it's the universal way to display "one real part + one imaginary/orthogonal part" as a single diagram, whether the two parts are (σ,τ), (P,Q), or (R,X).
