Two Dissimilar Plates in a Rigid Opening

strength-of-materialsstress-straincomposite-members

← up: Tutorial 4 · builds on: Composite Members under Axial Load

Setup

Two square plates of side a, materials (E1​,ν1​) and (E2​,ν2​), placed side by side (frictionless contact) inside a rigid opening of width l<2a — the plates are oversized and must be compressed to fit.

   ┌───────── l (opening) ─────────┐
   │←────a────→│←────a────→│
   ├────────────┼────────────┤
   │  Plate 1   │  Plate 2   │      2a > l  → both plates compressed
   │  E1, ν1    │  E2, ν2    │      frictionless interface (normal force only)
   └────────────┴────────────┘

Why this is the equal-stress counterpart of the composite member

In Composite Members under Axial Load, a rigid end plate forced equal strain on both materials. Here, the frictionless interface transmits normal force but no shear, and since both plates have the same cross-sectional area in contact, equilibrium (force balance across the interface) forces:

σ1​=σ2​=σ​butε1​=ε2​​(εi​=Ei​σ​)

— the mirror image of the composite-member case: there, stress differed while strain was shared; here, strain differs while stress is shared. This equal-stress/equal-strain duality is the same distinction as series vs parallel in circuit elements (see the cross-domain note below).

Compatibility

The total forced contraction must equal the total oversize 2a−l:

Δa1​+Δa2​=l−2a​ (a negative quantity, since 2a>l)

with Δa1​=aσ/E1​, Δa2​=aσ/E2​ (both compressive, i.e. negative σ). Substituting and solving:

σ=−E1​+E2​E1​E2​​⋅a2a−l​​

— note the harmonic-mean-like combination E1​+E2​E1​E2​​ (an equal-stress/series-style combination), in contrast to the arithmetic combination E1​A1​+E2​A2​ that appeared for the equal-strain/parallel-style composite member.

Individual plate contractions

Δa1​=−E1​+E2​E2​​(2a−l)​,Δa2​=−E1​+E2​E1​​(2a−l)​

The plate with the smaller E undergoes the larger deformation — the softer material yields more to accommodate the same shared stress, exactly as the more compliant (larger reactance) element in a series circuit carries a larger share of the total voltage drop for the same current.

Poisson expansion in the unconstrained (vertical) direction

Each plate's compressive strain in the loaded direction produces a lateral (vertical) expansion via Poisson's ratio:

Δb1​=ν1​E1​+E2​E2​​(2a−l)​,Δb2​=ν2​E1​+E2​E1​​(2a−l)​

directly from εlateral​=−νεlongitudinal​ (Generalized Hooke's Law and Poisson's Ratio) applied to each plate's own Δai​/a.

Cross-domain: series vs parallel, mechanically

This equal-stress ("series") vs the composite member's equal-strain ("parallel") duality is exactly the electrical series/parallel distinction under The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy: two mechanical "compliances" (elements sharing the same force, i.e. the same stress here) combine like impedances in series (Zeq​=Z1​+Z2​, mirrored here by the E1​E2​/(E1​+E2​) combination, which is precisely the "reciprocal of sum of reciprocal-stiffnesses" rule — springs in series), while two elements sharing the same displacement (equal strain, the composite-member case) combine like impedances in parallel (Yeq​=Y1​+Y2​, mirrored by E1​A1​+E2​A2​ — springs in parallel). Recognizing "do these share force or displacement?" is the mechanical-mechanics version of recognizing "do these share current or voltage?" before combining circuit elements.
