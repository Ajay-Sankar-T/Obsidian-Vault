Hydrostatic Force on Plane Surfaces and Centre of Pressure

fluid-mechanicsfluid-staticscentre-of-pressure

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Pressure and the Hydrostatic Equation, Second Moments of Area

Resultant force on a submerged plane surface

Pressure force on an elemental area: dF=pdA. Integrating over the surface, and using pc​=ρghc​=γhc​ at the centroid:

FR​=pc​A=ρghc​A=γhc​A​

where hc​ is the vertical depth of the centroid — the resultant force depends only on the centroid's depth and the total area, regardless of the surface's shape or orientation, because pressure varies linearly with depth and the linear terms average out to the centroid value.

Centre of pressure (CP)

The resultant force does not act through the centroid — it acts through the centre of pressure, always below the centroid, because the pressure (and hence the "weighting") is larger on the deeper part of the surface:

Vertical plane:

hCP​=hc​+Ahc​IG​​​⟹hCP​>hc​

Inclined plane (angle θ from horizontal, y measured along the plane):

yCP​=yc​+Ayc​IG​​​,hc​=yc​sinθ,hCP​=yCP​sinθ

IG​ is the second moment of area about the horizontal centroidal axis — see Second Moments of Area for standard shapes and for why this is the same IG​ used in beam bending.

Worked pattern (vertical projected rectangular gate)

b=50 ft, h=24 ft: A=bh=1200 ft2, centroid depth hCG​=12 ft, Ixx​=121​(50)(24)3. Since the surface is vertical, θ=90°, sinθ=1:

yCP,proj​=−hCG​Aproj​Ixx​sinθ​=−4 ft⟹hCP​=hCG​+4=16 ft

CP sits 4 ft below the centroid, at 16 ft total depth.
