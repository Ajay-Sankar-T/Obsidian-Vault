Deformation of a Prismatic Bar

For a bar of length L, constant cross-section A, modulus E, under constant axial force N:

σ=AN​,ε=Eσ​=AEN​,δ=εL=AENL​

AE is the axial stiffness of the member (units of force).

If N(x) or A(x) varies continuously along the length:

Axial Member as a Spring

The relation δ=NL/(AE) is exactly Hooke's-law-for-a-spring, N=kδ, with equivalent stiffness k=AE/L. Axial members in series/parallel combine like springs:

  • Series (same N through each): keq​1​=∑ki​1​
  • Parallel (same δ, loads share): keq​=∑ki​

This spring analogy is essential for Statically Indeterminate Axial Members, where compatibility of displacement (not just equilibrium) is needed to solve for the internal forces.

Saint-Venant's Principle

The formula σ=N/A assumes a uniform stress distribution across the section. Near points of load application or abrupt cross-section change, the actual stress distribution is non-uniform, but Saint-Venant's principle states that this local disturbance decays and the stress becomes essentially uniform at a distance comparable to the largest cross-sectional dimension away from the disturbance. Stress concentrations near holes, fillets, and notches are quantified by a stress-concentration factor K: σmax​=Kσavg​.
