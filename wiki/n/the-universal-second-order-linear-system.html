Where this is genuinely surprising

The buckling equation EIv′′+Pv=0 has zero damping term (b=0) — it looks like a pure oscillator in space rather than time, and indeed its solution v(x)=Asin(kx)+Bcos(kx), k=P/EI​, is undamped. But the RLC equation is a decaying oscillator in time. These look unrelated until you notice: buckling's independent variable is a spatial coordinate with fixed boundary conditions at both ends (v(0)=v(L)=0), exactly like a vibrating string or an undamped LC circuit with boundary conditions instead of initial conditions. The "damping" term R in the electrical case has no structural analogue in buckling because buckling has no dissipation mechanism built into the idealized model — the analogous term would be a distributed lateral elastic foundation or internal material damping, which real column-on-elastic-foundation problems do include (Winkler foundation: EIv′′′′+kv=q, a fourth-order cousin).

The deep point: both problems are eigenvalue problems, not initial-value problems, even though the RLC "characteristic equation" language usually gets taught as an IVP technique. Requiring v(0)=v(L)=0 with a nontrivial solution forces sin(kL)=0, i.e. kL=nπ — this is identical in structure to requiring a homogeneous linear system to have a nontrivial solution, i.e. det(A−λI)=0. Pcr​ is literally an eigenvalue of the differential operator EId2/dx2 subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions. See Resonance and Buckling — Instability as an Eigenvalue Problem for the parallel with electrical resonance.

Consequence you probably didn't have before

Because Pcr​=π2EI/L2 and ω0​=1/LC​ are computed by the same "smallest eigenvalue of a linear operator" logic, the design lever is structurally identical in both fields: you cannot fix an instability problem by making the material stronger — you can only fix it by changing the operator's coefficients (EI, or L,C). Euler Buckling of Columns already states this for columns ("buckling load depends on EI, not material strength"); the electrical mirror is that a circuit's resonant frequency depends only on L,C, never on R or on how "strong" (high-power-rated) the resistor is — R only controls the damping, exactly as a column's damping (if it had any) wouldn't move Pcr​ either.
