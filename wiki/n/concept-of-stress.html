Concept of Stress

Related: Strength of Materials - MOC

Normal and Shear Stress

For a member carrying an internal force F distributed over a cross-sectional area A, the average stress is

σ=AP​(normal stress, force⊥area),τ=AV​(shear stress, force∥area)

At a point, stress is defined as the limit of force per unit area as the area shrinks to a point:

σ=ΔA→0lim​ΔAΔFn​​,τ=ΔA→0lim​ΔAΔFt​​

Units: Pa (N/m²), commonly MPa (N/mm²) in engineering practice.

The Stress Tensor (General State of Stress)

At a point in a 3D body, the complete stress state requires 9 components (6 independent, by moment equilibrium τxy​=τyx​, etc.):

[σ]=​σxx​τyx​τzx​​τxy​σyy​τzy​​τxz​τyz​σzz​​​

Plane stress (thin plates, beam surfaces, etc.) reduces this to three independent components σx​,σy​,τxy​ — see Plane Stress Transformation.

Sign Convention

  • Tensile normal stress: positive.
  • Compressive normal stress: negative.
  • Shear stress: positive on a positive face pointing in the positive coordinate direction (right-hand rule consistent with the pair τxy​=τyx​).

Equilibrium and Free-Body Diagrams

Internal stress resultants (axial force N, shear force V, bending moment M, torque T) at any section are found by cutting the member and applying equilibrium (∑F=0, ∑M=0) to one side of the cut. This "method of sections" underlies:

  • axial members (Axial Deformation)
  • torsion members (Torsion of Circular Shafts)
  • beams (Shear Force and Bending Moment Diagrams)

Factor of Safety

FS=σallowable​σfailure​​

Failure stress is typically the yield strength σY​ (ductile materials) or ultimate strength σu​ (brittle materials) — see Stress-Strain Diagram and Hooke's Law.

Check yourself

Q1. A 20 mm diameter rod carries a 50 kN tensile load. What is the normal stress?

σ=P/A=50000/(π0.012)=159.2 MPa.

Q2. A 10 mm bolt in single shear transmits 12 kN. What is the average shear stress?

τ=V/A=12000/(π0.0052)=152.8 MPa.

Q3. Why does one axial load produce both normal and shear stress?

Stress depends on the plane you cut. On a plane at angle θ to the cross-section, σθ​=σcos2θ and τθ​=σsinθcosθ. The shear is largest, σ/2, at 45∘.
