Induction Motor – Slip, Rotor Circuit and Power Flow

What it is

An induction motor is a transformer with a rotating secondary. The stator is the primary; the rotor windings (or bars) are a short-circuited secondary. With the same flux linking both,

E1​=4.44f1​N1′​Φm​,E2​=4.44f2​N2′​Φm​ ⇒ E2​E1​​=N2′​N1′​​(N′=kw​N),

but the air gap between rotor and stator adds reluctance and leakage, so the no-load (magnetising) current is 30–50 % of rated current, against 2–6 % for a transformer.

Slip

The rotor cannot reach synchronous speed: at nr​=ns​ there would be no relative motion, no induced rotor emf and no torque.

s=ns​ns​−nr​​​,nr​=(1−s)ns​,ωr​=(1−s)ωs​,s≈2–6% at full load.

s=1 at standstill, s→0 near synchronous speed.

Rotor frequency. The field moves at sns​ relative to the rotor, so

f2​=sf1​.

The rotor current sets up its own rotating field, which turns at f2​/(P/2)=sns​ relative to the rotor; add the rotor's own speed nr​=(1−s)ns​ and the rotor field turns at ns​ relative to the stator. Stator and rotor fields are therefore stationary with respect to each other at every slip, which is the condition for steady torque.

Rotor circuit at slip s

At standstill the rotor emf is E2​ and reactance x2​. At slip s: E2s​=sE2​, x2s​=2πf2​L2​=sx2​. The rotor current

I2​=r2​+jsx2​sE2​​=sr2​​+jx2​E2​​,cosϕ2​=(r2​/s)2+x22​​r2​/s​.

So the rotor looks like a standstill rotor of reactance x2​ in series with the resistance r2​/s, and the power factor is poor at standstill (small r2​/s) and improves as the rotor speeds up.

Power flow

Power crossing the air gap into the rotor, Pg​=I22​sr2​​. Split the resistance:

sr2​​=r2​+sr2​(1−s)​⟹Pg​=sPg​ (rotor copper loss)I22​r2​​​+(1−s)Pg​=Pem​I22​sr2​(1−s)​​​

Pg​: Pcu2​: Pem​=1:s:(1−s).

  • Developed (electromechanical) power Pem​=Pg​−Pcu2​=(1−s)Pg​.
  • Shaft power Psh​=Pem​−(core loss+friction and windage).
  • Torque: Tem​=ωr​Pem​​=(1−s)ωs​(1−s)Pg​​=ωs​Pg​​=ωs​I22​r2​/s​​, and Tsh​=ωr​Psh​​=ωs​(1−s)Psh​​.
  • Efficiency is at best ≈(1−s) of the rotor stage: a motor running at 5 % slip loses at least 5 % of the air-gap power as rotor copper, so speed regulation and efficiency are the same trade-off.

Worked illustration (4-pole, 50 Hz, s=4%, Pg​=10 kW): ns​=1500 rpm, nr​=1440 rpm, f2​=2 Hz, ωs​=157.1 rad/s. Rotor copper loss sPg​=400 W, Pem​=9.6 kW, T=Pg​/ωs​=63.7 N·m (and Pem​/ωr​=9600/150.8=63.7 N·m ✓).

Traps
  • Slip is relative to the synchronous speed (ns​), not to the rotor speed.
  • r2​/s is not the same as r2​: the extra r2​(1−s)/s is the load, representing mechanical output.
  • Torque is Pg​/ωs​ (synchronous angular speed), not Pg​/ωr​.
  • A rotor at slip s sees frequency sf1​, so its iron loss is negligible at normal slip (the core loss is mostly in the stator).

Cross-domain links

  • Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance — the resistance r2​/s is a reflected impedance: the mechanical load seen from the electrical side. It is the transformer picture with the load resistor r2​(1−s)/s standing for shaft power.
  • Thevenin, Norton and Maximum Power Transfer (AC) — torque is proportional to the power delivered to r2​/s, so the maximum (pull-out) torque occurs where r2​/s equals the magnitude of the source-side impedance, r2​/s=rth2​+(xth​+x2​)2​, with Zth​=rth​+jxth​ the Thevenin impedance of the stator side. Beyond that slip the motor stalls.
