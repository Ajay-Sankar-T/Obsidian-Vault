T7-Q2 — Exciting Current and Primary Current Under Load

EE1100transformertutorial-solutionexciting-currentphasors

Hub: Tutorial 7 on Transformer · Previous: T7-Q1 - Turns and Core Area · Next: T7-Q3 - Voltage and Frequency Other Than Rated

(a) A 2200/220 V, 50 Hz transformer takes an exciting current of 0.6 A and a core loss of 361 W with the HV side at rated voltage. Find the two components of the exciting current. (b) It supplies 60 A at 0.8 pf lagging on the LV side. Find the primary current and its power factor, ignoring leakage drops. [(a) Ic​=0.164 A, Iϕ​=0.577 A (b) 6.48 A, 0.766 lag]

Tests: 10 Single Phase Transformers — no-load phasor diagram, current referred to the primary.

Given

V1​=2200 V, I0​=0.6 A, Pc​=361 W; I2​=60 A at cosϕ=0.8 lag, turns ratio 10.

Principle and why it applies

The exciting current I0​ has a core-loss component Ic​ in phase with V1​ and a magnetising component Iϕ​ lagging V1​ by 90∘; I0​=Ic​−jIϕ​. Under load the primary current is the phasor sum of I0​ and the load current referred to the primary, I2′​=I2​/a (a=10), with no leakage drops so V1​ stays at its rated value and I0​ is unchanged.

Solution

(a) Ic​=V1​Pc​​=2200361​=0.164 A, Iϕ​=I02​−Ic2​​=0.36−0.0269​=0.577 A.

(b) I2′​=1060​=6 A, lagging V by cos−10.8: I2′​=6(0.8−j0.6)=4.8−j3.6 A.

I1​=I0​+I2′​=(0.164−j0.577)+(4.8−j3.6)=4.964−j4.177 A

∣I1​∣=4.9642+4.1772​=6.49 A,cosϕ1​=6.494.964​=0.765 (lag).

Result

(a) Ic​=0.164 A, Iϕ​=0.577 A. (b) I1​=6.49 A at pf 0.765 lagging.

Check

The lagging load current plus a lagging magnetising current gives an even lower power factor than the load alone (0.8→0.765) ✓. Real power in: 2200×4.964=10.92 kW; real power out =220×60×0.8=10.56 kW, plus core loss 0.36 kW ✓.

Trap / alternative

Adding magnitudes (0.6+6): the currents are not in phase. The sheet gives 6.48 A, which differs from 6.488 only by rounding.
