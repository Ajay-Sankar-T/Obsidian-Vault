Laplace Equation on Rectangles and Strips

differential-equationspdesconceptlaplace-equationharmonic-functions

Related: PDEs - MOC · Separation of Variables · Laplace Equation in Polar Coordinates · Heat Equation - Separation of Variables · Sturm-Liouville Problems · solved in Assignment 10 - Laplace Equations - Solutions Q2–Q7

What it is

The Laplace equation ∇2u=uxx​+uyy​=0 governs steady-state temperature, electrostatic potential, ideal-flow potential and membrane height. Solutions are harmonic functions.

  • Dirichlet problem: u prescribed on the boundary. Neumann problem: ∂u/∂n prescribed (solution unique up to a constant, and the net flux must vanish).
  • Maximum principle: a harmonic function on a bounded region attains its maximum and minimum on the boundary.
  • Uniqueness: two Dirichlet solutions differ by a harmonic function that is 0 on the boundary, hence 0.

Solution on a rectangle 0<x<a, 0<y<b

u=X(x)Y(y) gives X′′+p2X=0, Y′′−p2Y=0. With zero on the sides x=0,a: Xn​=sinanπx​, pn​=anπ​ and

Yn​=Ccoshpn​y+Dsinhpn​y(not oscillatory in y).

Choose the combination that vanishes on the opposite edge. For data u(x,0)=f1​(x) and u(x,b)=0:

u=n∑​Dn​sinanπx​sinhanπ(b−y)​,Dn​sinhanπb​=a2​∫0a​f1​sinanπx​dx.

Similarly for data on y=b: sinhanπy​; on x=0 or x=a: swap the roles of x,y.

Several hot edges: the problem is linear, so solve four problems, each with one non-zero edge and the other three at zero, and add them.

Example (edge profile has a single mode). u(x,1)=sinπx, other three edges 0: u=sinhπsinhπy​sinπx, one term.

Other boundary conditions

  • Insulated side (ux​=0): use cosanπx​ (and n=0: u linear in y or constant). E.g. insulated sides and bottom edge u(x,0)=x on 0<x<π, top insulated: u=2c0​​+∑cn​cosnxcoshn(y−π), cn​=n2πcoshnπ2[(−1)n−1]​. The y-factor is chosen to have zero slope at the insulated top.
  • Insulated far edge on x=1: coshnπ(x−1) has zero slope at x=1.
0<x<L, 0<y<∞">

Semi-infinite strip 0<x<L, 0<y<∞

Boundedness as y→∞ removes the growing exponential: Yn​=e−nπy/L, so

u=∑Dn​sinLnπx​e−nπy/L,Dn​=L2​∫0L​u(x,0)sinLnπx​dx.

Mode n dies like e−nπy/L: far from the heated edge only the first term survives. With insulated sides the eigenfunctions are cosLnπx​ and the n=0 mode is a constant (a term linear in y is unbounded and is dropped), so u→ the mean of the edge data as y→∞. The requirement u→0 then only makes sense when that mean is zero, as in u(x,0)=2cosL3πx​+3cosL4πx​, which gives u=2cosL3πx​e−3πy/L+3cosL4πx​e−4πy/L.

Why it works

sinh/cosh appear because the sign of the separation constant is opposite to the wave and heat cases: the equation is elliptic, solutions are determined by the boundary as a whole and not by marching in y. Large-n modes are strongly damped away from the edge, which is why harmonic functions are smooth in the interior even when the boundary data have corners.

Method

  1. Draw the rectangle, mark which edges are non-zero. Split into one-edge problems.
  2. For each, choose Xn​ from the side conditions, build Yn​ to vanish (or have zero slope) on the opposite edge.
  3. Fit the edge data by a Fourier series (note symmetries: a tent gives n2π28H​sin2nπ​).
  4. Add the problems.
Traps
  • Using sin(ny) in y (that is the wave/heat case). In y it is sinh or cosh.
  • Writing sinhanπy​ for data on the bottom edge: it vanishes there. Use sinhanπ(b−y)​.
  • Compatibility: a Neumann problem needs zero net flux through the boundary.
