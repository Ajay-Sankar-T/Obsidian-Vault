T4-Q6: Transfer Function of a Series RLC Band-Pass Filter

EE1100tutorial-solutionfiltersRLC-circuitband-pass

Hub: Tutorial 4 - Resonance, Filter and Three-Phase Circuits · Theory: Theory - Resonance and Passive Filters · Previous: T4-Q5 - RC High-Pass Filter Design · Next: T4-Q7 - Band-Reject Filter Design

For the circuit with L=1 H and C=1 μF in series with the signal path and the output across R=1 kΩ: find the transfer function, the nature of the filter, the centre frequency, the half-power frequencies and the bandwidth. [Sheet: 1000, 618, 1818, 1000 rad/s. The upper half-power frequency printed as 1818 is a misprint; it is 1618.]

Tests: band-pass filter from a series RLC, transfer function and half-power points.

Given

Vi​ drives L in series with C in series with R; V0​ is taken across R. L=1 H, C=1 μF, R=1000 Ω.

Principle and why it applies

The three elements form a voltage divider with the output across R. L and C cancel at one frequency, leaving only R in the loop, so the output equals the input there: a band-pass filter. At very low ω the capacitor blocks; at very high ω the inductor blocks; in between the signal passes.

Diagram

Solution

Transfer function:

H(jω)=Vi​V0​​=R+jωL+jωC1​R​=R+j(ωL−ωC1​)R​=(jω)2+jωR/L+1/LCjωR/L​.

∣H∣→0 as ω→0 or ∞ and ∣H∣=1 (0 dB, zero phase) at ω0​: band-pass.

Centre frequency: ω0​=LC​1​=10−6​1​=1000 rad/s.

Bandwidth and Q: BW=LR​=11000​=1000 rad/s, Q=Rω0​L​=1 (a low-Q, broad filter).

Half-power frequencies (∣X∣=R, i.e. ∣H∣=1/2​): with Q=1 use the exact form,

ω1,2​=ω0​[1+4Q21​​∓2Q1​]=1000[1.118∓0.5] ⇒ ω1​=618 rad/s,ω2​=1618 rad/s.

Result

H=R+j(ωL−1/ωC)R​ (band-pass);  ω0​=1000, ω1​=618, ω2​=1618, BW=1000 rad/s​

Check

ω2​−ω1​=1000=BW ✓ (the printed 1818−618=1200 does not equal the printed bandwidth, which exposes the typo). ω1​ω2​=618×1618=106=ω02​ ✓. At ω1​: ωL−1/ωC=618−1618=−1000=−R, so ∣H∣=1/2​ ✓.

Trap / alternative

  • Taking the output across C gives a low-pass-with-peak, across L a high-pass-with-peak; only the output across R is a true band-pass.
  • Using ω0​±BW/2=500,1500 is the high-Q approximation and would be wrong at Q=1.
