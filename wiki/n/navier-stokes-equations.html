Navier-Stokes Equations

conceptfluid-mechanicsviscous-flownavier-stokes

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Newtonian Constitutive Relation, Stress in a Moving Fluid, Differential Continuity Equation · formulas: Formula Bank §1

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: the Navier–Stokes equations are Newton's second law for a Newtonian fluid element: ρDV/Dt=−∇p+ρg+μ∇2V, solved together with continuity ∇⋅V=0.

Intuition: compared with Euler's equation there is one new force, viscous diffusion μ∇2V: it smooths out velocity differences, spreading momentum from fast layers to slow ones. The nonlinear convective term (V⋅∇)V is what makes the equations hard.

Governing Relations

For incompressible flow with constant viscosity:

ρ[∂t∂V​+(V⋅∇)V]=−∇p+ρg+μ∇2V​,∇⋅V=0

Cartesian x-component:

ρ(∂t∂u​+u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​+w∂z∂u​)=−∂x∂p​+ρgx​+μ(∂x2∂2u​+∂y2∂2u​+∂z2∂2u​)

Cylindrical z-component (used for pipes, annuli):

ρ(∂t∂vz​​+vr​∂r∂vz​​+rvθ​​∂θ∂vz​​+vz​∂z∂vz​​)=−∂z∂p​+ρgz​+μ[r1​∂r∂​(r∂r∂vz​​)+r21​∂θ2∂2vz​​+∂z2∂2vz​​]

Terms:

Term Name Meaning
ρ∂V/∂t local inertia unsteadiness
ρ(V⋅∇)V convective inertia nonlinear; steady acceleration through a varying field
−∇p pressure gradient drives fluid to low pressure
ρg body force gravity
μ∇2V viscous diffusion momentum spreading by shear

Modified pressure: with P=p+ρgz (z up) gravity disappears: ρDV/Dt=−∇P+μ∇2V.

Vorticity form (2-D): DtDζ​=ν∇2ζ, where ν=μ/ρ: vorticity is advected and diffused, never created in the interior of an incompressible 2-D flow.

Where: ν=μ/ρ = kinematic viscosity; P = piezometric pressure.

Assumptions: continuum, Newtonian, incompressible, constant μ. (The general form keeps ∇⋅τ and a variable μ.)

Visual

No picture replaces the plane-flow reduction that follows: for u=u(y) with steady, fully developed flow, every term but pressure gradient and viscous diffusion vanishes. The profile figure in Plane Couette and Hagen–Poiseuille flows shows the result.

When To Use

  • To derive every exact viscous solution (Couette, Poiseuille, Hagen–Poiseuille, film flow).
  • To scale the physics (Reynolds number, boundary layers).
  • As the starting point of CFD.

Recognition pattern: "viscous flow", "find the velocity profile", "fully developed laminar flow", "derive from Navier–Stokes" → N–S with the symmetries applied.

Method

  1. Identify the symmetries: steady (∂t​=0), fully developed (∂x​=0), unidirectional (v=w=0), axisymmetric (∂θ​=0).
  2. Choose the coordinate system that matches the boundaries.
  3. Apply: continuity first (it fixes which components vanish), then the cross-stream momentum equations (pressure is hydrostatic across the flow), then the streamwise one reduces to an ODE.
  4. Integrate twice and apply two boundary conditions (Boundary Conditions in Fluid Flow).
  5. Check: units, limiting cases, and the dimensionless form's Re.

Derivation / Why It Works

Insert the Newtonian law into Cauchy's equation ρDV/Dt=ρg−∇p+∇⋅τ. With τij​=μ(∂j​ui​+∂i​uj​) and μ constant:

∂j​τij​=μ∂j​∂j​ui​+μ∂i​(∂j​uj​)=μ∇2ui​+μ∂i​(∇⋅V),

and the last term vanishes for incompressible flow, leaving μ∇2V.

Counting. Four unknowns (u,v,w,p), four equations (continuity and three momentum): the system is closed. The nonlinearity means there are very few exact solutions; those that exist have (V⋅∇)V≡0 (unidirectional flows).

Euler limit. For μ→0 the equations reduce to Euler's but the highest derivative disappears, so the no-slip condition cannot be met: a thin boundary layer forms (Convection Fundamentals).

Common Traps

  • Trap: keeping μ∇2V in a flow with variable viscosity.
  • Avoid it by: writing ∇⋅τ in full.
  • Trap: forgetting the cross-stream momentum equation (it gives hydrostatic pressure across a unidirectional flow).
  • Trap: keeping the convective term in a fully developed unidirectional flow (it vanishes since ∂u/∂x=0).
  • Trap: using the cylindrical form with the Cartesian Laplacian (the r1​∂r​(r∂r​) structure matters).

Example

Given

Verify that plane Couette flow u=Uy/h, v=w=0 satisfies Navier–Stokes with no pressure gradient.

Solution

Continuity: ∂u/∂x=0 ✓. y and z momentum: pressure is hydrostatic. x-momentum: steady and ∂x​=0 so the convective term is zero, and

0=−∂x∂p​+μdy2d2u​=−∂x∂p​+0,

so ∂p/∂x=0 ✓.

Answer: the linear profile is an exact solution with uniform pressure. Key takeaway: a unidirectional flow turns the nonlinear PDE into a linear ODE, μu′′=dp/dx.
