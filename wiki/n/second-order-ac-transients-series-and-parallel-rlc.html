Second-Order AC Transients (Series and Parallel RLC)

Damping explorer

Structure of the response

Complete response=AC steady state+natural RLC response​

The natural part's type (over/critical/underdamped) is a property of the circuit alone (R, L, C values), independent of whether the drive is DC or AC — this is exactly the same classification Tutorial 2 builds for DC-switched RLC circuits; only the forced term changes from a constant to a sinusoid.

Series RLC characteristic equation

LCdt2d2i​+RCdtdi​+i=0⟹s2+LR​s+LC1​=0

Define α=2LR​ (damping coefficient) and ωn​=LC​1​ (undamped natural frequency — the same ωn​ that sets the resonant frequency in Week 8):

s2+2αs+ωn2​=0,ζ=ωn​α​=2R​LC​​ (damping ratio)

Three damping cases

Overdamped (ζ>1, α>ωn​): real distinct roots s1,2​=−α±α2−ωn2​​,

xn​(t)=Aes1​t+Bes2​t(no oscillation)

Critically damped (ζ=1, α=ωn​): repeated root s=−α,

xn​(t)=(A+Bt)e−αt(fastest non-oscillatory decay)

Underdamped (ζ<1, α<ωn​), with damped frequency ωd​=ωn2​−α2​:

xn​(t)=e−αt[Acosωd​t+Bsinωd​t](decaying sinusoid)

Critical resistance (boundary between over/underdamped, α=ωn​):

Rc​=2CL​​⇒R>Rc​:overdamped,R=Rc​:critical,R<Rc​:underdamped

This matches the classification already used for the DC-switched RLC example in Tutorial 2 Q10 (Rc​=89.44 Ω, ζ=0.559, ωn​=447.2 rad/s, ωd​=370.8 rad/s, underdamped).

Complete response under AC drive (underdamped case)

x(t)=xss,AC​(t)+e−αt[Acosωd​t+Bsinωd​t]​

e.g. i(t)=Im​sin(ωt+ϕ)+e−αt[Acosωd​t+Bsinωd​t], where Im​,ϕ come from ordinary phasor analysis (Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits) and α,ωd​ come from the natural-response analysis above. A,B are found from i(0+) and i˙(0+) (two initial conditions are needed for a second-order system, unlike the single condition for first order).

The transient frequency is NOT the source frequency

If the source drives at ω=1000 rad/s but the circuit's own ωd​=500 rad/s, the response contains both: a forced oscillation at 1000 rad/s that persists forever, and a decaying transient oscillation at 500 rad/s that dies out:

i(t)=Im​sin(1000t+ϕ)+e−αt[Acos500t+Bsin500t]

The transient frequency is a property of the circuit's own L,C,R (ωd​), completely independent of the driving frequency ω — easy to conflate the two since both are angular frequencies in rad/s.

Parallel RLC

Natural equation for the node voltage:

dt2d2v​+RC1​dtdv​+LC1​v=0⟹α=2RC1​,ωn​=LC​1​

with ωd​=ωn2​−α2​ and ζ=2R1​L/C​ computed the same way as series RLC, just with α built from 1/(2RC) instead of R/(2L) — note R appears in the denominator of α for parallel RLC (a large R under-damps a parallel circuit) versus the numerator for series RLC (a large R over-damps a series circuit) — a frequent sign-confusion trap.

Series vs parallel RLC cheat table

Quantity Series RLC Parallel RLC
ωn​ 1/LC​ 1/LC​
α R/(2L) 1/(2RC)
ωd​ ωn2​−α2​ same form
ζ 2R​C/L​ 2R1​L/C​
Large R effect more damping (overdamped) less damping (underdamped)

Switching rules recap

vC​(0+)=vC​(0−),iL​(0+)=iL​(0−)

but iC​, vL​ may jump — see AC Transients for the full continuity discussion, and the connection to buckling-type ODEs (a different physical system with the same s2+2αs+ωn2​=0 mathematical skeleton) in Euler Buckling of Columns in the Strength of Materials vault.
