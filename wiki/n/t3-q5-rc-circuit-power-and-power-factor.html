T3-Q5: RC Series Circuit: Impedance, Current, Power and Capacitance

EE1100tutorial-solutionAC-circuitsimpedancepower

Hub: Tutorial 3 - AC Circuit Analysis · Theory: AC Power and Power Factor, Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits · Previous: T3-Q4 - RC Series Voltage Drops · Next: T3-Q6 - Adding Parallel Branch Currents

A circuit has R=6 Ω in series with a capacitive reactance of 8 Ω; e(t)=141sin377t. (a) Complex impedance. (b) rms and instantaneous current. (c) Power factor and power delivered. (d) The capacitance. [Sheet: Z=6−j8=10∠−53.13∘; Irms​=9.97 A, i=14.1sin(377t+53.13∘); pf=0.596 leading, S=596.41−j795.2 VA; C=331.56 μF]

Tests: series RC impedance, complex power Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗, power factor.

Given

R=6 Ω, XC​=8 Ω (so ZC​=−j8 Ω), e(t)=141sin377t: Vm​=141 V, Vrms​=141/2​=99.7 V, ω=377 rad/s.

Principle and why it applies

A capacitor's impedance is −jXC​ (current leads voltage by 90∘), so a series RC has a negative impedance angle and the current leads the voltage by that angle. Real power is dissipated only in R (P=I2R); the capacitor supplies reactive power, counted negative (Q=−I2XC​) in the convention where inductive Q is positive. So Sˉ=P+jQ has a negative imaginary part.

Solution

(a) Z=6−j8=10∠−53.13∘ Ω.

(b) Irms​=∣Z∣Vrms​​=1099.7​=9.97 A; peak Im​=14.1 A. The current leads the voltage by 53.13∘:

i(t)=14.1sin(377t+53.13∘) A.

(c) The power factor is cosϕ=∣Z∣R​=106​=0.6 leading (the sheet prints 0.596, which looks like P/1000 rather than P/∣S∣; the exact value is 0.600). Powers:

P=Irms2​R=9.972×6=596.4 W,Q=−Irms2​XC​=−9.972×8=−795.2 VAr,

Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=99.7∠0∘×9.97∠−53.13∘=596.4−j795.2 VA,∣S∣=994 VA.

(d) XC​=ωC1​⇒C=377×81​=331.6 μF.

Result

Z=10∠−53.13∘ Ω; Irms​=9.97 A; pf=0.6 leading; Sˉ=596.4−j795.2 VA; C=331.6 μF​

Check

pf=P/∣S∣=596.4/994=0.600 ✓. 596.42+795.22​=994 ✓. The sign of Q matches a capacitive load, and tanϕ=Q/P=−1.333=−8/6 ✓.

Trap / alternative

  • Leading versus lagging must always be stated: cosϕ=0.6 alone is ambiguous. A capacitive circuit has a leading power factor.
  • Use rms in P=VIcosϕ; using peak values overstates power by 2.
  • Only R dissipates, so P=I2R is a shortcut that avoids the angle altogether.

Concepts used

  • Series RC impedance, complex power, power factor (AC Power and Power Factor, Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits)
