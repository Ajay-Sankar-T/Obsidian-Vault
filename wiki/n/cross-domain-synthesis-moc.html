Your vault has three technical domains that were built separately — Differential Equations, Strength of Materials, and EE1100 Basic Electrical Engineering (with Fluid Mechanics joining at the edges) — but they are, underneath, the same handful of mathematical structures wearing different units. This folder makes those structures explicit: not just "these look similar" but the actual shared theorem, the actual shared matrix, the actual shared physical mechanism, and — in a few places — a genuine new-to-you observation that falls out once you line the three domains up side by side.

The six syntheses

  1. The Universal Second-Order Linear System — RLC circuits, column buckling, and every damped oscillator are literally the same ODE.
  2. The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy — a dictionary that turns any circuit theorem into a structural-mechanics theorem and vice versa.
  3. Eigenvalues, Rotation and Diagonalization Everywhere — principal stresses, phasor rotation by j, and Mohr's circle are one linear-algebra fact in three costumes.
  4. Exponential Decay — Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation — τ=RC, damping ratio ζ, and Saint-Venant's principle are all "how fast does the homogeneous solution die."
  5. Energy Methods — A Unified View — Castigliano's theorem and circuit energy storage are the same calculus-of-variations move.
  6. Resonance and Buckling — Instability as an Eigenvalue Problem — Pcr​ and ω0​ are both the point where a boundary-value problem's homogeneous solution stops being negligible.

Added 2026-10-08: KDoMs, Fluid Mechanics and the recurring structures

  1. Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance — gear trains, ideal transformers and DC motors as lossless two-ports; reflected inertia = reflected impedance; the matching condition.
  2. Rotating Vectors — Phasors, Velocity Polygons and Complex Kinematics — d/dt→jω; loop closure = KVL; the acceleration image has an impedance angle.
  3. Conservation Laws and Flow Networks — KCL, continuity and equilibrium; KVL, Bernoulli and compatibility; hydraulic and structural analogies.
  4. Second Moments of Area — One Integral, Many Jobs — bending, torsion, buckling, centre of pressure, metacentre, rotational inertia; Mohr's circle for inertia.
  5. Fluids and Solids — Pressure, Stress and Constitutive Laws — pressure as isotropic stress, hoop stress from projected area, G vs μ, Deborah number as a Maxwell time constant.
