Convection Fundamentals — Boundary Layers and Scale Analysis

conceptheat-transfer

In one breath

Convection is conduction through a thin fluid layer whose thickness the flow sets. Newton's law q′′=h(Ts​−T∞​) just hides that thickness inside h. Scale analysis finds it without solving the equations: a laminar boundary layer grows like δ∼xRex−1/2​, so h∼k/δ and Nux​∼Rex1/2​Pr1/3.

Part of Heat Transfer - MOC. Previous: Transient Conduction - Lumped Capacitance and Heisler Charts. Next: Forced Convection Correlations, Natural Convection.

1. h is a conduction problem in disguise

At a wall the fluid is at rest (no slip), so heat leaves by conduction there:

qs′′​=−kf​∂y∂T​​y=0​=h(Ts​−T∞​) ⇒ h=Ts​−T∞​−kf​∂T/∂y∣0​​,Nu=kf​hL​=conductiveconvective​

Nu is the dimensionless temperature gradient at the wall: a thin thermal layer means a large Nu.

2. Governing equations (steady, 2-D, constant properties)

Continuity, x-momentum and energy for a boundary layer (∂/∂x≪∂/∂y):

∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​=0,u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​=ν∂y2∂2u​,u∂x∂T​+v∂y∂T​=α∂y2∂2T​

The momentum and energy equations have the same form; the only difference is ν against α. Their ratio is the Prandtl number Pr=ν/α=μcp​/k: how fast momentum diffuses compared with heat. Air 0.71; water at 20 °C 7; engine oil 103–104; liquid metals 10−2.

3. Scale analysis (the exam method)

Velocity layer. At distance x from the leading edge the fluid has been in the layer for t∼x/U and diffuses momentum δ∼νt​:

xU2​∼νδ2U​ ⇒ xδ​∼Rex−1/2​(exact: δ=5xRex−1/2​)

Thermal layer, Pr≳1 (thermal layer sits inside the velocity layer): heat diffuses only δT​∼δPr−1/3. Pr≪1 (liquid metal): δT​≫δ and the flow is nearly uniform across it, δT​∼δPr−1/2.

Nux​=khx​∼δT​x​ ⇒ Nux​∼Rex1/2​Pr1/3​

The exact similarity (Blasius/Pohlhausen) solution fixes the constants:

Nux​=0.332Rex1/2​Pr1/3,NuL​=0.664ReL1/2​Pr1/3 (average is twice the local value at L)

Because hx​∝x−1/2, the heat transfer is largest near the leading edge, where the layer is thinnest.

4. The pattern of correlations

Scale analysis gives the form; experiments fix the constants:

Situation Dimensionless groups Typical form
Forced convection Re, Pr Nu=CRemPrn
Natural convection Ra=GrPr Nu=CRan
Mixed Gr/Re2 decides combine the two

This is the Buckingham Pi Theorem at work: h depends on ρ,μ,k,cp​,U,L, giving Nu=f(Re,Pr) by dimensional analysis alone.

5. Worked example: air over a plate

Air at 300 K (ν=1.57×10−5, k=0.0264, Pr=0.707) at 5 m/s over a 1 m plate:

ReL​=1.57×10−55(1)​=3.2×105 (<5×105: laminar),NuL​=0.664(564)(0.707)1/3=333,hˉ=8.8 W/m2K

Boundary-layer thickness at the trailing edge δ=5/ReL​​=8.9 mm. Transition at Rex​=5×105 would be x=1.57 m, beyond the plate.

Traps
  • Using k of the wall in Nu=hL/k: it is the fluid's conductivity.
  • Property temperature: evaluate fluid properties at the film temperature Tf​=(Ts​+T∞​)/2 (unless the correlation says bulk).
  • Applying 0.664Re1/2Pr1/3 when Pr<0.6 (liquid metals) or ReL​>5×105.
  • Forgetting Nu built on L for plates and on D for pipes and cylinders.
Predict first: for oil (Pr=1000), is the thermal layer thicker or thinner than the velocity layer?

Much thinner: δT​/δ∼Pr−1/3=0.1. Heat does not diffuse far in the time the oil takes to pass, so the thermal layer stays inside the slow fluid close to the wall.

If the plate length doubles at the same speed, what happens to the average h?

hˉ∝L−1/2, so it falls by 29 %. The total heat still rises as L1/2 because the area doubled.

6. Connections

  • Viscosity and Newton's Law of Viscosity — μ sets the momentum diffusivity ν in Pr.
  • Viscous Flow and Exact Solutions - Formula Bank — fully developed laminar flows give constants like Nu=3.66 exactly; the boundary layer is the developing counterpart.
  • Dimensional Analysis and Similitude - Formula Bank — why Nu=f(Re,Pr).
  • Exponential Decay - Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation — diffusion over a distance δ∼νt​ is the same scaling as heat penetration αt​ in transient conduction.
