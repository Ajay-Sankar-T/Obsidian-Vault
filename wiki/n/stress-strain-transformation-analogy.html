Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy

Related: Plane Stress Transformation · Mohr's Circle for Stress · Strain Transformation and Rosettes

Stress and strain transformation are the same mathematical structure — a symmetric 2×2 tensor being rotated — with one substitution to keep straight. This note is the fast-recall mnemonic layer on top of the derivations in the linked notes above.

The One Substitution That Matters

τ ⟷ 2γ​​

Everywhere a stress formula has σx​,σy​,τxy​, the corresponding strain formula has εx​,εy​,γxy​/2. This single fact generates every transformation, principal-value, and Mohr's-circle formula for strain from the stress version.

Stress Strain
x quantity σx​ εx​
y quantity σy​ εy​
engineering shear τxy​ γxy​
tensor shear component τxy​ γxy​/2

Matrix (Tensor) Form

σ=[σx​τxy​​τxy​σy​​]ε=[εx​γxy​/2​γxy​/2εy​​]

Both transform under rotation the same way: T′=QTQT, with

Q=[cosθ−sinθ​sinθcosθ​]

This is why the transformation equations in Plane Stress Transformation and Strain Transformation and Rosettes look identical after the τ↔γ/2 swap — they're literally the same rotation formula applied to two different (but structurally identical) tensors.

Universal Formula

Define a generic symmetric tensor A=[Ax​Axy​​Axy​Ay​​]. Its principal values (eigenvalues) are

A1,2​=2Ax​+Ay​​±(2Ax​−Ay​​)2+Axy2​​

Substitute:

  • Stress: Ax​=σx​, Ay​=σy​, Axy​=τxy​
  • Strain: Ax​=εx​, Ay​=εy​, Axy​=γxy​/2

That's the whole topic in one line: Principal = Average ± Radius, with the radius built from a "difference" term and a "shear" term under a square root — exactly the structure of Mohr's circle in Mohr's Circle for Stress.

Eigenvalue / Eigenvector Interpretation

det(σ−λI)=0⟹λ=σ1​,σ2​det(ε−λI)=0⟹λ=ε1​,ε2​

Principal stresses/strains are eigenvalues; principal directions are eigenvectors. This is the linear-algebra restatement of Mohr's circle — diagonalizing the tensor is exactly finding the rotation angle θp​ at which off-diagonal (shear) terms vanish.

Side-by-Side Transformation Equations

Stress Strain
Normal (x′) σx′​=σavg​+2σx​−σy​​cos2θ+τxy​sin2θ εx′​=εavg​+2εx​−εy​​cos2θ+2γxy​​sin2θ
Normal (y′) σy′​=σavg​−2σx​−σy​​cos2θ−τxy​sin2θ εy′​=εavg​−2εx​−εy​​cos2θ−2γxy​​sin2θ
Shear τx′y′​=−2σx​−σy​​sin2θ+τxy​cos2θ γx′y′​=−(εx​−εy​)sin2θ+γxy​cos2θ
Principal σ1,2​=σavg​±(2σx​−σy​​)2+τxy2​​ ε1,2​=εavg​±(2εx​−εy​​)2+(2γxy​​)2​
Principal angle tan2θp​=σx​−σy​2τxy​​ tan2θp​=εx​−εy​γxy​​
Max shear τmax​=R=2σ1​−σ2​​ γmax​=2R=ε1​−ε2​
Max-shear angle θp​+45° θp​+45°
Zero-shear condition principal planes: τ′=0 principal directions: γ′=0

Why the shear (normal-equation) rows look identical after substitution, but the shear-transformation row looks different at first glance: the strain shear-transformation equation is written for engineering γx′y′​ (not the tensor component εx′y′​=γx′y′​/2), so it comes out looking like it's missing a factor of 2 compared to the stress version — it isn't; the equation for εx′y′​ (tensor form) would match τx′y′​ exactly under the substitution. Concretely:

Normal equations: replace τ with γ/2. Shear equation: the tensor-form result must be multiplied by 2 to get engineering γx′y′​.

The one relation responsible for most sign/factor-of-2 mistakes:

γxy​=2εxy​​

Top Quiz Traps

  1. Forgetting the 2θ (not θ) inside sin,cos of the transformation equations.
  2. Using +θ for a clockwise physical rotation — clockwise is θ<0.
  3. Forgetting γxy​=2εxy​.
  4. Using γ instead of γ/2 when computing the strain "radius" Rε​.
  5. Confusing τmax​=R (stress) with γmax​=2R (strain).
  6. Forgetting principal planes/directions have zero shear stress/strain by definition.
  7. Forgetting max-shear planes sit at exactly 45° from the principal planes.
  8. Solving tan2θp​=(⋯) and reporting 2θp​ instead of θp​.
  9. Not checking which root (C+R vs C−R) is σ1​/ε1​ (the larger one) vs σ2​/ε2​.
  10. Mixing units (MPa/GPa, N/mm) without converting consistently.

Check yourself

Q1. Which strain quantity plays the role of shear stress in the transformation equations?

Half the engineering shear strain, γ/2. Replace σ→ε and τ→γ/2 and the stress formulas become the strain formulas.

Q2. What is plotted on the vertical axis of Mohr's circle for strain?

γ/2 (with the same sign convention as for τ), against ε on the horizontal axis.
