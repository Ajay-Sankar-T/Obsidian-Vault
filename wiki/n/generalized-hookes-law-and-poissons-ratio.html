Generalized Hooke's Law and Poisson's Ratio

Related: Stress-Strain Diagram and Hooke's Law · Axial Deformation · Plane Stress Transformation

Poisson's Ratio

When a bar is stretched axially, it contracts laterally. For uniaxial stress σx​:

εy​=εz​=−νεx​=−νEσx​​

where ν (Poisson's ratio) is a material property, typically 0.25–0.35 for metals (theoretical bounds for isotropic materials: −1<ν<0.5).

Generalized Hooke's Law (Isotropic, Linear Elastic)

For a general 3D stress state, superposing the effect of each normal stress on each direction:

εx​=E1​[σx​−ν(σy​+σz​)],εy​=E1​[σy​−ν(σx​+σz​)],εz​=E1​[σz​−ν(σx​+σy​)]

For shear (no coupling between shear and normal terms in isotropic materials):

γxy​=Gτxy​​,γyz​=Gτyz​​,γzx​=Gτzx​​

Shear Modulus and the Relation Between E,G,ν

G=2(1+ν)E​

G (modulus of rigidity) is the slope of the shear stress-shear strain diagram, directly analogous to E for normal stress-strain.

Bulk Modulus and Dilatation

Volumetric strain (dilatation) under general stress:

e=εx​+εy​+εz​=E1−2ν​(σx​+σy​+σz​)

Under uniform hydrostatic pressure σx​=σy​=σz​=−p:

p=−Ke,K=3(1−2ν)E​

K is the bulk modulus. As ν→0.5 (incompressible limit), K→∞.

Plane Stress Simplification (σz​=0)

εx​=E1​(σx​−νσy​),εy​=E1​(σy​−νσx​),εz​=−Eν​(σx​+σy​)

Inverting for stress in terms of strain (used in plane-stress finite element formulations):

σx​=1−ν2E​(εx​+νεy​),σy​=1−ν2E​(εy​+νεx​)

This generalized law is what connects Concept of Stress to every subsequent loading case: axial (Axial Deformation), torsion (Torsion of Circular Shafts), and bending (Flexure Formula) are all special cases of applying Hooke's law to a specific, simplified strain field.

Fully worked applications of this law — restrained/confined deformation, composite members, principal-stress conversion, thermal restraint, and dissimilar-plate problems — are in Tutorial 4 — Stress-Strain Relationship.

Check yourself

Q1. Steel (E=200 GPa, ν=0.3) carries a uniaxial stress of 100 MPa. Find the axial and lateral strains.

εx​=σ/E=5×10−4 and εy​=εz​=−νεx​=−1.5×10−4.

Q2. Find the shear modulus of the same steel.

G=E/[2(1+ν)]=200/2.6=76.9 GPa.

Q3. Why can ν not exceed 0.5 for an isotropic material?

The bulk modulus K=E/[3(1−2ν)] must be positive, so ν<0.5. At ν=0.5 the material is incompressible (rubber is close).
