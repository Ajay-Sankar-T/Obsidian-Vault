AC Power and Power Factor

EE1100AC-circuitspower

← up: Tutorial 3 · builds on: Week 6, Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits

The three powers

Real (active) power — the average power actually consumed/dissipated:

P=Vrms​Irms​cosϕ[W]

Reactive power — energy sloshed back and forth with inductors/capacitors, net zero average, but real in the sense that it still loads the supply and wiring:

Q=Vrms​Irms​sinϕ[VAR]

Apparent power — what the supply actually has to be rated for:

∣S∣=Vrms​Irms​[VA]

Here ϕ=θV​−θI​ is the angle between voltage and current, which — from Phasors and Complex Algebra's θI​=θV​−θZ​ — is exactly the impedance angle θZ​.

Complex power

Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=P+jQ

where I∗ is the complex conjugate of the current phasor (conjugating fixes the sign of Q so that inductive loads read positive Q, matching convention). Then:

P=Re(S),Q=Im(S),∣S∣=P2+Q2​

Power triangle: P horizontal, Q vertical, S hypotenuse; tanϕ=Q/P.

Reactive power of each element

Element P Q Behaviour
Resistor >0 0 dissipates only
Ideal inductor 0 Irms2​XL​>0 lagging
Ideal capacitor 0 −Irms2​XC​<0 leading
Inductive circuit (net) >0 >0 lagging
Capacitive circuit (net) >0 <0 leading

Memorize: +Q→ inductive/lagging, −Q→ capacitive/leading — directly consistent with the impedance-angle sign convention in Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits (XL​>0, XC​<0 contribution).

Power factor

pf=cosϕ=∣S∣P​

For an impedance Z=R+jX: pf=R/∣Z∣. If current leads voltage, pf is leading (capacitive load); if current lags, pf is lagging (inductive load) — the two-word qualifier is not optional, cosϕ alone doesn't distinguish the two.

Fast series-circuit power shortcut

For Z=R+jX carrying current of magnitude I:

P=I2R,Q=I2X,∣S∣=I2∣Z∣

For series RLC specifically: P=I2R, Q=I2(XL​−XC​), S=I2[R+j(XL​−XC​)] — read directly off Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits's Z formula without needing V at all.

Power conservation

∑Psupplied​=∑Pabsorbed​,∑S=0 (signed consistently)

Big trap: superposition does NOT apply to power

Week 7 establishes that superposition carries over from DC for voltage and current. It does not carry over for power, because P=I2R is quadratic: (I1​+I2​)2=I12​+I22​ in general (cross terms survive unless the two sources are at different frequencies and everything is properly time-averaged). Never write Ptotal​=P1​+P2​ from separate superposition solutions without justifying it.

RMS, not peak

Every formula above uses RMS values (see RMS and Average Value). Using peak values in P=VIcosϕ overstates power by a factor of 2 — one of the most common exam errors in this course.
