T8-Q3: The Synchronous Speed $ns=120f/P$

EE1100tutorial-solutionmachinesinduction-machinesynchronous-speed

Hub: Tutorial 8 - Induction Machines · Theory: Theory - Three-Phase Induction Machines, Revolving Magnetic Field and Synchronous Speed · Previous: T8-Q2 - The Revolving Field · Next: T8-Q4 - Slip

The speed at which the revolving field rotates in the air gap is the synchronous speed. Write the expression for the synchronous speed of an induction machine of P poles supplied at frequency f (Hz).

Tests: electrical versus mechanical angle, poles versus pole pairs.

Principle and why it applies

The field pattern rotates at the supply (electrical) angular frequency ω=2πf in electrical degrees. A machine with P poles has P/2 pole pairs; one pole pair spans 360∘ electrical but only 360∘/(P/2) mechanical. So the mechanical angle swept per electrical cycle is P2​ of a turn:

θelec​=2P​θmech​ ⇒ ωs​=P/2ω​=P4πf​ rad/s.

Solution

Convert to revolutions per minute (1 rad/s =60/2π rpm):

ns​=P120f​ rpm​(ns​=P2f​ rev/s=pf​ rev/s, p=2P​ pole pairs).

Poles P 2 4 6 8 12
ns​ at 50 Hz (rpm) 3000 1500 1000 750 500
ns​ at 60 Hz (rpm) 3600 1800 1200 900 600

(The motor of Q6 is a 4-pole, 50 Hz machine: ns​=120×50/4=1500 rpm.)

Result

ns​=P120f​ rpm.

The synchronous speed is fixed only by the supply frequency and the number of poles; the load changes the rotor speed, not ns​.

Check

P=2, f=50: one electrical cycle = one mechanical revolution, so ns​=50 rev/s =3000 rpm ✓. Doubling the poles halves the speed ✓.

Trap / alternative

  • P is the number of poles, not pole pairs: using the pole-pair count gives ns​=60f/p and the formula "120f/P" with P=p is off by a factor of 2.
  • To change the speed of a given motor: change f (inverter drive), change the number of poles (pole-changing windings), or accept more slip (rotor resistance, voltage control).
  • Speeds quoted on nameplates (e.g. 1440 rpm) are the rotor speed at full load, just below ns​.
