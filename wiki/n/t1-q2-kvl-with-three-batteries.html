T1-Q2: KVL with Three Batteries

EE1100tutorial-solutionDC-circuitskirchhoff

Hub: Tutorial 1 - Circuit Laws and Theorems · Theory: Theory - Circuit Laws and Network Theorems · Previous: T1-Q1 - R-2R Ladder · Next: T1-Q3 - Current Sources and Power

Using KVL, find the current in every resistor of the circuit with E1​=E2​=E3​=10 V and resistors 3,8,6,12 Ω. [Sheet: I3​=I8​=I6​=0, I12​=0.833 A]

Tests: KVL / KCL, reading polarity, an "all-currents-vanish" check.

Given

E1​=10 V (left, in series with the 3 Ω), E2​=10 V (in series with the 8 Ω), E3​=10 V (right, across node B), 6 Ω between nodes A and B, 12 Ω from B to the common wire. All sources have their + terminal at the top.

Principle and why it applies

Three branches meet at A, three at B, so KCL gives two equations and two independent loops give two KVL equations. There are four unknown currents and a fifth through E3​. The structure is special: every battery has the same EMF, so a single node potential might satisfy all branches at once. KVL decides whether it does.

Diagram

Solution

Let I1​ be the current in the 3 Ω (left to right), I2​ in the 8 Ω (downward), I3​ in the 6 Ω (towards B), I4​ in the 12 Ω (downward), I5​ the current E3​ pushes into node B.

KCL. At A: I1​=I2​+I3​. At B: I3​+I5​=I4​.

KVL.

  • Loop E1​–3 Ω–8 Ω–E2​:  10−3I1​−8I2​−10=0 ⇒ 3I1​+8I2​=0.
  • Loop E2​–8 Ω–6 Ω–E3​:  −8I2​−10+10+6I3​=0 ⇒ 6I3​=8I2​.
  • Loop E3​–12 Ω:  10−12I4​=0 ⇒ I4​=1210​=0.833 A.

From the first two, I1​=−38​I2​ and I3​=34​I2​. KCL at A then gives −38​I2​=I2​+34​I2​=37​I2​, so −8I2​=7I2​ and

I2​=0 ⇒ I1​=0, I3​=0,I5​=I4​=0.833 A.

Result

I3Ω​=I8Ω​=I6Ω​=0,I12Ω​=0.833 A (top to bottom)​

E3​ alone supplies the 12 Ω; E1​ and E2​ float.

Check

Both nodes sit at 10 V: A is held at E1​=10 V (no drop in the 3 Ω) and at E2​=10 V, so the 8 Ω has 0 V across it; likewise A and B are at the same potential so the 6 Ω carries nothing. Power: E3​ delivers 10×0.833=8.33 W =I42​×12=8.33 W ✓.

Trap / alternative

Do not assume currents must be non-zero because there are three batteries. A faster route is nodal (Millman): VA​,VB​ with VA​=1/3+1/8+1/6E1​/3+E2​/8+VB​/6​ etc. gives VA​=VB​=10 V in two lines. The lesson is that equal EMFs in parallel branches produce no circulating current; with unequal EMFs you would get circulating currents in the E1​–E2​ loop.

Concepts used
