Exergy and Reversible Work

conceptthermal-engineering
In one breath

The first law counts energy; it cannot tell a joule of electricity from a joule of lukewarm water. Exergy is the useful part: the maximum work you can get from a system as it comes to equilibrium with the environment (the dead state, T0​,P0​). Every irreversibility destroys exergy in proportion to the entropy it generates: Xdest​=T0​Sgen​.

Part of Foundations of Thermal Engineering - MOC. Next: Rankine Cycle - Superheat, Reheat and Regeneration.

1. Definitions

With the environment at T0​=298 K, P0​=101.3 kPa:

Quantity Exergy
Heat Q at constant T XQ​=Q(1−TT0​​) (the work of a reversible engine between T and T0​)
Flowing fluid (flow exergy, per mass) ψ=(h−h0​)−T0​(s−s0​)+2V2​+gz
Closed system ϕ=(u−u0​)−T0​(s−s0​)+P0​(v−v0​)
Shaft work all of it is exergy

Exergy balance for a control volume at steady state:

0=∑(1−Tk​T0​​)Q˙​k​−W˙+∑m˙in​ψin​−∑m˙out​ψout​−X˙dest​,X˙dest​=T0​S˙gen​≥0

Second-law efficiency: ηII​=exergy suppliedexergy recovered​=1−Xin​Xdest​​. A turbine's isentropic efficiency is a measure of the same loss.

2. Worked examples

Heat is not all work. 1000 kJ of heat at 1000 K has exergy 1000(1−298/1000)=702 kJ: no engine can turn it into more than 702 kJ of work with the environment at 298 K.

Destroyed by heat transfer. Move 1000 kJ from a reservoir at 1000 K to one at 500 K. Energy is conserved, but

Sgen​=Q(5001​−10001​)=1.0 kJ/K,Xdest​=T0​Sgen​=298 kJ

The exergy of the heat fell from 702 to 404 kJ; the difference, 298 kJ, is the work potential thrown away by letting heat flow across a finite temperature difference.

Flow exergy of steam. Steam at 5 MPa, 400 °C: h−h0​=3092 kJ/kg against liquid at 25 °C, yet ψ=1219 kJ/kg: only 39 % of the enthalpy can be turned into work.

Throttling that steam to 1 MPa: h is unchanged, but entropy rises and 213 kJ/kg of exergy is destroyed: a valve is a pure exergy loss.

An adiabatic turbine, 5 MPa/400 °C to 10 kPa, with isentropic efficiency 0.85: ideal work 1091, actual 928 kJ/kg, exergy destroyed T0​(s2​−s1​)=153 kJ/kg, second-law efficiency 928/(928+153)=0.858 (equal to ηs​ here).

3. Where exergy is destroyed in practice

Process Cause Typical share
Combustion chemical energy to heat at a finite T 25–30 % of fuel exergy in a power plant
Heat transfer across a finite ΔT boiler, condenser 10–20 %
Friction, throttling, mixing pressure drop, expansion without work few % each
Turbine, compressor irreversibility 5–10 %

Design rule: reduce irreversibility where it is cheap to do so: feed water heaters (smaller ΔT for heat addition), reheat, intercooling, regeneration. Each cycle improvement in the following notes is a reduction in ΔT across which heat is exchanged.

Traps
  • Using the hot-body temperature instead of the environment's T0​ in T0​Sgen​.
  • Treating exergy as conserved: energy is, exergy is not.
  • Using kelvin only in T0​/T and Celsius elsewhere.
  • Calling the first-law efficiency of a heat pump or refrigerator the second-law one: ηII​=COP/COPrev​.
  • Forgetting that the exergy of low-temperature heat near T0​ is almost zero.
Predict first: heat at 330 K (hot water, 30 K above the environment). How much of 1000 kJ is exergy?

1000(1−298/330)=97 kJ: about 10 %. Warm water holds plenty of energy but almost no work potential. This is why low-grade waste heat is hard to use.

Why does a throttling valve destroy exergy but not energy?

hin​=hout​ (energy is conserved), but the pressure falls and the entropy rises (sout​>sin​): Xdest​=T0​Δsm˙>0. The pressure drop could have driven a turbine.
