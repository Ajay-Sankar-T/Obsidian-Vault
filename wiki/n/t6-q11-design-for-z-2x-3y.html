T6-Q11 — Op-Amp Circuit for $z=2x-3y$

EE1100op-amptutorial-solutiondesignanalog-computer

Hub: Tutorial 6 on OP-AMP · Previous: T6-Q10 - Difference Amplifier Condition · Next: T6-Q12 - Weighing Summer

A physical system is described by z=2x−3y where x,y are voltages within the op-amp's range. Draw an op-amp circuit with z as the output, using standard resistor values.

Tests: difference-amplifier weights, summer / subtractor design.

Given

Output z=2x−3y: weight +2 on x, −3 on y.

Principle and why it applies

In the difference amplifier the inverting input has weight −R2​/R1​ and the non-inverting input has weight (1+R1​R2​​)R3​+R4​R4​​, so both weights are set by resistor ratios. The two weights are independent knobs: R2​/R1​ and R4​/(R3​+R4​).

Solution

One-stage design.

  1. y goes to the inverting input with weight −3: R1​R2​​=3. Take R1​=10 kΩ, R2​=30 kΩ (both standard E24 values).
  2. x goes to the non-inverting input with weight +2: (1+R1​R2​​)R3​+R4​R4​​=4⋅R3​+R4​R4​​=2⇒R3​+R4​R4​​=21​⇒R3​=R4​. Take R3​=R4​=10 kΩ.

z=4⋅21​x−3y=2x−3y ✓

Alternative (two op-amps, easier to adjust the weights independently). Stage 1: inverting amplifier on x with gain −2 (R=10 kΩ in, 20 kΩ feedback) gives −2x. Stage 2: inverting summer with Rf​=30 kΩ, the output of stage 1 through 30 kΩ (weight 1) and y through 10 kΩ (weight 3): z=−(1⋅(−2x)+3y)=2x−3y. The one-stage circuit saves an op-amp but ties the x weight to the y weight through R2​/R1​.

Result

One op-amp difference amplifier: R1​=10 kΩ, R2​=30 kΩ (on y), R3​=R4​=10 kΩ (on x): z=2x−3y.

Check

x=1, y=0: v+​=0.5, gain 1+3=4, z=2 ✓. x=0, y=1: z=−3 ✓. Source loading: x sees R3​+R4​=20 kΩ, y sees R1​=10 kΩ: sources must have much lower output resistance (or add voltage followers).

Trap / alternative

Setting R3​=R4​ out of habit (from the unit-gain subtractor) gives the x-weight 21​(1+R1​R2​​), which equals 2 here only because R2​/R1​=3. In general solve the two weights separately. Also note the limitation of one stage: the x-weight is (1+R1​R2​​)R3​+R4​R4​​, so for a given y-weight it can be made anything from 0 up to 1+R1​R2​​, but never above that.
