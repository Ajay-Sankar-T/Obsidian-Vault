1. The recipe for a unidirectional flow

A flow with one velocity component that depends on one coordinate, u=u(y) (or u(r)), is an exact solution of Navier–Stokes. Steps:

  1. Continuity is satisfied (∂u/∂x=0), so v=w=0.
  2. Cross-stream momentum gives ∂p/∂y=ρgy​: pressure is hydrostatic across the flow.
  3. Streamwise momentum collapses to one ODE (the data sheet's 0=−dxdp​+μr1​drd​(rdrdu​) for pipes and annuli, 0=ρgx​−dxdp​+μdy2d2u​ for plates).
  4. Integrate twice, apply two boundary conditions: no-slip on a wall, zero shear μdu/dy=0 on a free surface, or a moving wall speed.
  5. Read off Q=∫udA, V=Q/A, τw​=μdu/dn at the wall.

The only things that change from case to case are the geometry and the boundary conditions. μ cancels from the shape of the profile when the driver is a pressure gradient or gravity; it sets the amount.

Resistance picture

Laminar flow is linear: Δp=Rh​Q with Rh​=wh312μL​ (plates) or πd4128μL​ (pipe). Series and parallel connections add like resistors (see the GPS 10 syringe and dashpot problems). The h3 and d4 are what make small channels so sensitive to size.

3. Regimes and limits

  • Pipe: laminar for Re=ρVd/μ<2300. Plates: laminar for Re=ρVˉh/μ<1400.
  • The solutions need fully developed flow. The entry length is roughly 0.06Red for a pipe, negligible at low Re (not on the data sheet; use as a check).
  • Thin gap: a curved gap of width h≪ radius behaves as a plane gap of perimeter πD (dashpot, wire die).

4. Stresses and creeping flow

Newtonian stress tensor (data sheet): σxx​=−p+2μ∂u/∂x, σyy​=−p+2μ∂v/∂y, τxy​=μ(∂u/∂y+∂v/∂x).

At a no-slip wall ∂u/∂x=0, so the viscous normal stress vanishes there and only the shear μ∂u/∂y acts on the wall.

Scaling the Navier–Stokes equations with length ℓ, speed U gives inertia/viscous =ρUℓ/μ=Re. For Re≪1 inertia drops and the equations are linear (Stokes flow): ∇p=μ∇2u. A velocity field can satisfy continuity and the boundary conditions yet fail momentum; whether it is an exact solution is decided by whether a pressure exists, i.e. ∂2p/∂x∂y must be the same from both components.

5. Cross-domain

  • The laminar profile satisfies a Poisson equation ∇2u=const, the same equation as torsion of a bar (Prandtl's membrane analogy). The circular case gives d4 in both the flow Q∝d4 and the torsional constant J∝d4: Power Transmission by Shafts.
