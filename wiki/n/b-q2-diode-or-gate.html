B-Q2 — Diode OR Gate

EE1100diodestutorial-solutionlogic

Hub: Tutorial 5 on Diode Circuits and Applications · Previous: B-Q1 - Two-Diode Circuit with 0.7 V Drop · Next: B-Q3 - Series Battery Rectifier · Related: B-Q4 - Diode AND Gate

Fig. 2: three diodes with anodes at E1​,E2​,E3​ and cathodes joined at V0​ with RL​=1 kΩ to ground. Practical diodes (0.7 V). Find V0​ for E1​=E2​=10 V, E3​=0 V; and for E1​=E2​=0, E3​=10 V.

1. Theory

The anodes are the inputs and the cathodes are tied together to a load to ground. The diode with the highest anode voltage turns on first and pulls the common node up to Emax​−Vγ​; every other diode then has its cathode above its anode (reverse biased) and is OFF.

V0​=max(E1​,E2​,E3​)−0.7(if max>0.7 V),V0​=0 otherwise.

This is the OR function in positive logic (1 = high ≈ 10 V, 0 = low ≈ 0 V), with a 0.7 V loss.

2. Solution

Case 1: E1​=E2​=10 V, E3​=0. D1​ and D2​ conduct (equal inputs, they share the current); D3​ has its anode at 0 V and its cathode at V0​=9.3 V so it is reverse biased (OFF).

V0​=10−0.7=9.3 V​,IRL​​=1 k9.3​=9.3 mA (4.65 mA through each of D1​,D2​)

Case 2: E1​=E2​=0, E3​=10 V. Now only D3​ conducts; D1​,D2​ (anodes at 0 V, cathodes at 9.3 V) are OFF.

V0​=10−0.7=9.3 V​,ID3​=9.3 mA

The output is the same, because the output depends only on the largest input.

3. Truth table (positive logic, 10 V = 1)

E1​ E2​ E3​ V0​ logic
0 0 0 0 V 0
any one or more 10 V 9.3 V 1

OR gate. (The logic-high level is degraded by 0.7 V, and the low level is exact 0 V.)
