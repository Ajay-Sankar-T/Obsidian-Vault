Strain Transformation and Rosettes

Related: Mohr's Circle for Stress · Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy · Generalized Hooke's Law and Poisson's Ratio

From Displacement Field to Strain

For a 2D displacement field u(x,y) (in the x-direction) and v(x,y) (in the y-direction), the strains at a point are:

εx​=∂x∂u​,εy​=∂y∂v​,γxy​=∂y∂u​+∂x∂v​

γxy​ here is the engineering shear strain (total angular distortion). It relates to the tensor shear-strain component by

γxy​=2εxy​​

— the single most important fact to keep straight in this whole topic (see Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy for why).

Worked example: if u=u0​+7(10−3)x+4(10−3)y and v=v0​+2(10−3)x−1(10−3)y, then

εx​=7×10−3,εy​=−1×10−3,γxy​=4×10−3+2×10−3=6×10−3

Strain Transformation Equations

Exactly parallel in structure to the stress-transformation equations of Plane Stress Transformation, with σ→ε and τ→γ/2:

εx′​=2εx​+εy​​+2εx​−εy​​cos2θ+2γxy​​sin2θ

εy′​=2εx​+εy​​−2εx​−εy​​cos2θ−2γxy​​sin2θ

γx′y′​=−(εx​−εy​)sin2θ+γxy​cos2θ

Note the shear equation has no factor of 21​ on (εx​−εy​)'s coefficient or on γxy​ itself — it's written for the engineering strain γx′y′​ directly (equivalent to writing the tensor equation and then doubling the result).

Principal Strains and Maximum Shear Strain

εavg​=2εx​+εy​​,Rε​=(2εx​−εy​​)2+(2γxy​​)2​

ε1,2​=εavg​±Rε​,γmax​=2Rε​=(εx​−εy​)2+γxy2​​=ε1​−ε2​

Principal direction:

tan2θp​=εx​−εy​γxy​​

(compare to stress's tan2θp​=2τxy​/(σx​−σy​) — the strain version has γ where stress has 2τ, precisely because γ=2εxy​). Maximum-shear-strain planes are at θp​+45°, and there εx′​=εy′​=εavg​.

Special case: if γxy​=0, the x,y axes are already principal strain directions, and ε1​,ε2​ are simply εx​,εy​ (in whichever order is larger).

Strain Gauge Rosettes

A single strain gauge measures normal strain in one direction only. To determine the full 2D strain state (εx​,εy​,γxy​) at a point, three gauges at known, different angles (a rosette) are needed — each gives one equation via the normal-strain transformation formula above.

0°–60°–120° rosette (gauges a,b,c at those angles from the x-axis): with εa​=εx​ directly,

εy​=32(εb​+εc​)−εa​​,γxy​=3​2(εb​−εc​)​

Once εx​,εy​,γxy​ are recovered, treat the problem as an ordinary strain-transformation/Mohr's-circle problem (find ε1​,ε2​,γmax​, principal directions, etc.). Converting the resulting principal strains to principal stresses for a design check uses the generalized Hooke's law (plane-stress form), which is exactly why strain gauges — which measure strain directly, since stress can't be measured directly — are useful in the first place.

The Strain Tensor

[ε]=(εx​γxy​/2​γxy​/2εy​​),3D: [ε]=​εx​γxy​/2γxz​/2​γxy​/2εy​γyz​/2​γxz​/2γyz​/2εz​​​
