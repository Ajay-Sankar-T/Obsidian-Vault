EE1100 Tutorial 3: AC Circuit Analysis

EE1100tutorialAC-circuitsphasors

← up: MOC · all tutorials · builds on: Tutorial 2 · Source: Tutorial 3 on Analysis of AC Circuits · followed by: AC Transients and Tutorial 4

Twelve questions covering the full arc of single-phase AC analysis: from a raw waveform down to complex power and network theorems. The whole tutorial is one repeated reduction chain:

Time-domain signal→RMS→Phasor→Impedance→Circuit analysis→Power​

For v(t)=Vm​sin(ωt+θv​), the RMS phasor is Vˉ=2​Vm​​∠θv​, and every subsequent step uses the AC form of Ohm's law Vˉ=IˉZ, see Phasors and Complex Algebra.

How this folder is organised

One note per question (T3-Q1 … T3-Q12) with the diagram, the principle and why it applies, the full solution, a check and a trap. The theory notes the questions lean on live in Theory/ (seven atomic notes listed below). Every number is recomputed in ../../Tools/ee_t1_t4_t8.py; python ee_figs_t3_t4.py redraws the figures.

Universal solution workflow

  1. Frequency. Read ω off the source, or compute ω=2πf.
  2. Impedances. ZR​=R, ZL​=jωL, ZC​=−j/(ωC), see Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits.
  3. Source to RMS phasor. Vˉ=2​Vm​​∠θ.
  4. Reduce the circuit. Series/parallel Z, or nodal/mesh (Nodal and Mesh Analysis (AC)), or Thevenin (Thevenin/Norton (AC)); pick by circuit shape, exactly as for DC (Week 2, Week 3).
  5. Solve for current/voltage. Iˉ=Vˉ/Z.
  6. Power. Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=P+jQ, see AC Power and Power Factor.

This is the same six-step reduction that Week 6/Week 7 cover conceptually; this tutorial applies it to twelve concrete problems.

The master memory map

If you remember only one thing, remember this chain:

R→0∘,L→+jωL→+90∘,C→−ωCj​→−90∘ ⇒ Vˉ=IˉZ ⇒ Z=R+j(XL​−XC​) ⇒ Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=P+jQ ⇒ pf=cosϕ

AC circuits = DC circuit laws + complex numbers​

That single idea is why every theorem from Tutorial 1 survives into this tutorial unchanged in structure.

Pattern recognition

You see Do
a non-sinusoidal periodic wave integrate i (average) and i2 (rms) over a period (Q1, Q2)
"i(t)=…sin, find v(t)" peak phasor, multiply by Z, shift the angle (Q3)
angle between V and I given, no component values right-triangle of VR​, VX​ (Q4)
sines and cosines mixed convert everything to sine, then phasors (Q6)
series RLC, "compare element voltages with the source" magnification, L and C cancel (Q7)
"real/reactive power, power factor" P=I2R, Q=I2X, pf=P/∣S∣, and say lead or lag (Q5, Q8)
one unknown node, two sources Millman/nodal (Q9)
"make the current in phase" cancel the imaginary part of the total Z (Q10)
a load fed by a complicated network Thevenin; conjugate matching for max power (Q11, Q12)

Exam-pattern hints

Marks are lost on (i) peak versus rms, (ii) lead versus lag words, (iii) the sign of Q for capacitors, and (iv) adding magnitudes instead of phasors. Q10 is the representative "design" question: the unknown reactance must cancel the imaginary part.

Sheet answers that differ from a direct calculation

  • Q5: printed pf=0.596; exact 0.600 (the power factor of 6−j8).
  • Q7, Q8: the printed 389.75 V, 179.79 W, 449.72/584.27 VAr come from rounding I to 2.12 A; exact 390.0 V, 180 W, 450/585 VAr.
  • Q4: printed 60.14 V; exact 60.10 V.
  • Q12: the printed Q1​=26 VAr, S1​=60.5 VA and Q2​=−13.73 VAr, S2​=15.85 VA are swapped between the sources (correct: source 1 −13.73 VAr, 15.85 VA; source 2 +26.23 VAr, 60.55 VA); "P1​=P2​=23.32 W at maximum power" is each source's share of the load power, each actually delivers 37.5 W.
  • All other printed answers match.
