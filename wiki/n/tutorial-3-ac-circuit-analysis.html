Tutorial 3 — AC Circuit Analysis

EE1100tutorialAC-circuitsphasors

How to attack a problem
  1. Say what is asked and what is given.
  2. Pick the principle before calculating (open the hint).
  3. Solve symbolically, then plug in numbers.
  4. Check units and a limiting case.
  5. Mark it solved only if you could redo it.

← up: MOC · builds on: Tutorial 2 · followed by: AC Transients

Twelve questions (Q1–Q12) covering the full arc of single-phase AC analysis: from a raw sinusoidal waveform down to complex power and network theorems. The whole tutorial is one repeated reduction chain:

Time-domain signal→RMS→Phasor→Impedance→Circuit analysis→Power​

For v(t)=Vm​sin(ωt+θv​), the RMS phasor is Vˉ=2​Vm​​∠θv​, and every subsequent step uses the AC form of Ohm's law Vˉ=IˉZ — see Phasors and Complex Algebra.

Universal solution workflow

  1. Frequency. Read ω off the source, or compute ω=2πf.
  2. Impedances. ZR​=R, ZL​=jωL, ZC​=−j/(ωC) — see Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits.
  3. Source to RMS phasor. Vˉ=2​Vm​​∠θ.
  4. Reduce the circuit. Series/parallel Z, or nodal/mesh (Nodal and Mesh Analysis (AC)), or Thevenin (Thevenin/Norton (AC)) — pick by circuit shape, exactly as for DC (Week 2, Week 3).
  5. Solve for current/voltage. Iˉ=Vˉ/Z.
  6. Power. Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=P+jQ — AC Power and Power Factor.

This is the same six-step reduction as Week 6/Week 7 cover conceptually — this tutorial is where it gets applied to twelve concrete problems.

The master memory map

If you remember only one thing, remember this chain:

R→0∘,L→+jωL→+90∘,C→−ωCj​→−90∘ ⇒ Vˉ=IˉZ ⇒ Z=R+j(XL​−XC​) ⇒ Sˉ=VˉIˉ∗=P+jQ ⇒ pf=cosϕ

AC circuits = DC circuit laws + complex numbers​

That single idea is why every theorem from Tutorial 1 survives into this tutorial unchanged in structure.
