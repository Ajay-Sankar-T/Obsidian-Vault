Standard Form and Homogeneity

Related: 2nd Order ODEs - MOC · First Order ODEs

Standard Form

General form of a 2nd order ODE: F(x,y,y′,y′′)=0(∗)

  • x — independent variable, y — dependent variable
  • Linear 2nd order ODE: a0​(x)y′′+a1​(x)y′+a2​(x)y=a3​(x).
  • If a0​(x)=0 for every x in the domain of y, divide by a0​(x):

y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=R(x)

where P(x)=a0​(x)a1​(x)​, Q(x)=a0​(x)a2​(x)​, R(x)=a0​(x)a3​(x)​. P,Q are the coefficients of the DE.

Standard Form of the 2nd order ODE:

y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=R(x).(1)

Otherwise, (∗) is called nonlinear.

Homogeneous vs Non-homogeneous

  • If R(x)≡0 for all x considered, y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0 is a homogeneous DE.
  • If R(x)≡0, (1) is non-homogeneous.

Examples:

  1. y′′+25y=e−xcosx (linear, non-homogeneous)
  2. y′′+25y=0 (linear, homogeneous)
  3. xy′′+y′+xy=0⇒y′′+x1​y′+y=0, x=0 (linear, homogeneous)
  4. y′′y+y′2=0 (nonlinear)
