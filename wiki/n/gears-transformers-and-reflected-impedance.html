Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance

synthesiscross-domaingearstransformersimpedance

← up: Cross-Domain Synthesis MOC

A gear pair and an ideal transformer are the same device: a lossless two-port that trades one half of a power product for the other at a fixed ratio. Using the force-voltage dictionary already fixed in The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy (effort ↔ voltage, flow ↔ current), torque plays the role of voltage and angular speed the role of current.

The dictionary

Gear pair (A drives B) Ideal transformer (1 → 2)
torque T (effort) voltage V
angular speed ω (flow) current I
tooth numbers NA​,NB​ turns N1​,N2​
ωB​ωA​​=NA​NB​​ I2​I1​​=N1​N2​​
TA​TB​​=NA​NB​​ (magnitudes) V1​V2​​=N1​N2​​
TA​ωA​=TB​ωB​ (no friction) V1​I1​=V2​I2​ (ideal)
external mesh reverses sense dot convention / winding sense
friction, tooth losses copper and core loss

Gear side: speed ratio from Gear Formula Bank §10, nA​/nB​=NB​/NA​; the power statement is the epicyclic relation M1​ω1​+M2​ω2​+M3​ω3​=0 of §12 specialised to two members. Transformer side: Week 10, a=N1​/N2​≈V1​/V2​≈I2​/I1​.

Reflected impedance = reflected inertia

Move a load across the box and it is scaled by the square of the ratio.

  • Transformer: a load ZL​ on the secondary looks like Z′=a2ZL​ from the primary, a=N1​/N2​.
  • Gears: a load inertia JL​ on shaft B looks like Jref​=(NA​/NB​)2JL​ from shaft A.

The square comes from conserving energy, not from the geometry. Equate kinetic energies, 21​Jref​ωA2​=21​JL​ωB2​, and use ωB​/ωA​=NA​/NB​. For the transformer, a quantity that scales as V/I scales as (ratio)×(ratio). Inertia is the mechanical "inductance" in the dictionary, so a load inertia reflects exactly like a load inductance.

A consequence from either side: matching

With a motor of rotor inertia Jm​ and torque Tm​ driving a load JL​ through speed ratio n=ωm​/ωL​, the load acceleration is

αL​=n2Jm​+JL​nTm​​.

Setting ∂αL​/∂n=0 gives n2Jm​=JL​, i.e. JL​/n2=Jm​: the reflected load inertia equals the rotor inertia. This is the same stationarity condition as maximum power transfer, ZL​=ZTh∗​: a transformer or gear is the knob that makes the reflected load match the source. The gear ratio is chosen, not the load.

The motor sits between: a gyrator

DC Machines give Eb​=kΦN and T=k′ΦIa​. Electrical power converted equals mechanical power, Eb​Ia​=Tω, so with N in rpm the two constants are tied: k′=60k/2π. In SI units both constants are the same K: Eb​=Kω, T=KIa​. That swaps the roles: voltage ↔ speed, current ↔ torque. A two-port that does this is a gyrator, not a transformer. A whole drive train reads as

electrical source (V,I) → gyrator (motor) → (T,ω) → transformer-like (gears) → shaft → load

The shaft in the middle is a torsional spring of stiffness GJ/L (Torsion of Circular Shafts) and carries power P=Tω (Power Transmission by Shafts), the mechanical twin of P=VI.

Common traps

  • Reflecting stiffness or inertia by the ratio instead of its square.
  • Forgetting the sign on external meshes: the transformer dot convention is the same bookkeeping.
  • Treating the gear ratio like a free design parameter in a drive train: the matching condition above fixes the optimum.
