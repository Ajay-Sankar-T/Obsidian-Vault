Separation of Variables

differential-equationspdesconceptseparation-of-variableseigenvalue-problem

Related: PDEs - MOC · Sturm-Liouville Problems · Wave Equation on a Finite Interval · Heat Equation - Separation of Variables · Laplace Equation on Rectangles and Strips · solved in Assignment 9 - D'Alembert, Wave and Heat Equations - Solutions Q3

What it is

Look for solutions of a linear homogeneous PDE in product form u(x,y)=X(x)Y(y). If the PDE can be written with x-terms on one side and y-terms on the other,

XX′​ (or XX′′​)=Yfunction of y​=λ(a constant),

the PDE splits into two ODEs joined by the separation constant λ. The solutions for admissible λn​ are added (superposition), and the extra data fix the coefficients.

Why it works

The operator and the boundary conditions are linear and homogeneous, so products of eigenfunctions of the spatial part are solutions and so is their sum. The spatial ODE with its boundary conditions is a Sturm–Liouville eigenproblem: it has solutions only for special λn​, its eigenfunctions are orthogonal, and any initial or boundary profile can be expanded in them, which determines the coefficients.

Method

  1. Substitute u=XY and separate. A step that does not separate means the method (in this form) fails.
  2. Impose the homogeneous boundary conditions on the space factor to get the eigenproblem X′′+λX=0 with λn​,Xn​. (If the boundary conditions are not homogeneous, first subtract a simple function that satisfies them: Heat Equation - Separation of Variables.)
  3. Solve the other ODE for each λn​.
  4. Superpose: u=∑cn​Xn​Yn​.
  5. Fit the remaining data with an orthogonal expansion: cn​=⟨Xn​,Xn​⟩⟨f,Xn​⟩​.

First-order examples

  • y3ux​+x2uy​=0: x2XX′​=−y3YY′​=k gives X=ekx3/3, Y=e−ky4/4. With u(x,0)=ex3/3: k=1, u=ex3/3−y4/4.
  • ux​=2ut​+u: XX′​=λ, T=e(λ−1)t/2; data 6e−3x gives λ=−3, u=6e−3x−2t.
  • uxx​=uy​+2u with u(0,y)=0, ux​(0,y)=1+e−3y: X′′=λX, Y=e(λ−2)y: the e0⋅y term needs λ=2 (X=2​1​sinh2​x) and e−3y needs λ=−1 (X=sinx): u=2​1​sinh2​x+e−3ysinx.
Traps
  • Superposing before imposing the boundary conditions, or forgetting that non-homogeneous conditions break superposition of the boundary data.
  • Dropping λ≤0 without checking them; with Neumann or periodic ends λ=0 is a genuine eigenvalue (a constant mode).
  • Taking the product ansatz on a region whose boundary is not a coordinate line (use polar coordinates for a disk).
