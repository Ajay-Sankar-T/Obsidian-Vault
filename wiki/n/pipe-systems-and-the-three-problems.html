Pipe Systems and the Three Problems

conceptfluid-mechanicspipe-flowpipe-systemspump-curve

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Darcy–Weisbach, Minor Losses, Moody Chart · formulas: Pipe Bank §5 · problems: Solved Problems

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: a pipe system is solved with three rules: the energy equation between two known points, continuity at every junction (flow in = flow out), and the same head difference across parallel branches.

Intuition: this is circuit analysis. Head plays the role of voltage and flow rate the role of current; a pipe is a nonlinear resistor Δh=RQ2. Series elements carry the same current (Q) and add voltage drops; parallel elements share the same voltage drop and add currents.

The three pipe problems

Unknown Known Method
Δp (or hl​) Q (or V), D, L, e Re→f→hl​. Direct.
Q Δp, D, L, e guess f (fully rough), get V, then Re, new f; 2–3 passes.
D Δp, Q, L, e guess D, compute Δp, adjust (or solve with Haaland), then round up to a standard size.

Where: R = hydraulic resistance (s²/m⁵); Hp​ = pump head; Δz = static lift.

Assumptions: steady incompressible flow, fully turbulent (constant f per pipe, adjusted iteratively), junction losses folded into K.

When To Use

  • Water supply, siphons, drainage, hydraulic circuits, pump selection.
  • Several pipes in series (changes of diameter) or in parallel (looped mains).
  • Any problem where the unknown is the flow rate or the diameter, not the pressure drop.

Recognition pattern: "how much flows", "what diameter is needed", "two pipes in parallel", "pump operating point" → pipe system.

Method

  1. Identify the unknown (head loss, Q, or D) and the two reference points for the energy equation.
  2. Choose series, parallel or network structure; label flows and heads.
  3. Apply the energy equation (with hp​ and all losses) plus continuity and the parallel head rule.
  4. Iterate when f depends on the unknown V: guess fully rough f, solve for V, update Re and f (converges in 2–3 passes).
  5. Check: Re regime, positive pressure (above pv​, see Vapour Pressure and Cavitation), junction continuity, and round pipe diameters up to standard sizes.

Derivation / Why It Works

Express each pipe's loss as hl​=(fL/D+∑K)V2/2g and substitute V=4Q/πD2: hl​=RQ2. Series pipes carry the same Q, so their losses add. For parallel pipes, the pressure at the two junctions is common, so each branch has the same hl​: R1​Q12​=R2​Q22​. At fixed f, R∝L/D5, so for equal lengths Q1​/Q2​=(D1​/D2​)5/2. The pump–system intersection follows from requiring the head the pump delivers equal the head the system needs at the same flow.

Common Traps

  • Trap: adding flows in series or head losses in parallel.
  • Avoid it by: series = same Q, parallel = same Δh.
  • Trap: assuming a constant f across different pipes or after the flow changes.
  • Trap: choosing the next smaller standard diameter in the design problem (the loss then exceeds the budget).
  • Trap: treating a pump curve as a constant head.
  • Trap: forgetting the exit kinetic energy: a free jet carries V2/2g out.

Example

Given

(a) Find Q: water at 20 °C (ν=1.01×10−6 m²/s) drains from a tank through a 50 mm commercial-steel pipe (e=0.046 mm), L=50 m, with a square-edged entrance (K=0.5), discharging as a free jet 10 m below the tank surface.

(b) Parallel pipes: two pipes of equal length with DA​=100 mm and DB​=50 mm and the same f.

(c) Pump operating point (illustrative curves): Hsys​=10+2000Q2 and Hp​=30−5000Q2 (Q in m³/s, heads in m).

Solution

(a) Energy from the surface to the jet: gH=2V2​(1+K+fDL​), so V=1.5+fL/D2gH​​.

Pass f V (m/s) Re
1 (fully rough guess) 0.0192 3.08 1.5×105
converged 0.0212 2.94 1.46×105

Q=VA=2.94×1.963×10−3=5.8 L/s.

(b) Same f and L: R∝D−5, so QA​/QB​=25/2=5.66 and pipe A carries 5.66/6.66=85% of the flow.

(c) 10+2000Q2=30−5000Q2 ⇒ Q2=2.857×10−3, Q=0.0535 m³/s, H=15.7 m.

Answer: (a) 5.8 L/s; (b) the 100 mm pipe takes 85 %; (c) 53.5 L/s at 15.7 m. Key takeaway: twice the diameter carries 5.7 times the flow at the same head, so a modest oversize of the pipe pays off enormously; and the first iteration in (a) was already within 5 % of the converged velocity.
