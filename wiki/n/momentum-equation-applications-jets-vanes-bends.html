Jet hitting a flat plate

A jet with velocity V strikes a plate and stops (in the flow direction): V1x​=V, V2x​=0.

Fx​=m˙(0−V)⇒F=m˙V=ρAV⋅V⟹F=ρAV2​

Jet deflected by a vane

V1​=Vi, outlet at angle θ: V2​=Vcosθi+Vsinθj.

Fx​=m˙V(cosθ−1)​Fy​=m˙Vsinθ​

(forces on the fluid by the vane — the force of fluid on the vane is the negative of these, per Linear Momentum Equation's step 9).

Nozzle

Continuity (Conservation of Mass): A1​V1​=A2​V2​⇒V2​=A2​A1​​V1​. Since A2​<A1​, V2​>V1​ — the nozzle accelerates the flow. The momentum equation on the nozzle as a whole must include pressure forces at the inlet/outlet and the wall reaction holding the nozzle in place.

Pipe bend

Even if V1​=V2​ (same speed), a force is still required because the direction of velocity changes — momentum is a vector, and turning it (even at constant speed) requires a net force, exactly as Phasors and Complex Algebra requires vector (not scalar) addition for anything with both magnitude and direction.

For a 90° bend: V1x​=V1​, V1y​=0 (inlet) and V2x​=0, V2y​=V2​ (outlet):

∑Fx​=−m˙V1​​∑Fy​=m˙V2​​

plus any pressure, weight, and support forces acting on the bend.
