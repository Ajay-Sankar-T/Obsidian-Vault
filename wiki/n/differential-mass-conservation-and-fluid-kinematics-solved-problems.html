1. P4.1 Acceleration of an unsteady 3-D field

Given. V=4txi+2t2yj+4xzk. Steady? Dimension? a at (−1,1,0).

Principle. a=∂V/∂t+(V⋅∇)V.

Steps.

ax​=4x+(4tx)(4t)=4x+16t2x,ay​=4ty+(2t2y)(2t2)=4ty+4t4y,

az​=(4tx)(4z)+(4xz)(4x)=16txz+16x2z

At (−1,1,0):  a=(−4−16t2)i+(4t+4t4)j​ (and az​=0). The flow is unsteady (explicit t) and three-dimensional (u,v,w all depend on x,y,z, and w is nonzero away from z=0). A unit vector normal to a is k (or (4+16t2, 4t+4t4)/norm rotated in the plane).

2. P4.2 / P4.6 Converging nozzle

Given. u=V0​(1+2x/L), v=w=0, V0​=10 ft/s, L=0.5 ft (=6 in).

Steps. Steady, so only convection:

ax​=udxdu​=V02​(1+L2x​)L2​

  • x=0: a=L2V02​​=400 ft/s² =12.4g​
  • x=L: a=L6V02​​=1200 ft/s² =37.3g​

Transit time (P4.6). dtdx​=V0​(1+2x/L)⇒t=2V0​L​ln(1+L2x​), so for x=L:  t=2V0​L​ln3=0.55V0​L​​ (=0.027 s here).

Trap. "Steady" does not mean zero acceleration; the particle accelerates because it moves into a faster region.

3. P4.9 Constant for incompressible and irrotational flow

Given. V=(3y2−3x2)i+Cxyj.

  • Incompressible: ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​=−6x+Cx=0⇒C=6.
  • Irrotational: ζz​=∂x∂v​−∂y∂u​=Cy−6y=0⇒C=6.

C=6 for both​

(Coincidence of the two conditions: V=∇ϕ with ϕ=3xy2−x3, which is harmonic.)

4. Ex. 4.2 and 4.3 Using continuity

Ex. 4.2. V linear in x,y,z with constants ai​,bi​,ci​: ∇⋅V=a1​+b2​+c3​=0. Only the diagonal constants matter; b1​,c1​,a2​,c2​,a3​,b3​ are free (they shear and rotate but do not change volume).

Ex. 4.3. u=a(x2−y2), w=b, v unknown: ∂v/∂y=−2ax⇒v=−2axy+f(x,z,t).

5. P4.13 A flow with a 1/r velocity

Given. u=x2+y2Kx​, v=x2+y2Ky​.

Steps.

  1. ∇⋅V=0 for r=0 (sympy ✓): incompressible continuity satisfied.
  2. Polar components: vr​=ucosθ+vsinθ=rK​,  vθ​=−usinθ+vcosθ=0.

Interpretation. Purely radial with vr​=K/r: a line source of strength m=2πK (sink if K<0); singular at the origin, where continuity fails because mass is created.

6. P4.22 Density change in a nozzle

Given. Steady, 1-D compressible flow, A=A0​[1−x/(2L)], V:76→167 m/s from x=0 to L, ρ(0)=2.0 kg/m³.

Principle. Steady continuity ρAV=const.

ρL​=ρ0​AL​VL​A0​V0​​=2.0×0.5×16776​=1.82 kg/m3​

(The density falls 9 % while the speed more than doubles, which is why Ma>0.3 flows need the compressible form.)

7. P4.47 and P4.49 Stream functions

P4.47. V=3yi+2xj.

  • Continuity: ux​+vy​=0+0=0 ✓.
  • u=ψy​=3y⇒ψ=23​y2+f(x); v=−ψx​=2x⇒f=−x2:

ψ=23​y2−x2​(hyperbolas, centre at the origin)

  • Vorticity ζz​=vx​−uy​=2−3=−1=0: rotational, so no velocity potential exists.

P4.49. ψ=K(x2−y2): u=ψy​=−2Ky, v=−ψx​=−2Kx.

  • Stagnation point where u=v=0: the origin only.
  • ζz​=vx​−uy​=−2K+2K=0: irrotational (∇2ψ=0).
  • Streamlines y=±x are the separating lines; this is stagnation-point (corner) flow rotated by 45° (compare ψ=Kxy).

8. P4.54 Flow through a surface from ψ

Given. ψ=L2U​(3x2y−y3); surface joining (2L,0) to (0,L) with width b.

ψ(0,L)=L2U​(−L3)=−UL,ψ(2L,0)=0

Q=b∣Δψ∣=ULb​

Direction: ψB​−ψA​<0, so the flow crosses to the left of the path A→B, i.e. toward the origin side. The field is the same cubic pattern as Ex. 4.7/4.9 (there ψ=a(x2y−y3/3), here a=3U/L2).

9. P4.56 and Ex. 4.9 Velocity potentials

P4.56. ϕ=Kxy: u=ϕx​=Ky, v=ϕy​=Kx; ∇2ϕ=0 ✓; stagnation point at the origin. Stream function: u=ψy​=Ky⇒ψ=2K​y2+f(x); v=−ψx​=Kx⇒f=−2K​x2:

ψ=2K​(y2−x2)

Potential lines xy=c (hyperbolas in the quadrants) are orthogonal to ψ lines y2−x2=c′ (rotated by 45°): corner/stagnation flow.

Ex. 4.9. u=a(x2−y2), v=−2axy: ζz​=−2ay+2ay=0 ✓; ϕ=a(3x3​−xy2) (verified: ϕx​=u, ϕy​=v). Streamlines and potential lines have the same shape rotated by 30° (the angle at the origin where both u,v→0).

10. P4.59 Deforming fluid element

Given. V=xi−yj; element with corners (2,2),(3,2),(3,3),(2,3) at t=0. Find its shape at t=0.5.

Steps. x˙=x⇒x=x0​et; y˙​=−y⇒y=y0​e−t. At t=0.5: e0.5=1.6487, e−0.5=0.6065.

corner t=0 t=0.5
1 (2, 2) (3.30, 1.21)
2 (3, 2) (4.95, 1.21)
3 (3, 3) (4.95, 1.82)
4 (2, 3) (3.30, 1.82)

Rectangle 1.65×0.61; area =1.6487×0.6065=1.000 (unchanged).

Interpretation. Right angles stay right angles ⇒ no angular deformation net and no rotation: irrotational (ζz​=0). Area conserved ⇒ incompressible (∇⋅V=1−1=0). Linear deformation ε˙xx​=+1, ε˙yy​=−1 only. See the figure in the Formula Bank.

12. P4.60 Draining vortex

Given. vr​=vz​=0, vθ​=ΩR2/r (the problem's constant; symbol lost in the PDF text layer – I take it as Ω with units 1/s), water depth z=H far from the hole, free surface at atmospheric pressure.

Rotational? ζz​=r1​drd(rvθ​)​=r1​drd(ΩR2)​=0 ⇒ irrotational (free vortex).

Depth at r=R. Irrotational ⇒ one Bernoulli constant for the whole flow. At the free surface p=pa​: 21​V2+gz=const=gH (far away V→0). At r=R: V=ΩR:

zC​=H−2gΩ2R2​​

Near the axis zs​=H−Ω2R4/(2gr2)→−∞: the surface plunges and the model fails at small r (a real vortex has a viscous core).
