Transformer Voltage Regulation and Efficiency

EE1100concepttransformervoltage-regulationefficiency

Related: 10 Single Phase Transformers (equivalent circuit, OC and SC tests) · EE1100 MOC · tested in Tutorial 7 on Transformer (Q8–Q10) · animation: Parallel flux lines

What it is

Two numbers characterise the performance of a transformer: how much the secondary voltage sags under load (voltage regulation) and how much of the input power reaches the load (efficiency).

Voltage regulation

With the primary voltage held constant, regulation is the change in secondary voltage when the full load is thrown off. E2​ is the no-load secondary voltage and V2​ the full-load terminal voltage:

%VR=V2,rated​E2​−V2​​×100(regulation up),%VRdown​=E2​E2​−V2​​×100.

Referred to the secondary, E2​=V2​+I2​(re2​+jxe2​) with re2​=r2​+r1′​, xe2​=x2​+x1′​. From the phasor diagram, for load power-factor angle ϕ2​ (+ for lagging),

E2​−V2​≈I2​re2​cosϕ2​±I2​xe2​sinϕ2​  ⟹  VR≈re2,pu​cosϕ2​±xe2,pu​sinϕ2​​

(sign + for lagging, − for leading).

Zero regulation. VR=0 when tanϕ2​=−re2​/xe2​, i.e. ϕ2​=−tan−1(re2​/xe2​): a leading current (capacitive load). A larger capacitive load gives negative regulation (the voltage rises). A normal inductive load gives positive regulation; a good transformer keeps it to a few percent.

Approximation. The formula neglects the quadrature part of the drop; it is accurate when the drop is small (it is the form used in the lecture and in the tutorial answers). The exact value comes from V1′2​=(V2​+I2​re2​cosϕ±I2​xe2​sinϕ)2+(I2​xe2​cosϕ∓I2​re2​sinϕ)2.

Efficiency

η=Pin​Pout​​=V2​I2​cosϕ2​+I22​re2​+Pc​V2​I2​cosϕ2​​.

  • Copper (ohmic) loss I22​re2​: variable, grows as the square of the load.
  • Core loss Pc​=Ph​+Pe​ (hysteresis + eddy current): fixed, depends on the voltage and frequency, not on the load.

Maximum efficiency

Differentiate η with respect to I2​ (at fixed V2​, cosϕ2​) and set it to zero:

I22​re2​=Pc​​(variable loss = fixed loss),I2​=Pc​/re2​​

and d2η/dI22​<0 confirms a maximum. Then

ηmax​=V2​I2​cosϕ2​+2Pc​V2​I2​cosϕ2​​.

In terms of the load fraction x=I2​/I2,rated​: xopt​=Pc​/Pcu,FL​​ and the load at maximum efficiency is xopt​× kVA rating. Note ηmax​ depends on the power factor, but the load at which it occurs does not.

Worked example (Lecture 39)

20 kVA, 2500/500 V, 50 Hz. HV: r1​=2.5 Ω, x1​=10 Ω; LV: r2​=0.1 Ω, x2​=0.4 Ω; core loss 200 W. Ratio N2​/N1​=51​.

  • Referred to LV: re2​=0.1+252.5​=0.2 Ω, xe2​=0.4+2510​=0.8 Ω; I2​=50020000​=40 A.
  • (a) 0.8 lagging: E2​−V2​=40(0.2⋅0.8+0.8⋅0.6)=25.6 V, VR=50025.6​=5.12 %. 0.8 leading: E2​−V2​=40(0.2⋅0.8−0.8⋅0.6)=−12.8 V, VR=−2.56 % (the lecture rounds this to −12 V and −2.4 %).
  • (b) Efficiency at 0.8 lagging: half load (I2​=20 A): 8000+202(0.2)+2008000​=96.6 %; full load: 16000+320+20016000​=96.9 %.
  • (c) Maximum efficiency: I2​=200/0.2​=31.6 A =79 % of full load; ηmax​=500⋅31.6⋅0.8+400500⋅31.6⋅0.8​=96.9 %.

Per-unit method (Lecture 39, Tutorial Q10). When voltages are not given, work with V2​=1 pu, I2​=1 pu. If ηmax​=98 % at 0.8 load, 0.8 pf, 100 kVA: Pout​=64 kW, Pc​=Pcu​(0.8 load)=653.06 W, full-load copper loss =653.06/0.64=1020 W so re2​=0.0102 pu. The lecture then takes xe2​=0.05 pu (the whole 5 % impedance), which gives VR=3.82 %. Splitting the impedance correctly, xe2​=0.052−0.01022​=0.0489 pu and VR=0.0102(0.8)+0.0489(0.6)=3.75 %, the sheet's answer (see T7-Q10 - Maximum Efficiency and Regulation).

Traps
  • Using the wrong sign for leading loads: xe2​sinϕ2​ changes sign with the load angle.
  • Copper loss at full load versus at the test current: the short-circuit test may be run below rated current. Scale by (Irated​/Itest​)2 (T7-Q9 - Efficiency and Regulation from Tests).
  • The load at maximum efficiency does not depend on cosϕ, but the value of ηmax​ does.
  • Percent regulation can be quoted against E2​ or V2,rated​; the values differ slightly.

Cross-domain links

  • Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance — the same bookkeeping as for a gearbox: a fixed (no-load) loss plus a load-squared loss, so the best efficiency is at the load where they are equal.
  • Thevenin, Norton and Maximum Power Transfer (AC) — "variable = fixed" is the same algebraic optimum as RL​=RTh​, but here it maximises a ratio (efficiency), not power, and sits at about 80 % of full load, not at the matched point.
