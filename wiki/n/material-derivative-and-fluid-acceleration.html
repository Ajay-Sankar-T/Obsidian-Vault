Material Derivative and Fluid Acceleration

fluid-mechanicskinematicsmaterial-derivative

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Lagrangian and Eulerian Descriptions

The question it answers

How does a fluid particle experience a quantity as it moves through a (possibly steady) field?

Definition

For a scalar field ϕ(x,y,z,t):

DtDϕ​=∂t∂ϕ​+V⋅∇ϕ​=∂t∂ϕ​+u∂x∂ϕ​+v∂y∂ϕ​+w∂z∂ϕ​

Two parts

Local (temporal) change — ∂t∂ϕ​ — the change at a fixed point, present even if no fluid were moving through it.

Convective (advective) change — V⋅∇ϕ — the change a particle experiences purely because it is moving through a spatially varying field, even if the field itself is frozen in time (∂ϕ/∂t=0).

Material change=Local change+Convective change​

Fluid acceleration

Applying the material derivative to velocity itself:

a=DtDV​=∂t∂V​+(V⋅∇)V​

Cartesian components:

ax​=∂t∂u​+u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​+w∂z∂u​

ay​=∂t∂v​+u∂x∂v​+v∂y∂v​+w∂z∂v​

az​=∂t∂w​+u∂x∂w​+v∂y∂w​+w∂z∂w​

Critical point: steady flow does NOT necessarily mean zero acceleration​ — "steady" only kills the local term ∂V/∂t; the convective term (V⋅∇)V survives whenever the velocity field varies in space, which it does in almost any real flow (e.g. fluid speeding up through a nozzle in steady flow is still accelerating).

V=(x,−y)">

Worked example: V=(x,−y)

u=x, v=−y, no time dependence so ∂V/∂t=0.

ax​=u∂x∂u​+v∂y∂u​=x(1)+(−y)(0)=x

ay​=u∂x∂v​+v∂y∂v​=x(0)+(−y)(−1)=y

a=(x,y)​

— nonzero and growing away from the origin, despite the flow being perfectly steady; this is the streamline pattern xy=C (a stagnation-point flow), and the fluid genuinely accelerates as it moves outward/inward along those hyperbolas.

Cross-domain: material derivative as a moving-frame time constant

The split "local change + convective change" is structurally the chain rule applied along a moving trajectory — the exact same operation that turns a spatial exponential decay (σ(x)∼e−x/ℓ, Saint-Venant's principle) into a temporal one when the observer moves at fixed speed through it. See Exponential Decay — Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation for the general spatial/temporal-decay unification this belongs to.
