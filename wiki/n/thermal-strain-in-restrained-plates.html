The pattern (Q6): a rectangular plate heated inside a rigid, oversized opening

εT​=αT​ΔT​ΔL=αT​LΔT​

Total strain combines mechanical and thermal contributions (superposition — the thermal strain simply adds to the mechanical strain from Generalized Hooke's Law):

εx​=Eσx​−νσy​​+αT​ΔT​,εy​=Eσy​−νσx​​+αT​ΔT​

   gap δ         gap δ
  ┌──┬──────────────┬──┐
  │  │   plate a×b   │  │   rigid frame, opening larger than plate
  │  │  (a > b)      │  │   by gap δ on each side
  └──┴──────────────┴──┘

Stage 1 — before touching the walls

The plate is completely free: σx​=σy​=0​, so all the strain is thermal:

εx​=εy​=αT​ΔT​

Stage 2 — the first gap closes

Side a (the longer side) needs ΔTa​ to expand by the gap δ: αT​aΔTa​=δ⇒ΔTa​=δ/(αT​a)​. Side b needs ΔTb​=δ/(αT​b)​. Since a>b: ΔTa​<ΔTb​​ — the longer side closes its gap first (it has farther to expand relative to... actually it needs less temperature rise to cover the same absolute gap δ, since ΔL=αT​LΔT grows with L for fixed ΔT). Between ΔTa​ and ΔTb​, only one direction is restrained; the other is still free, exactly the mixed Case-2-style restraint pattern from Restrained and Confined Deformation.

Stage 3 — both directions restrained (ΔT>ΔTb​)

εx​=εy​=0​

By symmetry of restraint, σx​=σy​=σ. Substituting into the total-strain equation:

0=Eσ(1−ν)​+αT​ΔT⟹σx​=σy​=−1−νEαT​ΔT​​

Heating a fully restrained plate produces compressive stress — the plate "wants" to expand, the rigid frame won't let it, so the prevented expansion appears as internal compression, exactly the biaxial generalization of the uniaxial thermal-stress result σ=−EαΔT already derived in Thermal Stresses — here divided by (1−ν) because restraint acts in two directions simultaneously and each direction's Poisson coupling to the other partially resists the restraint's own stress buildup (the same (1−ν) or (1−2ν) denominator pattern seen in Restrained and Confined Deformation's confined-block cases).
