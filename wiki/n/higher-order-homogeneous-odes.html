Higher Order Homogeneous ODEs

Related: Variation of Parameters · 2nd Order Linear ODE with Constant Coefficients · Undetermined Coefficients - Higher Order

An n-th order linear homogeneous ODE with constant coefficients:

an​y(n)+an−1​y(n−1)+⋯+a1​y′+a0​y=0,an​=0.

Try y=erx: substituting gives the auxiliary/characteristic equation

an​rn+an−1​rn−1+⋯+a1​r+a0​=0,

a degree-n polynomial with exactly n roots (real or complex, with multiplicity). Each root determines one basis solution; together they span an n-dimensional solution space.

Case 1: Distinct Real Roots

r1​,…,rn​ real and distinct ⇒ basis er1​x,…,ern​x (linearly independent, generalized Wronskian nonzero).

y=c1​er1​x+c2​er2​x+⋯+cn​ern​x

Case 2: Repeated Real Roots

A real root r of multiplicity m contributes m independent solutions (via repeated Reduction of Order):

erx, xerx, x2erx, …, xm−1erx

contributing (c1​+c2​x+⋯+cm​xm−1)erx to y.

Case 3: Complex Roots

Since coefficients are real, complex roots occur in conjugate pairs α±iβ. Each simple pair contributes eαxcosβx, eαxsinβx. If repeated with multiplicity m, the pair contributes 2m solutions:

xkeαxcosβx,xkeαxsinβx,k=0,1,…,m−1.

Fundamental Set and Generalized Wronskian

The solution space is n-dimensional. A set of n linearly independent solutions {y1​,…,yn​} is a fundamental set, verified by

W(y1​,…,yn​)(x)=det​y1​y1′​⋮y1(n−1)​​⋯⋯⋯​yn​yn′​⋮yn(n−1)​​​=0.

General solution: y=c1​y1​+⋯+cn​yn​.

Step-by-step procedure

  1. Write the auxiliary equation from the coefficients.
  2. Solve for all roots and their multiplicities.
  3. For each distinct real root: include erx,xerx,… up to its multiplicity.
  4. For each conjugate complex pair: include eαxcosβx,eαxsinβx,… up to its multiplicity.
  5. Combine with arbitrary constants for the general solution.
  6. Apply initial conditions if given.
