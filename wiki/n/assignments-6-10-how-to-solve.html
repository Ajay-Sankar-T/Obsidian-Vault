What this page is for

"I see this kind of problem: what do I do, in what order, and how do I know I am right?" A recognition table for every problem type on Assignments 6–10 (§1), then a recipe with one worked example for each family (§2–§8), a list of 30-second checks (§9) and a map from each sheet to the skills it tests (§10). The formulas themselves are in the Assignments 6-10 - Formula Bank; this page is about choosing and sequencing.

2. Bessel problems (Assignment 6)

Recipe A: an integral of xpJν​

  1. Write xpJν​=xp−ν−1⋅xν+1Jν​ and note xν+1Jν​=(xν+1Jν+1​)′.
  2. Integrate by parts: ∫xpJν​=xpJν+1​−(p−ν−1)∫xp−1Jν+1​.
  3. Repeat. It stops when the power reaches the matching exponent (closed form), or ends in ∫J0​ (no closed form).
  4. Differentiate the result.
Worked: ∫01​x3J0​(x)dx

p=3, ν=0, so p+ν=3 is odd: a closed form exists. ∫x3J0​=∫x2(xJ1​)′=x3J1​−2∫x2J1​=x3J1​−2x2J2​ (since (x2J2​)′=x2J1​). At the limits: J1​(1)−2J2​(1)=0.44005−0.22981=0.21024. Quadrature agrees.

Recipe B: reduce an ODE to Bessel form

  1. Make the equation monic: y′′+….
  2. Compare with y′′+x1−2a​y′+(b2c2x2c−2+x2a2−n2c2​)y=0.
  3. Read a from the y′ term, c from the exponent in the y term, then b, then n.
  4. Write y=xa[AJn​(bxc)+BJ−n​(bxc)] (use Yn​ if n is an integer).
Worked: y′′+9xy=0

No y′ term: 1−2a=0, so a=21​. b2c2x2c−2=9x: 2c−2=1, c=23​, b2⋅49​=9, b=2. a2−n2c2=0: n=a/c=31​. Hence y=x​[AJ1/3​(2x3/2)+BJ−1/3​(2x3/2)]. (Residual <10−8 at three points.)

Recipe C: Fourier–Bessel coefficients

  1. Find αn​ from the boundary condition (Jν​(α)=0 for y(L)=0).
  2. Substitute t=αn​x/L: ∫0L​xf(x)Jν​(Lαn​x​)dx=(αn​L​)2∫0αn​​tf(αn​Lt​)Jν​(t)dt.
  3. Close the integral with Recipe A (at a zero of Jν​, R3 simplifies the result).
  4. Divide by the norm 2L2​Jν+1​(αn​)2. Check with f=xν: an​=αn​Jν+1​(αn​)2​.

Recipe D: eigenvalues

  1. Put the equation in S–L form to get the weight r (the only thing orthogonality needs).
  2. Reduce to z′′+μz=0 if possible (the diagram above).
  3. For each sign of μ write the general solution and impose both boundary conditions; the nontrivial case is where the determinant of the 2×2 system vanishes.
  4. List λn​, yn​, the weight; check that λ=0 was not missed; check the number of interior zeros (n−1 for Dirichlet).
Worked: x2y′′+xy′+λy=0, y(1)=0, y′(e2)=0

t=lnx: Y′′+λY=0, Y(0)=0, Y′(2)=0 (since y′=Y′/x). Dirichlet–Neumann on length 2: Y=sinμt, μcos2μ=0, μn​=4(2n−1)π​. So λn​=μn2​, yn​=sin(μn​lnx), weight x1​ (S–L form (xy′)′+xλ​y=0).

Recipe E: expand a function

  1. Match the basis to the boundary conditions (sine for Dirichlet, cosine for Neumann).
  2. an​=∫rfyn​/∫ryn2​ with the weight.
  3. Predict convergence: f breaks a boundary condition of the basis (or jumps) ⇒an​∼n−1 and Gibbs; matches it ⇒an​∼n−2 or faster.
  4. Check with x= a convenient point (a series of known sum) or Parseval.

4. First-order PDEs (Assignment 8)

Recipe F: Lagrange

  1. Write P,Q,R from Pp+Qq=R.
  2. Two independent first integrals: try the pair of ratios that omit the third variable; then multipliers (1,1,1), (x,y,u), (x1​,y1​,u1​); then, if the coefficients are linear, the eigen-method.
  3. Verify each integral: Pφx​+Qφy​+Rφu​=0.
  4. General solution F(φ,ψ)=0, or solve for u.
  5. With data on a curve: parametrize, find c1​(s),c2​(s), eliminate s, check transversality Py0′​−Qx0′​=0.
Worked: xp+yq=2u

xdx​=ydy​=2udu​: xy​=c1​ and x2u​=c2​. General solution u=x2f(y/x): every homogeneous function of degree 2 (Euler's theorem). With u(1,y)=y one gets f(y)=y, so u=xy.

Recipe G: when it goes wrong

Symptom Reason Action
∂(φ,ψ) degenerate the two integrals are dependent find another
Data curve is a characteristic no or infinitely many solutions compare u0′​ with Rx0′​/P
Solution blows up at finite y characteristics cross (Burgers) yb​=−1/minu0′​
No singular integral envelope equations inconsistent (∂b​f=1) say so; do not invent one

5. Second-order classification and canonical form (Assignment 8)

Recipe H

  1. Read a,b,c; compute Δ=b2−4ac; state the type and the region if it varies.
  2. Solve dxdy​=2ab±Δ​​. Hyperbolic: two real families; parabolic: one; elliptic: complex conjugates.
  3. Set ξ,η (parabolic: second variable any independent function; elliptic: real and imaginary parts).
  4. Compute A′,B′,C′,D′,E′ (they must give A′=C′=0, B′=0 for hyperbolic).
  5. Divide by the leading coefficient. If the constants are constant, remove first-order terms by u=eαξ+βηv.
Worked: uxx​+2uxy​−3uyy​=0

Δ=4+12=16>0 (hyperbolic). dxdy​=22±4​=3, −1: ξ=y−3x, η=y+x, uξη​=0, so u=f(y−3x)+g(y+x). Check: f(y−3x)→9−6−3=0 ✓, g(y+x)→1+2−3=0 ✓.

Recipe I (separation for a string).

  1. Eigenfunctions from the boundary conditions. 2. ωn​=cλn​​. 3. An​ = coefficients of f; Bn​=ωn​1​× coefficients of g. 4. Simplify with parity: a product of sines may reduce by 2sinAcosB=sin(A+B)+sin(A−B) so that only a few modes appear.

7. Heat problems (Assignment 9)

Recipe J (the four steps)

  1. Homogenise. Find a simple w(x) (or w(x,t)) that satisfies the PDE and the non-zero boundary conditions: steady line for Dirichlet, quadratic for flux, parabola for a source.
  2. Eigenfunctions of the homogeneous boundary conditions for v=u−w.
  3. Coefficients from f−w (not f).
  4. Attach e−c2λn​t and add w back.
Worked: ends u(0,t)=0, u(L,t)=10, u(x,0)=0

w=10x/L. v(x,0)=−10x/L, bn​=−L2​⋅L10​⋅nπ(−1)n+1L2​=−nπ20(−1)n+1​. So

u=L10x​−π20​n≥1∑​n(−1)n+1​sinLnπx​e−c2n2π2t/L2.

Check: at t=0 the series is the sine series of 2Lπx​, giving u=0 ✓; at x=L every sine vanishes so u=10 ✓.

8. Laplace problems (Assignment 10)

Recipe K: rectangle

  1. List the four edges. The pair of opposite edges with zero data fixes the eigenfunctions (Dirichlet: sin; Neumann: cos).
  2. Across: sinh (or cosh) built to vanish (or have zero slope) on the opposite edge.
  3. One driven edge: one series. Two driven edges: add two one-edge problems.
  4. Coefficients: bn​=a2​∫0a​fsinanπx​dx, divided by sinh at the driven edge.
Worked: a=3, b=2, top edge u=sin3πx​+2sin32πx​, others 0

The data are already eigenfunctions: u=sin3πx​sinh32π​sinh3πy​​+2sin32πx​sinh34π​sinh32πy​​. At y=b=2: sinh3nπ⋅2​/sinh3nπ⋅2​=1 ✓; at y=0, x=0, x=3: 0 ✓; ∇2u=0 term by term ✓.

Recipe L: polar

  1. Disk: rn (drop r−n and lnr at the centre); exterior: r−n; annulus: both, and A+Blnr.
  2. Sector: Dirichlet on the rays gives sinαnπθ​ with r±nπ/α.
  3. Coefficients: full Fourier series on a circle; sine series on a sector.
  4. Checks: u(0)= mean of f; minf≤u≤maxf.
