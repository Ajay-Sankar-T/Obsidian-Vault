T6-Q9 — Op-Amp Circuit with a Single Source

EE1100op-amptutorial-solutionnodal-analysis

Hub: Tutorial 6 on OP-AMP · Previous: T6-Q8 - Photodiode Amplifier · Next: T6-Q10 - Difference Amplifier Condition

Fig. 9: find V0​ and i0​. A 3 V source drives a 2 kΩ resistor to the inverting node va​ and a 5 kΩ resistor to the non-inverting node vb​; 10 kΩ from vb​ to ground; 8 kΩ feedback; 4 kΩ load. [V0​=−2 V, i0​=−1 mA]

Tests: ideal op-amp rules with both inputs driven; KCL.

Given

Source 3 V; R=2 kΩ (va​), 5 kΩ (vb​), 10 kΩ (vb​ to ground), 8 kΩ (feedback), 4 kΩ (load).

Principle and why it applies

No current enters the inputs, so vb​ is a pure voltage divider, and negative feedback forces va​=vb​.

Solution

  1. Divider at the + input: vb​=3×5+1010​=2 V.
  2. Virtual short: va​=vb​=2 V.
  3. KCL at va​ (no input current): 2 k3−va​​=8 kva​−V0​​, i.e. 23−2​=82−V0​​ mA, so 2−V0​=4, V0​=−2 V​.
  4. Currents at the output: through the load, 4 kV0​​=−0.5 mA (downwards). Through the feedback resistor, 8 kva​−V0​​=84​=0.5 mA flows into the output node from va​. KCL at the output node: the op-amp output current i0​=4kV0​​−8kva​−V0​​=−0.5−0.5=−1 mA.

Result

V0​=−2 V,i0​=−1 mA

(i0​ is the current the op-amp output delivers: it sinks 1 mA, 0.5 mA to the load side and 0.5 mA through the feedback resistor.)

Check

Node va​ currents: +0.5 mA in from the source branch, 0.5 mA out through the 8 kΩ: balance ✓. The op-amp sinks current at its output: it is pulling the output negative, which matches V0​<0. The 4 kΩ load alone would take only −0.5 mA; the answer −1 mA in the sheet is the total output current.

Trap / alternative

Taking i0​ as the load current only (V0​/4 k =−0.5 mA). The arrow for i0​ in the figure is on the op-amp output lead, upstream of the feedback tap, so it includes the feedback current.
