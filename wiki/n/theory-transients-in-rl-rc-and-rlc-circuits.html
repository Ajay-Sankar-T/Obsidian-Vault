The idea in one paragraph

Resistors respond instantly; capacitors and inductors store energy and so remember. A capacitor remembers its voltage (21​Cv2), an inductor its current (21​Li2), and neither can change instantly while the other quantities can jump. A transient is the circuit's passage from one stored-energy state to another after a switch operates. For one storage element the passage is an exponential with time constant τ; for two it is an exponential, a critical form or a damped sinusoid depending on how much resistance there is.

Why the continuity rules. A jump in vC​ would need an infinite current (i=Cdv/dt) and an infinite power; a jump in iL​ would need an infinite voltage. So for a finite source, vC​(0+)=vC​(0−) and iL​(0+)=iL​(0−). Every other current or voltage in the circuit (the capacitor current, the inductor voltage, a resistor voltage) may jump at the switching instant and is found afterwards from the circuit equations.

Power and energy. p=vi. When p>0 the element absorbs energy (stores it); when p<0 it returns it. The energy in the element is the area under p(t) (E=∫pdt=21​Cv2 or 21​Li2). This is the content of Q1: plot v, i and p for a given waveform and see that the area under p is the stored energy.

2. DC steady state (Q2, Q3)

Long after any switching, all derivatives are zero: replace each capacitor by an open circuit and each inductor by a short circuit, solve the resistive circuit, then compute the stored energy 21​CvC2​+21​LiL2​. The capacitor voltage is whatever the resistive circuit puts across the open gap, the inductor current whatever flows through the short.

3. First-order circuits (one storage element)

Any circuit with one L or C and resistors is, as seen from the storage element, a Thevenin source Vth​ with resistance Rth​ driving it (theory of Theory - Circuit Laws and Network Theorems). Then:

RC:  Rth​CdtdvC​​+vC​=Vth​,RL:  Rth​L​dtdiL​​+iL​=Rth​Vth​​.

The solution of both is the universal first-order formula

x(t)=x∞​+[x(0+)−x∞​]e−t/τ,τRC​=Rth​C,τRL​=Rth​L​​

where x is any voltage or current in the circuit (not only the storage variable), x(0+) its value just after switching and x∞​ its final value. Five steps solve every switching problem:

  1. Before the switch (t=0−): DC steady state of the old circuit gives vC​(0−) or iL​(0−).
  2. Continuity: vC​(0+)=vC​(0−), iL​(0+)=iL​(0−).
  3. After the switch, t→∞: DC steady state of the new circuit gives x∞​.
  4. Time constant: Rth​ seen by the storage element in the new circuit (independent sources off).
  5. Assemble the formula; for another variable x, evaluate x(0+) from the new circuit with the storage element replaced by a source (vC​(0+) as a voltage source, iL​(0+) as a current source).

Facts worth knowing. After one τ the response has covered 63.2 % of the way to its final value; after 5τ it is within 0.7 %. For a source-free decay the energy is ∝e−2t/τ, so half of it is gone after t1/2​=2τ​ln2=0.347τ (Q4). The slope at t=0 is −(x(0+)−x∞​)/τ, so the tangent at 0 hits the final value at t=τ.

5. Reading a differential equation as a circuit (Q11)

The integro-differential equation adtdi​+bi+c∫idt=e is KVL for a series loop with L=a, R=b, 1/C=c and source e. The initial value of the integral term is the initial capacitor voltage vC​(0)=c∫−∞0​idt. A non-zero steady state exists only if the source is not blocked by the capacitor, i.e. for a DC source, the series current i∞​ is zero because the capacitor eventually charges to e.

6. Common traps

  • Using the initial value of a resistor voltage or capacitor current from before the switch: those jump; only vC​ and iL​ are continuous.
  • Computing τ with the old circuit's resistance, or leaving independent sources on when finding Rth​.
  • Forgetting that Rth​ for an inductor is in the denominator: τ=L/R (a bigger resistance makes an RL circuit faster).
  • Mixing up series vs parallel damping (series: α=R/2L; parallel: α=1/2RC).
  • Missing the amplitude factor 1/(ωd​L) (series, current) or 1/(ωd​C) (parallel, voltage) in the underdamped step response.

7. Cross-domain links

  • Conservation Laws and Flow Networks: the RC time constant is the same as a tank draining through a pipe, τ=Rhyd​Ctank​.
  • First-Order Systems - Time Constant and Response: the first-order formula of §3 is exactly the first-order system response.
  • Resonance and Buckling - Instability as an Eigenvalue Problem and 2nd Order ODEs - MOC: the damping cases of §4 are the damping cases of a mass–spring–damper (mx¨+cx˙+kx=0 with L↔m, R↔c, 1/C↔k).
