Plane Poiseuille Flow

conceptfluid-mechanicsviscous-flowexact-solutionpoiseuille

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Navier-Stokes Equations, Plane Couette Flow · formulas: Formula Bank §2

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: plane Poiseuille flow is the steady laminar flow between two fixed parallel plates driven by a constant pressure gradient; the velocity profile is a parabola and the flow rate grows as the cube of the gap.

Intuition: pressure pushes the fluid, the walls hold the edge layers still, and viscosity transmits the resulting shear. The fastest fluid sits mid-gap where the shear stress is zero; the shear grows linearly towards each wall, which forces a parabolic profile.

Governing Relations

Geometry: plates at y=0 and y=h, both fixed; flow in x; G=−dp/dx>0 constant.

Navier–Stokes reduction (steady, fully developed, v=w=0; gravity absorbed in p):  μdy2d2u​=dxdp​.

u(y)=2μG​y(h−y)​,umax​=8μGh2​ (at y=h/2)

uˉ=12μGh2​=32​umax​,q=uˉh=12μGh3​  (per unit width)

τ(y)=μdydu​=G(2h​−y),τw​=2Gh​=h6μuˉ​

Hydraulic resistance: Δp=Rh​Q with Rh​=wh312μL​ (width w): linear, like Ohm's law.

Where: G = driving pressure gradient (Pa/m); q = flow per unit width (m²/s); τw​ = wall shear stress.

Assumptions: laminar (Re=ρuˉh/μ<1400), steady, fully developed, plates wide compared with h (no side effects), Newtonian, constant dp/dx.

Visual

Figure: middle panel: the parabola with mean velocity 32​umax​. Compare the linear Couette profile (left) and the axisymmetric version (right).

When To Use

  • Microchannels, lab-on-chip, narrow gaps, lubrication films.
  • Flow in fractures and porous layers (the "cubic law" q∝h3).
  • Estimating pressure drop in slot-like passages, and as the 2-D model of duct flow.

Recognition pattern: "fluid between two stationary parallel plates", "pressure gradient drives", "thin slot" → plane Poiseuille flow.

Method

  1. Identify the plate gap h and the pressure gradient G=−dp/dx.
  2. Choose the ODE μu′′=−G with u(0)=u(h)=0.
  3. Apply: integrate twice to get u=2μG​y(h−y).
  4. Compute q=Gh3/12μ, uˉ, τw​=Gh/2.
  5. Check: laminar Re, and that the mean speed times area gives the same q.

Derivation / Why It Works

Force balance on a slab. Take a slab between y and y+dy of length dx. Net pressure force −dpdy must equal net shear force dτdx: dτ/dy=dp/dx=−G, so τ=G(h/2−y) using symmetry (τ=0 at the centre). Newton's law τ=μdu/dy then gives, with u(0)=0:

u=μG​(2hy​−2y2​)=2μG​y(h−y).

Flow rate. q=∫0h​udy=2μG​(2h3​−3h3​)=12μGh3​.

Dimensional check. The Π theorem predicts qμ/(h3G)= const (one group), and the solution fixes the constant at 1/12.

Common Traps

  • Trap: using umax​ instead of uˉ in the flow rate.
  • Avoid it by: q=32​umax​h.
  • Trap: halving the gap and expecting half the flow: q∝h3 gives an 8-fold drop.
  • Trap: using the pipe formula (πR4/8μ) for slot flow.
  • Trap: forgetting gravity for inclined or vertical channels (use the piezometric gradient d(p+ρgz)/dx).

Example

Given

Water (μ=10−3 Pa·s, ρ=998 kg/m³) in a gap of h=1 mm driven by dp/dx=−100 Pa/m.

Solution

umax​=8μGh2​=8×10−3100(10−6)​=0.0125 m/s,uˉ=12μGh2​=8.33×10−3 m/s,

q=uˉh=8.33×10−6 m2/s,τw​=2Gh​=0.050 Pa,Re=μρuˉh​=8.3.

Answer: peak 1.25 cm/s, mean 0.83 cm/s, q=8.3 mm²/s, wall shear 0.05 Pa, Re=8.3 (laminar). Key takeaway: doubling the gap multiplies the flow by 8 for the same pressure gradient.
