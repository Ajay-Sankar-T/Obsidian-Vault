Static Equilibrium (of a Fluid)

fluid-mechanicsfundamentals

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC

A fluid is in static equilibrium when every fluid particle is at rest (or moving with the same constant velocity as a rigid body) relative to the chosen reference frame — no relative motion between adjacent fluid layers.

Why this condition is special for a fluid

Because a fluid cannot sustain shear stress while at rest (see Fluids and Solids), static equilibrium forces the entire stress state down to a single scalar field, pressure p(x,y,z), with no shear component anywhere. This is the starting assumption for the whole of Fluid Statics: the only force a static fluid can exert on any surface (real or imaginary) is a normal (perpendicular) pressure force, dF=pdA, directed into the surface.

Equilibrium of a fluid element

For a fluid element at rest under gravity, Newton's second law with zero acceleration reduces to a balance between the pressure-gradient force and the weight:

−∇p−ρgk^=0

which, expanded along the vertical direction, is exactly the hydrostatic equation dP/dz=−ρg derived in Pressure and the Hydrostatic Equation. In the horizontal directions, ∂p/∂x=∂p/∂y=0 — pressure in a static fluid under gravity depends only on depth, never on horizontal position, which is why every point at the same depth in a connected body of the same fluid has the same pressure (the rule used constantly in Manometers).
