1. Energy equation with losses

Bernoulli plus a loss term between sections 1 and 2 of a steady incompressible pipe flow:

(ρp1​​+α1​2V12​​+gz1​)−(ρp2​​+α2​2V22​​+gz2​)=hlT​=hl​+hlm​

α≈1 (turbulent), α=2 (fully developed laminar). V is the mean velocity, Q=VA, Re=VD/ν. hlT​ is energy per unit mass (J/kg); divide by g for head in metres.

If the section velocities are equal (constant-diameter pipe, or the meter and tap in a house) the kinetic terms cancel and the pressure and elevation difference all goes into friction.

2. Major loss (Darcy–Weisbach)

hl​=fDL​2V2​⟹Δp=ρfDL​2V2​=π28ρfL​D5Q2​

The last form shows why diameter dominates: at fixed Q, Δp∝fLQ2/D5. A 20 % bigger bore cuts Δp to 40 %.

The Moody chart is a plot of Colebrook with the laminar line added. On the chart:

  • left of Re=2300 one line, slope −1 on the log–log axes;
  • at high Re each e/D curve flattens to its fully rough value, so f stops depending on speed and Δp∝V2 exactly;
  • the smooth-pipe curve keeps falling with Re.

Roughness e (mm): riveted steel 0.9–9, concrete 0.3–3, wood stave 0.2–0.9, cast iron 0.26, galvanized iron 0.15, asphalted cast iron 0.12, commercial steel 0.046, drawn tubing 0.0015, smooth plastic ≈ 0. Water: 15 °C: ρ=999, ν=1.14×10−6; 20 °C: ρ=998, ν=1.01×10−6 (SI).

5. The three pipe problems

Unknown Known Method
Δp (or hl​) Q (or V), D, L, e Re→f→hl​. Direct.
Q Δp, D, L, e Guess f (fully rough value), get V, then Re, then new f. Converges in 2–3 passes.
D Δp, Q, L, e Guess D, compute Δp, adjust (or solve with Haaland); then round up to a standard size.

6. Laminar tube flow

Fully developed laminar flow in a round tube (Re<2300): f=64/Re, kinetic-energy coefficient α=2, Q=128μLπD4Δp​. The largest laminar flow has Vmax​=2300ν/D.

Cross-domain links

  • Conservation Laws and Flow Networks — a pipe is a resistor: Δp plays the voltage and Q the current. Laminar flow (Δp∝Q, Ohm's law) is a linear resistor; rough turbulent flow (Δp∝Q2) is a nonlinear one, and two pipes in series add their Δp at the same Q, just as resistors do in Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws.
  • Dimensional Analysis and Similitude - Formula Bank — f is a function of Re and e/D because the pipe-flow problem has exactly those two Π groups besides Δp/ρV2.
