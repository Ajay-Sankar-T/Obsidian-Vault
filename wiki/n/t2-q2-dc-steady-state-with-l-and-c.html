T2-Q2: DC Steady State with an Inductor and a Capacitor

EE1100tutorial-solutionDC-circuitstransientssteady-state

Hub: Tutorial 2 - Transient Analysis of DC Circuits · Theory: Theory - Transients in RL, RC and RLC Circuits · Previous: T2-Q1 - Capacitor and Inductor Waveforms · Next: T2-Q3 - DC Steady State with a Current Source

Find Iin​, V1​ and V2​ under DC steady state with Vin​=15 V. If L=16 mH and the total energy stored in the circuit is 5 mJ in the steady state, find C. [Sheet: Iin​=0.5 A, V1​=10 V, V2​=5 V, C=60 μF]

Tests: DC steady state, stored energy.

Given

15 V source; 20 Ω in parallel with the capacitor C (voltage V1​); then the inductor L=16 mH in series with a 10 Ω resistor (voltage V2​ across the resistor).

Principle and why it applies

"DC steady state" means all currents and voltages are constant, so dvC​/dt=0 and diL​/dt=0: the capacitor carries no current (open circuit) and the inductor has no voltage (short circuit). The circuit collapses to resistors, and the stored energies follow from the values of vC​ and iL​.

Diagram

Solution

With C open and L shorted, the 20 Ω and 10 Ω are in series:

Iin​=20+1015​=0.5 A.

The capacitor sits in parallel with the 20 Ω: V1​=0.5×20=10 V. The inductor is a short, so V2​ is the whole drop in the 10 Ω: V2​=0.5×10=5 V.

Energy: the inductor carries IL​=Iin​=0.5 A, so

EL​=21​LIL2​=21​(16 mH)(0.5)2=2 mJ.

The capacitor must hold the remainder: EC​=5−2=3 mJ =21​CV12​, so

C=V12​2EC​​=1002×3×10−3​=60 μF.

Result

Iin​=0.5 A,V1​=10 V,V2​=5 V,C=60 μF​

Check

KVL: V1​+V2​=10+5=15 V =Vin​ ✓ (the inductor drops no DC voltage).

Trap / alternative

The capacitor value is not needed to find the three steady-state quantities. It enters only in the energy part, which is why the question gives the energy last. A common slip is to use the source voltage (15 V) for the capacitor energy; the capacitor sees only V1​.
