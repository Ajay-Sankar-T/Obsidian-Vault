Series Solutions About x0 not equal to 0

Related: Power Series Method · Airy's Equation (worked example at x0​=2)

In general, x0​=0 can be any ordinary point of y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0 (see Ordinary and Singular Points). The method is identical to the x0​=0 case, but the series is expanded in powers of (x−x0​) instead of x.

Steps

  1. Substitute t=x−x0​, so x=t+x0​ and t=0 corresponds to x=x0​.
  2. Rewrite P(x) and Q(x) as functions of t using x=t+x0​.
  3. Assume y=∑n=0∞​an​tn and substitute as before.
  4. Derive the recurrence relation in terms of an​.
  5. Convert back to x using t=x−x0​ at the end.

The radius of convergence is the distance from x0​ to the nearest singular point — see Radius of Convergence of Series Solutions.

Worked Example: y′′+xy′+y=0 about x0​=1

P(x)=x, Q(x)=1 are entire (analytic everywhere), so x0​=1 is ordinary (as is every real number). Substitute t=x−1, so x=t+1; the equation becomes

y′′+(t+1)y′+y=0.

With y=∑an​tn, y′=∑nan​tn−1, y′′=∑n(n−1)an​tn−2, expand (t+1)y′=ty′+y′ and match every power of t — this requires shifting three different series onto a common index k:

  • y′′: let n=k+2: ∑k=0∞​(k+2)(k+1)ak+2​tk
  • ty′: let n=k (no shift needed): ∑k=0∞​kak​tk
  • y′: let n=k+1: ∑k=0∞​(k+1)ak+1​tk
  • y: already ∑k=0∞​ak​tk

Adding all four series and setting the coefficient of tk to zero:

(k+2)(k+1)ak+2​+kak​+(k+1)ak+1​+ak​=0

Since kak​+ak​=(k+1)ak​:

(k+2)(k+1)ak+2​+(k+1)ak+1​+(k+1)ak​=0⟹ak+2​=−k+2ak+1​+ak​​, k≥0.

With a0​=1,a1​=0: a2​=−21​,a3​=61​,a4​=121​,…

y1​(t)=1−21​t2+61​t3+121​t4+⋯

With a0​=0,a1​=1: a2​=−21​,a3​=−61​,a4​=61​,…

y2​(t)=t−21​t2−61​t3+61​t4+⋯

General solution in t: y=C1​y1​(t)+C2​y2​(t). Substituting back t=x−1:

y=C1​[1−21​(x−1)2+61​(x−1)3+⋯]+C2​[(x−1)−21​(x−1)2−61​(x−1)3+⋯]

Since P(x),Q(x) have no singularities, this series converges for all x.

For a second worked example (Airy's equation at x0​=2, including the subtlety of a series that starts contributing only from k=1), see Airy's Equation.
