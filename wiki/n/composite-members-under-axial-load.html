The setup (Q4)

A composite block — e.g. a brass core surrounded by aluminium plates — loaded axially through a rigid end plate.

   P (total load)
       │
   ┌───▼───┐
   │ rigid │  ← end plate forces equal displacement
   └─┬───┬─┘
     │   │
  ┌──▼┐ ┌▼──┐
  │Al │ │Br │   both same length L
  └───┘ └───┘

Compatibility

Because the end plate is rigid, both materials are forced to the same displacement, and since they share the same length:

δb​=δAl​​⟹εb​=εAl​=ε​

But their stresses generally differ (different E): σb​=Eb​ε, σAl​=EAl​ε.

Equilibrium

P=Pb​+PAl​​=σb​Ab​+σAl​AAl​=ε(Eb​Ab​+EAl​AAl​)

Solving:

ε=Eb​Ab​+EAl​AAl​P​​⟹σb​=Eb​ε,σAl​=EAl​ε

Composite-member shortcut

Since both materials share the same strain:

σ1​:σ2​=E1​:E2​​

— the stiffer material always carries the proportionally larger stress; a direct generalization of Statically Indeterminate Axial Members's "composite/parallel members sharing displacement" case, now with the sum Eb​Ab​+EAl​AAl​ playing the role of an equivalent axial stiffness, exactly as keq​=k1​+k2​ for springs in parallel.

Cross-domain: this is impedance in parallel

Compare with Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits: two circuit elements forced to the same voltage (parallel connection) carry currents split by their individual admittances, Ii​=Yi​V, with total current I=V∑Yi​ — structurally identical to P=ε∑Ei​Ai​ here, with strain ε playing the role of the shared "potential" (voltage) and Ei​Ai​ playing the role of admittance Yi​. Both are instances of The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy's stiffness ↔ elastance pairing: equal-displacement (parallel spring) sharing is the mechanical mirror of equal-voltage (parallel admittance) sharing.
