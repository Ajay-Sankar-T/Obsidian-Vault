T7-Q4 — Referred Impedances and Short-Circuit Voltage

EE1100transformertutorial-solutionreferred-impedanceshort-circuit

Hub: Tutorial 7 on Transformer · Previous: T7-Q3 - Voltage and Frequency Other Than Rated · Next: T7-Q5 - Secondary Voltage Under Load

A 33 kVA, 2200/220 V, 50 Hz transformer: HV r1​=2.4 Ω, x1​=6 Ω; LV r2​=0.03 Ω, x2​=0.07 Ω. (a) r1​,x1​ referred to the secondary. (b) r2​,x2​ referred to the primary. (c) Equivalent resistance and reactance referred to (i) primary, (ii) secondary. (d) Total ohmic loss at full load. (e) The voltage to apply to the HV side for a short-circuit current of 160 A in the LV winding, and the power input. [(a) 0.024, 0.06 Ω (b) 3, 7 Ω (c) (i) 5.4, 13 Ω (ii) 0.054, 0.13 Ω (d) 1216 W (e) 225 V, 1382 W]

Tests: reflected impedance Z′=Z/a2 or a2Z, equivalent impedance, short-circuit test.

Given

a=N1​/N2​=2200/220=10.

Principle and why it applies

An impedance on one side appears on the other multiplied by the square of the turns ratio (impedance =V/I, voltage scales by a, current by 1/a). Referring both windings to one side puts them in series.

Solution

(a) To the LV side divide by a2=100: r1′​=1002.4​=0.024 Ω, x1′​=1006​=0.06 Ω. (b) To the HV side multiply by 100: r2′​=0.03×100=3 Ω, x2′​=0.07×100=7 Ω. (c) (i) Primary: Re1​=r1​+r2′​=5.4 Ω, Xe1​=x1​+x2′​=13 Ω. (ii) Secondary: Re2​=r2​+r1′​=0.054 Ω, Xe2​=0.13 Ω. (d) Full-load currents: I1​=220033000​=15 A, I2​=22033000​=150 A.

Pcu​=I12​Re1​=152(5.4)=1215 W(=1502×0.054=1215 W).

(e) Short-circuit impedance on the LV side: Ze2​=0.0542+0.132​=0.1408 Ω. LV voltage for 160 A: 160×0.1408=22.52 V, so on the HV side

Vsc​=10×22.52=225.2 V,Psc​=I2Re2​=1602(0.054)=1382 W.

Result

(a) 0.024 Ω, 0.06 Ω (b) 3 Ω, 7 Ω (c) (i) 5.4 Ω, 13 Ω (ii) 0.054 Ω, 0.13 Ω (d) 1215 W (e) 225 V, 1382 W.

Check

Re1​=a2Re2​: 100×0.054=5.4 ✓. The ohmic loss is the same whichever side it is computed on ✓. The sheet's 1216 W is rounding of the same 1215 W.

Trap / alternative

Using a instead of a2 for resistances, or dividing instead of multiplying when going the other way. A short-circuit "voltage" applied for the test is only about 10 % of rated (here 225/2200=10.2 %): the flux is low, so core loss is negligible and the input power is all copper loss.
