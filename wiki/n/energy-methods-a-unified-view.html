Same integral, three notations

Strain Energy and Castigliano's Theorem's table of U for axial/torsion/bending loading all share the structure

U=2×stiffness(stress resultant)2×length​

Compare directly with the electrical energy-storage terms implicit in The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy:

UL​=21​Li2,UC​=21​Cv2=2Cq2​

UC​=q2/(2C) is literally the same shape as U=N2L/(2AE) — a "flow-like" quantity squared, over twice a stiffness-like constant. This isn't coincidence: both come from integrating a linear constitutive law (σ=Eε or v=q/C) against its own conjugate variable, i.e. both are ∫(effort)d(displacement) for a linear spring-like element — see Stress-Strain Diagram and Hooke's Law's modulus-of-resilience definition u=σ2/2E, which is exactly UC​=q2/2C with σ↔q and E↔1/C (i.e. E plays the role of an elastance, not a capacitance — matching the stiffness/elastance pairing already found in the impedance-analogy note).

Castigliano's theorem is a small-signal AC sensitivity, generalized

Strain Energy and Castigliano's Theorem's δi​=∂U/∂Pi​ finds a displacement by differentiating stored energy with respect to a load. There's no electrical note in your vault that states the direct circuit analogue, but it exists and is worth having explicitly: for a linear reciprocal network, the same partial-derivative trick recovers a Thevenin-equivalent relationship — differentiating total stored energy U=21​LI2+21​CV2 with respect to a source parameter gives the sensitivity of that source's own terminal variable to itself, which is exactly how self- and mutual-inductance/capacitance are defined in energy terms in more advanced treatments (energy method for computing L and M in magnetically coupled circuits — directly relevant to Week 8's magnetic coupling, which currently states M and k without deriving them from energy).

The genuinely new theorem you can now state

Castigliano's theorem for maximum power transfer. Thevenin, Norton and Maximum Power Transfer (AC) derives ZL​=ZTh∗​ by an impedance argument. It can equally be derived as a Castigliano-style stationarity condition: treat average power delivered to the load as a function P(RL​,XL​) of the two load parameters, and set ∂P/∂RL​=0, ∂P/∂XL​=0 simultaneously. This is exactly the same "set the partial derivative of the energy-like functional to zero" move as Castigliano's first theorem, ∂U/∂R=0, used in Strain Energy and Castigliano's Theorem to solve statically indeterminate structures. In both cases: a physical extremum (max power delivered; zero displacement at a redundant support) is found by differentiating a scalar energy/power functional with respect to a free parameter and setting the result to zero — the calculus-of-variations principle underlying both fields, usually taught as two unrelated "tricks" but actually one idea (a discrete instance of the principle of stationary/least action / Castigliano's theorems being a special case of the more general variational principles that also produce Maxwell's reciprocity and Rayleigh-Ritz).
