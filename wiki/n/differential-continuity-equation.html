Differential Continuity Equation

conceptfluid-mechanicskinematicscontinuity

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Integral Continuity, Material Derivative · formulas: Formula Bank §2

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: at every point in a flow, the rate at which density rises equals minus the divergence of the mass flux: ∂ρ/∂t+∇⋅(ρV)=0; for incompressible flow this reduces to ∇⋅V=0.

Intuition: mass is neither created nor destroyed. If more fluid flows out of a tiny box than flows in, the density inside must drop. In incompressible flow the density cannot change, so what goes in must come out through the other faces: velocity has zero divergence.

Governing Relation

∂t∂ρ​+∂x∂(ρu)​+∂y∂(ρv)​+∂z∂(ρw)​=0​⟺∂t∂ρ​+∇⋅(ρV)=0

Following a particle:

DtDρ​+ρ∇⋅V=0⟹∇⋅V=−ρ1​DtDρ​=V1​DtDV​  (dilatation)

Case Condition
steady ∇⋅(ρV)=0
incompressible (steady or unsteady) ∇⋅V=ux​+vy​+wz​=0​
plane incompressible ux​+vy​=0, so a stream function exists: u=ψy​, v=−ψx​
cylindrical, incompressible r1​∂r∂(rvr​)​+r1​∂θ∂vθ​​+∂z∂vz​​=0

Where: (u,v,w) = velocity components; ρ = density.

Assumptions: a continuum with no sources or sinks inside the element. It holds for viscous or inviscid, steady or unsteady flow.

Visual

Draw an element dxdydz with mass entering on the left face and leaving on the right face: net outflow in x is ∂(ρu)/∂xdxdydz. The sum of the three directional net outflows equals the rate of decrease of the mass inside, −∂ρ/∂tdxdydz. (The motion figures in Motion of a Fluid Element show the same box deforming with constant area when ∇⋅V=0.)

When To Use

  • Check that a proposed velocity field is physically possible.
  • Find a missing component: given u (and w), integrate to get v.
  • Introduce the stream function in 2-D incompressible flow.
  • Decide the incompressibility condition from ∇⋅V≈0.

Recognition pattern: "is this flow incompressible?", "find v given u", "satisfies conservation of mass" → ∇⋅V=0.

Method

  1. Identify whether the flow is compressible (then keep ρ) or incompressible (Ma<0.3, liquids).
  2. Choose the coordinate system (Cartesian or cylindrical) to match the geometry.
  3. Apply the divergence; to find a missing component, integrate: ∂v/∂y=−∂u/∂x gives v=−∫(∂u/∂x)dy+f(x,t).
  4. Check: fix the "constant" f(x,t) by a boundary condition (usually v=0 at a wall); confirm ∇⋅V=0 after substitution.

Derivation / Why It Works

Mass flows through the six faces of a fixed element dxdydz. Inflow minus outflow in x: −∂x∂(ρu)​dxdydz, and similarly for y,z. This equals the accumulation ∂t∂ρ​dxdydz. Equivalently, apply the divergence theorem to the integral form in Conservation of Mass: ∮ρV⋅ndA=∫∇⋅(ρV)dV; since the control volume is arbitrary, the integrand must vanish.

Common Traps

  • Trap: using the incompressible form when the density varies in time or space.
  • Avoid it by: checking Ma; use the full equation for gases above Ma≈0.3.
  • Trap: forgetting the function of integration f(x,t) when integrating continuity.
  • Trap: confusing "steady" with "incompressible": a steady compressible flow has ∇⋅(ρV)=0, not ∇⋅V=0.
  • Trap: applying the Cartesian divergence to polar components (the factors 1/r matter).

Example

Given

A 2-D incompressible flow with u=x2−y2 (check later: is this a possible flow?) and v=0 at the wall y=0. Find v. Also test the radial flow vr​=Q/(2πrh), vθ​=0.

Solution

∂y∂v​=−∂x∂u​=−2x ⇒ v=−2xy+f(x).

v(x,0)=0⇒f(x)=0, hence v=−2xy​. Check: ux​+vy​=2x−2x=0 ✓.

Radial flow: r1​drd(rvr​)​=r1​drd​(2πhQ​)=0 ✓ (a line source of strength Q/h).

Answer: v=−2xy; the radial source flow conserves mass for r>0. Key takeaway: continuity alone fixes one component once the others are known, up to a boundary-condition function.
