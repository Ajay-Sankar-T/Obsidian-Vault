The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy (Impedance Analogy)

synthesiscross-domainimpedanceanalogy

← up: Cross-Domain Synthesis MOC

The dictionary

Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits defines ZR​=R, ZL​=jωL, ZC​=−j/(ωC). A structural spring-mass-damper system has the literal same equation if you substitute:

Electrical (force-voltage analogy) Mechanical Where it lives in your vault
voltage v force F —
current i velocity x˙ —
resistance R damping coefficient c dissipative, in-phase with velocity
inductance L mass m opposes change in velocity, stores kinetic energy 21​Li2↔21​mv2
1/C (elastance) spring stiffness k stores potential energy 21​Cv2↔21​kx2
charge q=∫idt displacement x=∫x˙dt —

This isn't a loose metaphor — it's the same substitution that makes The Universal Second-Order Linear System's ODE identical across domains: Lq¨​+Rq˙​+q/C=v(t) is term-for-term mx¨+cx˙+kx=F(t).

Why this matters for your Strength of Materials notes specifically

Strain Energy and Castigliano's Theorem gives the spring stiffness analogy U=21​kδ2=21​Pδ for an axially loaded member, with k=AE/L (already noted in Axial Deformation's "spring analogy" section). Under the dictionary above, k=AE/L is the mechanical stiffness that corresponds to electrical elastance 1/C — meaning an axially loaded bar behaves, in the impedance sense, exactly like a capacitor: it stores energy proportional to (displacement)², releases it fully on unloading (elastic), and its "impedance" to a time-varying force would be 1/(jωCmech​) where Cmech​=L/(AE) — a mechanical compliance, literally the reciprocal of stiffness, playing the role of capacitance.

Impedance triangle ↔ mechanical impedance

Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits's Z=R+j(XL​−XC​) has a direct mechanical-impedance twin (force/velocity, in the frequency domain):

Zmech​(ω)=c+j(ωm−ωk​)

Both vanish their reactive part at the same kind of condition: electrical resonance ω0​=1/LC​ corresponds exactly to mechanical resonance ωn​=k/m​ — the frequency at which the mass's inertial reactance (ωm) exactly cancels the spring's compliance reactance (k/ω), just as XL​=XC​ cancels in Week 8's resonance condition.

A consequence you probably didn't have before

Your Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy note already builds a "master substitution" mindset (τ↔γ/2) for relating stress and strain transformation. The impedance analogy is the same habit of mind one level up: instead of relating two tensor quantities within mechanics, it relates an entire mechanics problem to an entire circuit problem. Concretely, this means every AC network theorem in Tutorial 3 has a structural-vibration reading:

  • AC maximum power transfer's ZL​=ZTh∗​ becomes: a vibration absorber/damper attached to a structure extracts maximum energy from it when the absorber's mechanical impedance is the complex conjugate of the structure's — the founding idea behind tuned mass dampers, which are literally designed by conjugate-impedance matching in the mechanical domain.
  • Power factor correction (cancel a lagging reactance with a capacitor) becomes, mechanically, adding a compliant element to cancel excess inertial reactance in a vibrating machine mount — the same Im(Z)=0 condition, achieved by adding "mechanical capacitance" (a softer spring) instead of an electrical one.
