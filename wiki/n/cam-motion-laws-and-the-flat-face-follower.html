Cam Motion Laws and the Flat-Face Follower

conceptKDoM
In one breath

A cam turns a prescribed lift curve s(θ) into a surface. For a flat-face translating follower the surface is fixed completely by s(θ) and the base-circle radius R0​; the offset only decides how far the driving force sits from the follower axis, not the shape of the cam.

Part of KDoMs - MOC. Solved in Tutorial-9-solution.

1. Choose the motion law first

Law s over a rise h in angle β, u=φ/β Ends Peak speed Peak acceleration
Uniform hu speed jumps (infinite a) hω/β impact
Simple harmonic 2h​(1−cosπu) a jumps 1.57hω/β 4.93hω2/β2
Cycloidal h(u−2πsin2πu​) v=0 and a=0 2hω/β 2πhω2/β2=6.28hω2/β2

Cycloidal pays a higher peak acceleration to get a=0 at both ends, so no acceleration step (finite jerk only) where the follower leaves a dwell.

Derivatives with respect to cam angle (what the geometry needs):

s=h(u−2πsin2πu​),s′=βh​(1−cos2πu),s′′=β22πh​sin2πu

Time derivatives follow with ω constant: s˙=s′ω, s¨=s′′ω2. The return is the mirror image, s=h−srise​.

2. Geometry of the flat face

Take the follower moving along y, cam turning anticlockwise at ω, face perpendicular to y.

  • Face position. The face plane is at distance p(θ)=R0​+s(θ) from the cam centre O. At a dwell on the base circle the face is tangent to it.
  • Contact point. Cam and follower have an instantaneous centre on the line through O perpendicular to the follower's motion, and on the common normal. So the contact point lies at lateral distance

xc​=ωs˙​=s′(θ)

from the line through O parallel to the follower axis (to the right during the rise for anticlockwise rotation).

  • Radius of curvature. The profile is the envelope of the face lines, so

ρ=R0​+s+s′′

It must stay positive everywhere: a flat face cannot follow a concave cam.

  • Eccentricity of the driving effort. The cam pushes along y at the contact point; the guide holds the follower on its axis at offset e. The lever arm is

ε=s′−e(axis on the rise side of O, e>0)

A large ε makes the follower jam in its guide. Driving effort exists only on the rise; on the return the spring pushes.

  • Face width must cover every contact position s′−e, so it grows with ∣s′∣max​ and with e if the face is symmetric about the axis.
Why the offset exists

Offset moves the axis towards the contact point on the rise, cutting ε=s′−e from smax′​ to smax′​−e. The price is a wider face and a larger ε on the return. It never changes the cam outline.

3. Design order

graph TD
    A["Lift h, angles, speed"] --> B["Motion law s(θ)"]
    B --> C["v, a limits → h, β, ω"]
    B --> D["Pick R0 so ρ > 0 everywhere"]
    D --> E["Profile: x = s' cos θ + (R0+s) sin θ"]
    E --> F["Offset e from ε = s' − e limit"]
    F --> G["Face width from |s' − e|"]

Profile of the cam in cam-fixed axes (θ measured from the start, cam anticlockwise):

x=s′cosθ+(R0​+s)sinθ,y=−s′sinθ+(R0​+s)cosθ

4. Traps

Common slips
  • Using the offset to compute ρ. It does not enter: ρ=R0​+s+s′′.
  • Forgetting that s′′ reduces ρ on the second half of the rise. The profile is sharpest there (here ρmin​≈9 mm).
  • Using β in degrees inside hω/β. Use radians.
  • Measuring "into the rise" from θ=0 when a dwell comes first.
Predict first: which is bigger, peak acceleration with cycloidal or SHM, for the same h,β,ω?

Cycloidal: 6.28 against 4.93 in units of hω2/β2, about 27 % more. It buys a=0 at the ends, so there is no acceleration jump entering or leaving a dwell.

Quick check: the follower is flat-faced and s′=0 at some angle. Where is the contact point?

On the line through the cam centre parallel to the follower axis, because xc​=s′=0. That is the case through every dwell. It moves farthest out where the speed peaks, mid-rise, at smax′​=2h/β.

5. Connections

  • Plane Kinematics of Rigid Bodies — the instantaneous centre that gives xc​=s˙/ω.
  • Rotating Vectors - Phasors, Velocity Polygons and Complex Kinematics — s˙=ωxc​ is the same v=ω×r that drives every velocity polygon.
  • Vibration of Particles and Rigid Bodies — a follower with a spring is a forced oscillator; high peak acceleration and sharp acceleration changes excite it, which is why cycloidal is chosen at speed.
