Plane Stress Transformation

Related: Shear Stress Formula for Beams · Generalized Hooke's Law and Poisson's Ratio · Mohr's Circle for Stress · Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy

Why Transformation Is Needed

The stress components σx​,σy​,τxy​ at a point depend on the orientation of the coordinate axes chosen to describe them — they are not invariant. The same physical stress state, viewed on a plane rotated by angle θ, has different components σx′​,σy′​,τx′y′​. Since failure (yielding, fracture) is governed by the actual stress on the "weakest" plane, not by whatever orientation the analyst happened to draw axes at, engineers need a systematic way to find the stress on any plane through a point — in particular, the plane of maximum normal stress (principal stress) and the plane of maximum shear stress.

Derivation via Wedge Equilibrium

Consider a small triangular wedge cut from the stressed element, with one face at angle θ to the x-axis. Equilibrium of forces on the wedge (resolving σx​,σy​,τxy​ acting on the two orthogonal faces into components along and perpendicular to the inclined face) gives:

σx′​=2σx​+σy​​+2σx​−σy​​cos2θ+τxy​sin2θ

τx′y′​=−2σx​−σy​​sin2θ+τxy​cos2θ

σy′​=2σx​+σy​​−2σx​−σy​​cos2θ−τxy​sin2θ

(obtained from σx′​ by replacing θ→θ+90°). Note the invariant: σx′​+σy′​=σx​+σy​ — the sum of normal stresses on any two perpendicular planes is constant (this is the trace of the stress tensor, invariant under rotation).

Principal Stresses

The orientation θp​ that makes τx′y′​=0 (no shear on that plane) defines the principal planes:

tan2θp​=σx​−σy​2τxy​​

The two roots of this equation (90° apart) give the principal stresses, found by maximizing/minimizing σx′​:

σ1,2​=2σx​+σy​​±(2σx​−σy​​)2+τxy2​​

By convention σ1​≥σ2​. On principal planes, shear stress is exactly zero — this is why principal stresses are the natural quantities to compare against material strength.

Maximum In-Plane Shear Stress

Occurs on planes at 45° to the principal planes:

τmax​=(2σx​−σy​​)2+τxy2​​=2σ1​−σ2​​

with an associated normal stress equal to the average 2σx​+σy​​ (i.e., the max-shear planes are generally not shear-free in the normal direction).

Eigenvalue Interpretation

Writing the plane-stress state as the symmetric matrix

[σ]=(σx​τxy​​τxy​σy​​),

the principal stresses are exactly the eigenvalues of [σ] (roots of det([σ]−λI)=0), and the principal directions are the corresponding eigenvectors. Rotating the reference frame corresponds to the similarity transform [σ]′=Q[σ]QT with rotation matrix Q=(cosθ−sinθ​sinθcosθ​) — this is the linear-algebra structure underlying every transformation formula above. See Stress-Strain Transformation Analogy for the parallel matrix/eigenvalue treatment of strain, which uses exactly the same machinery.
