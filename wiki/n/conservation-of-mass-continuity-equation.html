Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)

fluid-mechanicscontrol-volumecontinuity

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Reynolds Transport Theorem

From RTT to mass conservation

Set B=m, b=1 in Reynolds Transport Theorem. Since the mass of a system is, by definition, conserved, dmsystem​/dt=0, so:

∂t∂​∫CV​ρdV+∮CS​ρ(V⋅n)dA=0​

Accumulation+Net outflow=0​i.e.Accumulation=Inflow−Outflow​

Mass flow rate

m˙=∫A​ρ(V⋅n)dA​uniform flow: m˙=ρAV​[kg/s]

Steady flow continuity

For steady flow the accumulation term vanishes:

∑m˙in​=∑m˙out​​

One inlet, one outlet: ρ1​A1​V1​=ρ2​A2​V2​. For incompressible flow (ρ1​=ρ2​):

A1​V1​=A2​V2​​Memory: smaller area⇒higher velocity

Differential (pointwise) form

∂t∂ρ​+∇⋅(ρV)=0​incompressible: ∇⋅V=0​

Cartesian, 3D: ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​+∂z∂w​=0; 2D: ∂x∂u​+∂y∂v​=0.

Cross-domain: continuity is KCL, is inductor current continuity, is Castigliano compatibility

m˙in​=m˙out​ (steady, no accumulation) is the direct fluid-mechanics analogue of ∑Iin​=∑Iout​ (Nodal Analysis's KCL) — both are the steady-state special case of Reynolds Transport Theorem's "accumulation = 0." More surprisingly, the unsteady accumulation term ∂(ρVCV​)/∂t=0 (e.g. a tank filling up) is mathematically the same statement as capacitor voltage continuity vC​(0+)=vC​(0−): both say a conserved "stored" quantity (fluid mass in a tank; charge on a capacitor) cannot jump instantaneously — only changes at a finite rate set by the net flow through the boundary. (Earlier this paragraph paired the tank with inductor current continuity; the inductor's twin is the fluid's inertia, see Conservation Laws and Flow Networks.) A1​V1​=A2​V2​'s "smaller area, higher velocity" is, in the impedance analogy of The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy, the geometric version of a series circuit forcing the same current through every element regardless of each element's own "resistance" to it.
