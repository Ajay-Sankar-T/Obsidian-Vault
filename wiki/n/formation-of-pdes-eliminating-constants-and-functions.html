Formation of PDEs – Eliminating Constants and Functions

What it is

A family of surfaces can be described by a PDE: eliminate the family's arbitrary constants or arbitrary functions by differentiating. This is the reverse of solving, and it shows what the general solution of a PDE looks like. (Compare ODEs: y=cx2 eliminates c to give xy′=2y.)

Eliminating arbitrary constants

For f(x,y,u,a,b)=0 differentiate with respect to x and y (chain rule through u):

fx​+fu​p=0,fy​+fu​q=0,

then eliminate a,b among the three equations. With k constants and n=2 independent variables:

k vs n Result
k=n one first-order PDE (usually nonlinear)
k>n need second derivatives: a PDE of order 2 or more
k<n several first-order PDEs possible
Family PDE
u=(x2+a)(y2+b) pq=4xyu
u=ax+by+ab u=px+qy+pq (Clairaut)
(x−a)2+(y−b)2+u2=1 u2(p2+q2+1)=1
u=(x−a)2+(y−b)2 p2+q2=4u
spheres with centre on the u-axis yp−xq=0 (linear, though the family is not)

Eliminating an arbitrary function

Let φ=φ(x,y,u), ψ=ψ(x,y,u) be given and G(φ,ψ)=0 with G arbitrary. Differentiating,

Gφ​(φx​+φu​p)+Gψ​(ψx​+ψu​p)=0,Gφ​(φy​+φu​q)+Gψ​(ψy​+ψu​q)=0.

This is a homogeneous linear system in (Gφ​,Gψ​), so the determinant must vanish. The pq terms cancel, and the result is quasi-linear:

∂(y,u)∂(φ,ψ)​p+∂(u,x)∂(φ,ψ)​q=∂(x,y)∂(φ,ψ)​.

Eliminating one arbitrary function always gives a quasi-linear first-order PDE. Lagrange's method (Lagrange's Method for Quasi-linear PDEs) reverses exactly this step.

Relation PDE
u=f(x2+y2) yp−xq=0 (surfaces of revolution)
u=xnf(y/x) xp+yq=nu (Euler's relation, homogeneous of degree n)
u=emxf(x+y) p−q=mu
G(xy+u2,x+y+u)=0 (2u−x)p+(y−2u)q=x−y
xyu=f(x+y+u) x(y−u)p+y(u−x)q=u(x−y)
G(x+y+u,x2+y2−u2)=0 (y+u)p−(x+u)q=x−y
u=f(x−ct) ut​+cux​=0 (transport)
u=f(x+ct)+g(x−ct) utt​=c2uxx​ (wave, two functions, second order)
u=f(x+iy)+g(x−iy) uxx​+uyy​=0 (Laplace)

Rule of thumb: k arbitrary functions lead to a PDE of order k.

Method

  1. Decide what is arbitrary (constants or a function) and count it against the number of independent variables.
  2. Differentiate with respect to each independent variable, using the chain rule for arguments of f (a common slip is f′(x+y) with a missing factor).
  3. Eliminate: for functions, divide the two equations to remove f′ (or use the determinant).
  4. Simplify and check the class of the result.

Example (u=xnf(y/x)). p=nxn−1f−xn−2yf′, q=xn−1f′. Then xp=nxnf−xn−1yf′=nu−yq, so xp+yq=nu. ✓

Trap

u=f(x+y)g(x−y) has two arbitrary functions: expect a second-order PDE, not first order.

Cross-domain links

  • Cauchy-Euler Equations — xp+yq=nu is the PDE version of the Euler equation: homogeneous functions are the eigenfunctions of the Euler operator x∂x​+y∂y​ with eigenvalue n.
  • Exact Differential Equations — the first integrals of a characteristic system play the role of the potential F with Fx​=M, Fy​=N.
