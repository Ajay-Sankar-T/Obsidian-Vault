Strain Energy and Castigliano's Theorem

Strain Energy Density

Work done by a gradually applied force, stored as recoverable elastic energy, per unit volume:

u=∫0ε​σdε=linear elastic21​σε=2Eσ2​

This is the area under the stress-strain curve up to the current state — see modulus of resilience in Stress-Strain Diagram and Hooke's Law. For shear: u=21​τγ=τ2/(2G).

Total Strain Energy for Common Members

Integrating u over the volume of the member gives the total strain energy U, expressed directly in terms of the internal stress resultant:

Loading Strain energy U
Axial (constant N) 2AEN2L​
Axial (varying N(x)) ∫0L​2AEN(x)2​dx
Torsion (constant T) 2GJT2L​
Torsion (varying) ∫0L​2GJT(x)2​dx
Bending ∫0L​2EIM(x)2​dx

Each has the same structure: U=2×stiffness(stress resultant)2×length​ — the energy analogue of the "force × deformation / 2" formula for a linear spring, U=21​kδ2=21​Pδ.

Castigliano's Second Theorem

For a linear-elastic structure with strain energy U expressed as a function of the applied loads P1​,P2​,…, the displacement in the direction of Pi​ (at its point of application) is

δi​=∂Pi​∂U​

This is extremely powerful for finding a single deflection/rotation without solving for the full deflection curve (contrast with Double Integration and Macaulay's Method):

  1. Express internal N(x),T(x), or M(x) in terms of the actual loads, including a variable P at the point/direction where the deflection is sought (introduce a fictitious "dummy load" Q there if no real load acts, then set Q=0 after differentiating).
  2. Write U as an integral/sum in terms of P (and other fixed loads).
  3. Differentiate under the integral sign: δ=∂P∂U​=∫EIM​∂P∂M​dx (bending case; analogous for axial/torsion terms, and combinable by superposition when several loading types act together).
  4. Set P (or the dummy load Q) to its actual value.

For a rotation (slope) at a point, use a dummy moment M0​ in place of a dummy force, and θ=∂U/∂M0​.

Why This Matters for Statically Indeterminate Structures

Castigliano's first theorem (a companion result) provides that for a statically indeterminate structure, the redundant reaction R can be found from the condition that the displacement at the redundant support is zero:

∂R∂U​=0

This gives an alternative, often more systematic route to the same compatibility equations used in Statically Indeterminate Axial Members and indeterminate beam problems, especially useful when there are multiple redundants.

Connection to Buckling

The energy stored during buckling (Euler Buckling of Columns) can also be analyzed via energy methods (Rayleigh-Ritz), where an assumed deflected shape is used to estimate Pcr​ by equating the work done by P to the bending strain energy stored in the assumed curvature — a preview of the finite-element / variational approach to structural mechanics.

Cross-domain: strain energy and circuit energy storage (½LI², ½CV²) are the same integral · mechanical stiffness ↔ electrical elastance 1/C

Check yourself

Q1. Write the strain energy of an axially loaded bar.

U=P2L/(2AE), which is 21​Pδ.

Q2. State Castigliano's theorem.

The deflection at a load, in the direction of that load, is ∂U/∂P (the partial derivative of the total strain energy with respect to that load).

Q3. Use it for a cantilever with end load P.

U=∫M2/(2EI)dx=P2L3/(6EI), so δ=∂U/∂P=PL3/(3EI).
