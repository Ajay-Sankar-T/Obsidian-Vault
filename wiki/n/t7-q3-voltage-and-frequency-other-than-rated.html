T7-Q3 — Transformer on a Supply Other Than Rated

EE1100transformertutorial-solutionfluxsaturation

Hub: Tutorial 7 on Transformer · Previous: T7-Q2 - Exciting Current and Primary Current · Next: T7-Q4 - Referred Impedances and Short-Circuit Voltage

A single-phase transformer is designed for 240/120 V, 50 Hz. Find the no-load secondary voltage and frequency when the HV side is connected to (a) 240 V, 40 Hz; (b) 120 V, 25 Hz; (c) 120 V, 50 Hz; (d) 480 V, 50 Hz; (e) 240 V dc. [(a) 120 V, 40 Hz (b) 60 V, 25 Hz (c) 60 V, 50 Hz (d) overrated, 240 V, 50 Hz (e) may burn]

Tests: E=4.44fNΦm​, flux ∝V/f, turns ratio, saturation.

Given

Rated 240/120 V at 50 Hz: ratio 2:1, rated flux Φrated​∝240/50=4.8.

Principle and why it applies

The secondary voltage is the turns ratio times the primary voltage and has the supply frequency: V2​=V1​/2, f2​=f1​. But the core flux is Φm​=4.44fN1​V1​​∝fV1​​: it must not exceed the rated flux or the core saturates (magnetising current rises sharply, the iron overheats, the waveform distorts).

Solution

Case V1​, f V2​ (ideal) f2​ V/f vs 4.8 Verdict
(a) 240 V, 40 Hz 120 V 40 Hz 6.0 (125 %) over-fluxed by 25 %: likely saturation, high exciting current
(b) 120 V, 25 Hz 60 V 25 Hz 4.8 (100 %) normal flux, safe; lower voltage and frequency
(c) 120 V, 50 Hz 60 V 50 Hz 2.4 (50 %) under-excited, safe, half voltage
(d) 480 V, 50 Hz (240 V) 50 Hz 9.6 (200 %) overrated: deep saturation, winding insulation over-stressed
(e) 240 V dc 0 none no changing flux, no induced emf: only V/R limits the current, so the winding burns

Result

(a) 120 V, 40 Hz (but overflux). (b) 60 V, 25 Hz. (c) 60 V, 50 Hz. (d) nominally 240 V, 50 Hz but the transformer is overrated and saturates. (e) 0 V; transformer may burn.

Check

Frequency never changes across an ideal transformer. In (e), a DC source gives dΦ/dt=0, so v2​=0 and the primary current is 240/R1​, tens or hundreds of times rated.

Trap / alternative

Concluding that (a) is fine because the output is 120 V. The flux is what matters: lowering frequency at the same voltage raises Φm​ in proportion. This is why a transformer for 60 Hz can usually run at 50 Hz only at 5/6 of its voltage (the same V/f).
