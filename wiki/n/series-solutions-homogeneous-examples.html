Example 1: y′′−y=0

Assume y=∑an​xn; substituting y′′=∑(n+2)(n+1)an+2​xn into y′′−y=0 and equating coefficients:

(n+2)(n+1)an+2​−an​=0⟹an+2​=(n+2)(n+1)an​​.

Even coefficients: a2k​=(2k)!a0​​. Odd coefficients: a2k+1​=(2k+1)!a1​​.

y=a0​k=0∑∞​(2k)!x2k​+a1​k=0∑∞​(2k+1)!x2k+1​=a0​coshx+a1​sinhx

Converges for all x (no singular points); radius of convergence R=∞ for both series (R=lim∣an​/an+1​∣).

Example 2: y′′+y=0

Same setup gives an+2​=−(n+2)(n+1)an​​, so a2k​=(2k)!(−1)ka0​​, a2k+1​=(2k+1)!(−1)ka1​​.

y=a0​cosx+a1​sinx

Converges for all x∈R — matching the known Existence, Uniqueness, and Fundamental Sets example for y′′+y=0 via a completely different method.

Example 3: (1+x)y′′+y′−y=0 around x=0

Since 1+0=1=0, x=0 is ordinary. With y′=∑(n+1)an+1​xn, y′′=∑(n+2)(n+1)an+2​xn, substituting into y′′+xy′′+y′−y=0 gives coefficient of xn:

(n+2)(n+1)an+2​+(n+1)2an+1​−an​=0⟹an+2​=(n+2)(n+1)an​−(n+1)2an+1​​.

a0​,a1​ arbitrary; e.g. a2​=2a0​−a1​​, a3​=63a1​−2a0​​, a4​=12a2​−9a3​​.

y=a0​+a1​x+2a0​−a1​​x2+63a1​−2a0​​x3+a4​x4+⋯

Two independent solutions come from (a0​,a1​)=(1,0) and (0,1).

With xy′=∑n=1∞​nan​xn, substitution gives (n+2)(n+1)an+2​+(n+1)an​=0⟹an+2​=−n+2an​​.

a2k​=2kk!(−1)ka0​​,a2k+1​=1⋅3⋅5⋯(2k+1)(−1)ka1​​
