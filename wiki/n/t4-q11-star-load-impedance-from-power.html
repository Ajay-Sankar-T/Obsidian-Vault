T4-Q11: Star-Load Impedance from Total Power and Line Current

EE1100tutorial-solutionthree-phasestar-connectionpower

Hub: Tutorial 4 - Resonance, Filter and Three-Phase Circuits · Theory: Theory - Three-Phase Circuits · Previous: T4-Q10 - Delta Load Power · Next: T4-Q12 - Star Load Voltage and Current

A three-phase 208 V generator supplies a total of 1800 W at a line current of 10 A when three identical impedances are connected in star across its line terminals. Find the resistive and reactive components of each phase impedance. [Sheet: 6 Ω and ±10.39 Ω]

Tests: star connection, P=3​VL​IL​cosϕ, recovering R and X from P and ∣Z∣.

Given

VL​=208 V, IL​=10 A, Ptotal​=1800 W; star load, so Ip​=IL​=10 A and Vp​=VL​/3​.

Principle and why it applies

Total real power is dissipated only in the three phase resistances, so P=3Ip2​R gives R directly; the magnitude ∣Z∣=Vp​/Ip​ then fixes X through X2=∣Z∣2−R2. The sign of X cannot be told from power alone, hence the ±.

Diagram

Per-phase equivalent: Vp​=120.1 V across Z=R±jX, current 10 A.

Solution

Vp​=3​208​=120.1 V,∣Z∣=Ip​Vp​​=10120.1​=12.01 Ω.

R=3Ip2​P​=3×1001800​=6 Ω,X=∣Z∣2−R2​=144.2−36​=10.40 Ω (sheet 10.39).

Equivalently from the power factor: cosϕ=3​VL​IL​P​=3602.21800​=0.4997≈0.5, ϕ=60∘, R=∣Z∣cosϕ=6, X=∣Z∣sinϕ=10.39.

Result

Zp​=6±j10.39 Ω(∣Zp​∣=12 Ω, ϕ=±60∘)​

+j if the load is inductive (lagging current), −j if capacitive (leading current): the power data cannot distinguish them.

Check

P=3Ip2​R=3×100×6=1800 W ✓ and S=3​VL​IL​=3602 VA, Q=Ssin60∘=3120 VAr =3Ip2​X=3×100×10.4 ✓.

Trap / alternative

  • Using VL​ (208 V) as the phase voltage gives ∣Z∣=20.8 Ω, which is the delta value; in a star load Vp​=VL​/3​.
  • Assuming the load is inductive is a guess; the question only supports "reactive component of ±10.39 Ω".
  • If the same three impedances were connected in delta, each would carry VL​=208 V and the line current would be three times as large.

Concepts used
