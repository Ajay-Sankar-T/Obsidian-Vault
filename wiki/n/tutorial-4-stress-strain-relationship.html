Seven questions (Q1–Q7), plus an extra two-material plate problem, that together are almost entirely applications of the Generalized Hooke's Law matrix — the theory itself doesn't change from problem to problem; only which stresses/strains are known, zero, or coupled by a constraint changes. This tutorial is best read as a catalogue of the ways that matrix equation gets specialized.

The golden rule that resolves half the ambiguity in this tutorial

Restricted deformation⇒ε=0​Free direction⇒σ=0​

These are not interchangeable, and confusing them is the single most common error in this tutorial: a restrained direction generally still develops nonzero stress (the restraint is exactly what causes the stress, via Poisson coupling to the other directions), while a free direction genuinely carries zero stress but may still deform (via Poisson effect from stress in the other directions).

Master problem-solving algorithm

  1. Identify axes x,y,z and draw the loading.
  2. Identify what is known — e.g. an applied pressure σz​=−p, or an applied load P.
  3. Classify each remaining direction: free (σ=0) or restrained (ε=0) — per the golden rule above.
  4. Write Hooke's law for each direction: εi​=E1​[σi​−ν(σj​+σk​)].
  5. Apply equilibrium (∑F=0) where multiple members/parts share a load.
  6. Apply compatibility (δ1​=δ2​ or ε1​=ε2​) where parts are rigidly connected.
  7. Solve the resulting system for the unknown stresses/strains.
  8. Check signs: positive stress = tension, negative = compression; positive strain = expansion, negative = contraction.
