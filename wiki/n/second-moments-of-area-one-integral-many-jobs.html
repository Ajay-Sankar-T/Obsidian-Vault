Second Moments of Area — One Integral, Many Jobs

synthesiscross-domainsecond-momentstensor

← up: Cross-Domain Synthesis MOC

I=∫y2dA

shows up wherever something grows linearly with distance and is then multiplied by that same distance as a lever arm. One factor of y comes from the physics (strain, pressure, tilt volume, speed), the other from the moment arm. Second Moments of Area and Flexure Formula already point at each other; this note collects every appearance.

Where Result Why the square
Beam bending σ=−My/I,  ρ1​=EIM​ strain ε=−y/ρ is linear in y; moment arm is y
Torsion τ=Tρ/J,  ϕ=GJTL​,  J=∫ρ2dA shear strain linear in ρ; arm is ρ

Rotating machines 21​Jω2,  T=Jα, J=∫r2dm speed ∝r; momentum arm r (reflected through gears in Gears, Transformers and Reflected Impedance)

The "stiffness × deformation = load" family AE, GJ, EI (Torsion notes this) is the same pattern; only the geometric integral changes: A, J, I.

Facts that connect them

  • Perpendicular-axis theorem: J=Ix​+Iy​ for a plane section. Check: a circle has I=πR4/4 and J=πR4/2, both in the vault's tables. So J=2I for a circle: GJ is twice GI, the torsional analogue of EI.
  • It is a rank-2 tensor. Ix​,Iy​,Ixy​ transform with cos2θ,sin2θ, so Mohr's circle applies unchanged to second moments, with principal axes as the eigen-directions. This is the missing case flagged in Eigenvalues, Rotation and Diagonalization Everywhere ("moment of inertia principal axes"); it is why Unsymmetric Bending needs principal axes. Rule: angle at 2× means rank 2.
  • Buckling picks the weakest axis. The column fails about the axis with the smallest principal I, so section choice (tubes over solid bars: π(ro4​−ri4​)/4) is about pushing area outward, where the square pays.
  • Parallel-axis theorem I=IG​+Ad2 is the same in all rows: floating bodies, composite beams, centre of pressure from a free surface.

Common traps

  • Cubing the wrong dimension: in bh3/12 the dimension perpendicular to the bending/tilt axis is cubed, in beams and in waterplanes alike.
  • Using J for non-circular shafts (the formula holds only for circular sections).
  • Taking the mass moment J=∫r2dm for the area moment ∫y2dA: different units, same structure.
