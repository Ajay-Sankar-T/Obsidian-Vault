AC Transients

EE1100AC-circuitstransients

← up: MOC · bridges: Tutorial 2 (DC transients) and Tutorial 3 (AC steady state)

The EE1100 syllabus lists DC transients (RL, RC, RLC, parallel RLC — Tutorial 2) and single-phase AC/phasors/impedance/resonance (Tutorial 3) as separate units, without a dedicated "AC transient" topic. This folder is the natural bridge: what happens when a switch operates on a circuit that has an AC source, combining the time-constant/damping machinery of Tutorial 2 with the phasor/impedance machinery of Tutorial 3.

The big idea

Complete response=Transient response+Steady-state response​

or

x(t)=xforced​(t)+xnatural​(t)

For a DC source (Tutorial 2), xforced​ is a constant. For an AC source, xforced​ is itself a sinusoid — the phasor-domain steady-state solution:

x(t)=xss,AC​(t)+xtransient​(t)

As t→∞, the transient decays to zero and x(t)→xss,AC​(t) — the pure AC steady-state answer that Tutorial 3's phasor methods compute directly.

Why phasors alone are not enough

Phasor analysis (Phasors and Complex Algebra) gives only the steady-state part xss,AC​(t). It says nothing about what happens in the first few time constants after a switch operates — that information lives entirely in the natural (transient) term, found the same way as in Tutorial 2: set independent sources to zero and analyze the resulting RC/RL/RLC decay.

Two sub-topics

  • First-Order AC Transients (RC and RL) — single storage element, exponential transient added to a sinusoidal steady state.
  • Second-Order AC Transients (Series and Parallel RLC) — both L and C present, transient is itself a decaying sinusoid at a different frequency from the source.

Universal workflow

  1. Initial conditions. Find vC​(0−), iL​(0−) from the pre-switch circuit (steady state — DC or AC as applicable), then apply continuity: vC​(0+)=vC​(0−), iL​(0+)=iL​(0−).
  2. AC steady-state solution. Solve the post-switch circuit by phasors exactly as in Tutorial 3 — ZR​=R, ZL​=jωL, ZC​=1/(jωC) — to get xss​(t).
  3. Natural response. Deactivate independent sources (as in Thevenin reduction) and find τ (first order) or α,ωn​,ωd​ (second order) exactly as in Tutorial 2.
  4. Complete response. Add the forms — see the two sub-notes for the exact templates.
  5. Apply initial conditions from step 1 to solve for the unknown constants.
  6. Sanity check: the exponential/decaying part must vanish as t→∞, leaving exactly the steady-state sinusoid from step 2.

Two switching rules that never change

vC​(0+)=vC​(0−)​iL​(0+)=iL​(0−)​

C → voltage stays, L → current stays. These continuity rules hold regardless of whether the source is DC or AC — they follow purely from iC​=CdvC​/dt and vL​=LdiL​/dt being finite (an instantaneous jump would need infinite current/voltage). By contrast, iC​ and vL​ themselves can jump discontinuously at switching.

A trap worth flagging explicitly

At t→∞ you cannot use the DC shortcuts "capacitor → open, inductor → short" for an AC-driven circuit — those apply only to true DC steady state. For AC steady state you must use the actual impedances ZL​=jωL, ZC​=1/(jωC), exactly as Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits defines them.
