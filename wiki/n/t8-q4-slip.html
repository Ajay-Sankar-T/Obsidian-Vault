T8-Q4: Slip and Its Typical Value

EE1100tutorial-solutionmachinesinduction-machineslip

Hub: Tutorial 8 - Induction Machines · Theory: Theory - Three-Phase Induction Machines, Induction Motor - Slip, Rotor Circuit and Power Flow · Previous: T8-Q3 - Synchronous Speed · Next: T8-Q5 - Torque-Slip Characteristic

Define the slip s of an induction machine. What is its typical value for an induction motor running in steady state?

Tests: definition and meaning of slip, its range of values, the rotor frequency f2​=sf1​.

Principle and why it applies

The rotor runs slower than the revolving field. The slip measures the fractional speed lag of the rotor behind the field:

s=ns​ns​−nr​​=ωs​ωs​−ωr​​​,nr​=(1−s)ns​.

It also measures the relative motion that induces the rotor emf: the field sweeps past the rotor at ns​−nr​=sns​, so the induced emf and its frequency are proportional to s:

E2s​=sE20​,f2​=sf1​.

Solution

Values of slip:

Condition nr​ s
standstill (instant of starting) 0 1
full-load, steady-state motoring slightly below ns​ about 0.02–0.06 (2–6 %)
no load almost ns​ below 0.01
synchronous speed (ideal, no torque) ns​ 0
above synchronous (generator) >ns​ <0
reverse (braking, "plugging") opposite to field >1

Typical steady-state value: s≈2–6 % (about 1 % or less in very large machines, up to 5–8 % in small ones). Example: a 4-pole, 50 Hz motor with s=4 % runs at 1440 rpm and the rotor frequency is 2 Hz.

Result

s=ns​ns​−nr​​;steady-state motoring: s≈0.02–0.06.

Check

A small slip means the motor is nearly a constant-speed machine (speed varies by only a few % from no load to full load), and the rotor current frequency sf1​≈1–3 Hz is low, so the rotor core loss is negligible. Also, from the power split Pcu2​=sPg​, s=4 % means 4 % of the air-gap power is lost as rotor copper.

Trap / alternative

  • Slip is a fraction of the synchronous speed, not of the rotor speed.
  • Slip can be quoted as a fraction or in per cent (0.04=4 %); don't put the percentage into f2​=sf1​ without converting.
  • "Low slip" means high efficiency in the rotor stage; a high-slip motor (large resistance, e.g. a crane motor) wastes more as rotor copper.
