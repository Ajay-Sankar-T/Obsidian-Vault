Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle

fluid-mechanicsfluid-staticsbuoyancy

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Hydrostatic Force on Curved Surfaces

Archimedes' principle

FB​=ρf​gVD​​

ρf​ — fluid density, VD​ — volume of fluid displaced by the submerged (or partially submerged) body.

Buoyant force=weight of displaced fluid​

This is exactly the vertical-force result from Hydrostatic Force on Curved Surfaces applied to a closed curved surface (the whole body): summing the vertical pressure-force components over every point on a closed surface leaves only the net difference between the (larger) upward pressure on the bottom and the (smaller) downward pressure on the top — precisely the weight of the fluid that would otherwise occupy that volume. The buoyant force acts through the centre of buoyancy B, the centroid of the displaced volume (not necessarily the centroid of the body itself, if the body has non-uniform density).

Submerged body — stability of orientation

Condition Behaviour
B above G stable
G above B unstable
G=B neutral

And for whether it sinks, rises, or hovers: ρbody​>ρf​⇒ sinks; ρbody​<ρf​⇒ rises; ρbody​=ρf​⇒ neutral buoyancy.

Floating body — equilibrium condition

W=FB​​⟹W=ρf​gVD​

If the body's own (average) density is ρB​: ρB​gV=ρf​gVD​, so

VVD​​=ρf​ρB​​​

the fraction of the body submerged — this is why an iceberg (density ≈0.92ρwater​) floats with about 92% of its volume underwater.
