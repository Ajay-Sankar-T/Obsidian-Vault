Moving and Accelerating Control Volumes

conceptfluid-mechanicscontrol-volumemomentum

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Reynolds Transport Theorem, Linear Momentum Equation · see also: Angular Momentum Principle

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: when the control volume itself moves, fluid crosses its surface at the relative velocity W=V−VCV​, and if it accelerates, a fictitious force −∫aCV​dm must be added to the momentum balance.

Intuition: the equations in Linear Momentum Equation assume a CV fixed in an inertial frame. A cart, a rocket or a moving vane is not. Either work in the frame of the CV and use relative velocities, or keep the inertial frame and correct the mass flux.

Governing Relations

Velocity relation:  V=W+VCV​ (absolute = relative + CV velocity).

Mass (any moving or deforming CV):

∂t∂​∫CV​ρdV+∮CS​ρ(W⋅n)dA=0

Case 1: CV moving at constant velocity (inertial). Newton's law is unchanged; only the flux uses W:

∑F=∂t∂​∫CV​WρdV+∮CS​Wρ(W⋅n)dA​

(equivalently, work entirely with relative velocities; the constant offset VCV​ drops out because of mass conservation).

Case 2: CV with rectilinear acceleration aCV​ (non-inertial):

∑F−∫CV​aCV​ρdV=∂t∂​∫CV​WρdV+∮CS​Wρ(W⋅n)dA​

∑F contains only real forces (pressure, weight, supports). The term −∫aCV​ρdV is the inertial (d'Alembert) force on the mass inside.

Where: W = fluid velocity measured relative to the CV; VCV​, aCV​ = velocity and acceleration of the CV in an inertial frame.

Assumptions: rigid CV translating without rotation (rotation adds Coriolis and centrifugal terms).

Visual

Sketch a jet of speed V striking a vane that moves away at U. In the vane's frame the jet arrives at V−U. Draw the CV on the vane, label Win​=V−U and Wout​ along the vane exit, and check the mass flow entering the CV is ρA(V−U) for a single vane.

When To Use

  • A vane, plate or cart moving under a jet; a boat or aircraft with a steady flow past it in its own frame.
  • Rockets and anything whose mass changes while accelerating.
  • A tank on wheels that is pushed by its own outflow.

Recognition pattern: "the vane moves at speed U", "rocket accelerates", "mounted on a cart" → relative velocity, possibly with an inertial term.

Method

  1. Identify whether the CV moves at constant velocity (inertial) or accelerates.
  2. Choose axes attached to the CV; write every velocity as W=V−VCV​.
  3. Apply mass conservation with W to find the relative mass flow at each port.
  4. Apply the momentum equation (add −∫aCV​ρdV if accelerating) using W in the flux.
  5. Check: a single moving vane receives ρA(V−U), a train of vanes receives the full ρAV; signs of the inertial term.

Derivation / Why It Works

The RTT holds in any frame provided the velocity in the flux term is the velocity relative to the control surface, because that is what carries a property across it. For a constant-velocity CV, Newton's law is frame-invariant (Galilean invariance), so the force balance is the same. For an accelerating CV, apply Newton's law in the CV's frame by adding the force −maCV​ to each fluid element: summing over the CV mass gives the extra integral.

Common Traps

  • Trap: using the jet's mass flow ρAV for a single receding plate.
  • Why: the plate runs away from the jet, so only the part ρA(V−U) arrives per second.
  • Avoid it by: counting the mass that actually crosses the CV in its own frame.
  • Trap: using absolute velocity in the flux term of an accelerating CV.
  • Trap: forgetting the inertial term for an accelerating CV, or giving it the wrong sign.
  • Trap: applying the formula to a rotating CV without Coriolis terms.

Example

Given

A water jet of speed V=20 m/s and area A=1×10−3 m² (ρ=998 kg/m³) strikes a flat plate normal to the jet, which moves away at U=8 m/s. Also a rocket of mass 500 kg burning 10 kg/s with exhaust speed 2000 m/s relative to the rocket.

Solution

Plate: relative speed W=V−U=12 m/s, mass flow on the plate ρAW. The relative momentum is destroyed:

F=ρA(V−U)2=998×10−3×122=143.7 N.

For a fixed plate F=ρAV2=399.2 N. For a series of plates (a wheel) that eventually intercepts the entire flow, F=ρAV(V−U)=239.5 N.

Rocket: thrust =m˙Ve​=10×2000=20 kN; initial acceleration, ignoring drag:

a=Mm˙Ve​​−g=50020000​−9.81=30.2 m/s2.

Answer: 143.7 N on the single moving plate (399 N fixed, 240 N for a train); 30.2 m/s² for the rocket. Key takeaway: the same jet pushes harder on a series of vanes than on one running vane because the mass flow entering the control volume is larger.
