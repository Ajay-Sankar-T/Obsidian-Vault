2. Fundamental law of gearing

Law

The common normal at the point of contact must cut the line of centres at a fixed point P (the pitch point). Then the angular-velocity ratio is constant.

ω3​ω2​​=O2​PO3​P​=const(Fig. 9.3, instantaneous centre 23=P)

Relation Formula Eq.
Average speeds (ω1​)av​=2πn/N1​, (ω2​)av​=2πn/N2​
Proper action ω2​ω1​​=−N1​N2​​=const
Rolling pitch circles ω2​ω1​​=rp1​rp2​​ (9.4)
Belt (base circle) analogue ω2​ω1​​=rb1​rb2​​=rp1​rp2​​ (9.5)
Involute advantage ratio unchanged if C changes; pitch radii change, rp1​/rp2​=rb1​/rb2​, rp1​+rp2​=C

3. Involute geometry

String unwrapped length ρ from base circle rb​:

ρ=rb​(ψ+δ)=rb​tanα(9.6),ψ+δ=tanα(9.7)

x=rb​[cosψ+(ψ+δ)sinψ] (9.8),y=rb​[sinψ−(ψ+δ)cosψ] (9.9)

ψ=tanα−α−δ=inv(α)−δ,invα=tanα−α​(9.10)

(Here δ is the angular offset of the involute's start. With δ=0 the point Q lies at polar angle θ=invα from the start of the curve, as drawn in the figure; the tangent point T is at tanα.)

Radius at pressure angle αr​ r=cosαr​rb​​
Base pitch pb​=N2πrb​​=pcosα
Involute exists only for x2+y2≥rb2​ (none inside the base circle; radial below it)
Common normal tangent to both base circles = line of action

4. Contact ratio

Two gears (length of action EF=AF+BE−AB):

EF=ro12​−rb12​​+ro22​−rb22​​−Csinα(9.11)

mc​=pb​EF​=2πrb2​/N2​ro12​−rb12​​+ro22​−rb22​​−Csinα​(9.12)

Pinion and rack (rack addendum ar​):

EF=sinαar​​+ro12​−rb12​​−rb1​tanα(9.13)

mc​=2πrb1​N1​​[sinαar​​+ro12​−rb12​​−rb1​tanα](9.14)

Rule of thumb

mc​=1.3 means alternately one and two tooth pairs in contact (time average 1.3). Aim for mc​≳1.4. Raising the addendum raises mc​ but can cause interference.

5. Interference and undercutting

Condition to avoid interference: both addendum circles must cut the common tangent AB between the tangent points A and B. With equal addenda, the pinion interferes first.

a=O2​P[1+λ(λ+2)sin2α​−1],λ=ω1​ω2​​=N2​N1​​(9.15)

Case Minimum pinion teeth Eq.
Pinion with wheel, a=fm N1,min​=1+λ(λ+2)sin2α​−12fλ​ (9.17)
Pinion with rack N1,min​=sin2α2f​ (9.18)
Equal gears (λ=1) 3N2sin2α−4fN−4f2=0 Prob. 9.13

Rack, f=1: 14.5° → 31.9, 20° → 17.1, 25° → 11.2.

Remedies for small N

Undercut (weakens tooth), larger α, shorter addendum, or involute-cycloidal profiles. Round N1,min​ up, then take N2​=N1​/λ and check the speed ratio (Prob. 9.3).

6. Conjugate profile synthesis

Analytic method — gears with pitch radii rp1​,rp2​, C=rp1​+rp2​, tooth y1​=f1​(x1​); θ1​,θ2​ are the inclinations of the line of centres in each frame.

Step Formula Eq.
Given tooth, gear 1 y1​=f1​(x1​) (9.19)
Wanted tooth, gear 2 y2​=f2​(x2​) (9.20)
Rolling rp1​θ1​=rp2​θ2​
Angle between frames θ1​+θ2​=γθ1​,γ=1+rp2​rp1​​ (9.21)
Transformation x2​=(x1​−Ccosθ1​)cosγθ1​+(y1​−Csinθ1​)sinγθ1​ (9.22)
y2​=−(x1​−Ccosθ1​)sinγθ1​+(y1​−Csinθ1​)cosγθ1​ (9.23)
Normal PN through P=(rp1​cosθ1​, rp1​sinθ1​) y1​−rp1​sinθ1​=−f1′​(x1​)1​(x1​−rp1​cosθ1​) (9.24)
Parametric form x1​=x1​(θ1​), y1​=y1​(θ1​) → x2​=x2​(θ1​), y2​=y2​(θ1​) (9.25, 9.26)

Solve (9.19)+(9.24) for the contact point N, then map with (9.22), (9.23). The locus of N in a fixed frame is the line of action.

Constant pressure angle (constant bearing reaction): Fc​cosα and Fc​sinα components, torque T1​=Fc​cosαrp1​, profile slope

y1′​=tan(θ1​+α)(9.27),y1​=rp1​sinθ1​−tan(θ1​+α)x1​−rp1​cosθ1​​(9.28)

Linear ODE for the contact point, and its solution (constant of integration (tanα−α)rp1​cosα):

dθ1​dx1​​−x1​cot(θ1​+α)=−sin(θ1​+α)rp1​cosαcos2(θ1​+α)​(9.29)

x1​=rp1​cosα[(θ1​+tanα)sin(θ1​+α)+cos(θ1​+α)](9.30)

y1​=rp1​cosα[sin(θ1​+α)−(θ1​+tanα)cos(θ1​+α)](9.31)

This is an involute with rb1​=rp1​cosα (9.32), ψ=θ1​+α (9.33), δ=tanα−α=invα; it cuts the pitch circle on the x1​-axis (θ1​=0⇒x1​=rp1​, y1​=0). With θ2​=rp1​θ1​/rp2​, the mating tooth is

x2​=−rp2​cosα[(−θ2​+tanα)sin(−θ2​+α)+cos(−θ2​+α)](9.34)

y2​=rp2​cosα[(−θ2​+tanα)cos(−θ2​+α)−sin(−θ2​+α)](9.35)†

† Sign in (9.35)

The book prints +sin(−θ2​+α). Pushing (9.30)–(9.31) through the book's own transformation (9.22)–(9.23) gives −sin (checked numerically, 7 values of θ1​; (9.34) matches exactly). Use the minus sign above. See 14. Notes on the book's equations.

Worked pattern (Prob. 9.5: trapezoidal tooth, 12→24 teeth, rp1​=12.5 cm)

Flank y1​=4.96−0.268x1​, line of action y1​−12.5sinθ1​=3.73(x1​−12.5cosθ1​), intersection x1​=1.24+11.65cosθ1​−3.125sinθ1​, y1​=4.628−3.13cosθ1​+0.837sinθ1​, then (9.22), (9.23) with γ=1.5. Plotted in

Graphical method: tangent at Q1​ → normal through Q1​ meets the arc of radius O1​P about O1​ → rotate back to bring that point to P → carry PQ round gear 2 using rp1​θ1​=rp2​θ2​ (arc PQ1′​ = arc PQ12​).

Mating helices have opposite hands; helical gears give axial thrust (cancelled by double-helical / herringbone).

8. Spiral (crossed-helical) gears

Hands Shaft angle θ Eq.
same θ=α+β (9.40)
opposite θ=α−β (9.41)

α,β = helix angles. Point contact only → light loads. Centre distance = shortest distance between the shaft axes. Prob. 9.6: θ=15∘, one gear right-hand 25∘ → the other is left-hand, 10°.

9. Bevel and worm gears

Bevel (intersecting shafts): pitch surfaces are cones; pitch diameter specified at the large end.

θ=γ1​+γ2​(γi​=pitch angles, θ=shaft angle)

γ=90∘ → crown (face) gear (analogue of a rack). Spiral-bevel = helical teeth; hypoid = offset shafts (hyperboloids).

Worm and wheel

Quantity Formula Eq.
Lead l=px​N1​ (9.42)
Speed ratio ω2​ω1​​=N1​N2​​ (9.43)
Shafts at 90° px​=p (wheel circular pitch); lead angle of worm = helix angle of wheel
Shafts at 90° ω2​ω1​​=lπDp2​​ (9.44)

px​ = axial pitch of worm, N1​ = threads on worm, N2​ = teeth on wheel. Line contact → higher loads than spiral gears; large reduction.

10. Gear trains

Simple train (one gear per shaft) – idlers drop out of the ratio:

nB​nA​​=NA​NB​​,  nC​nB​​=NB​NC​​,  nD​nC​​=NC​ND​​ ⇒ nD​nA​​=NA​ND​​(9.45, 9.46)

Parallel axes: external mesh reverses sense, so include signs, nB​nA​​=−NA​NB​​, …, nD​nA​​=−NA​ND​​ (9.45a, 9.46a); bevel trains use axis vectors (right-hand screw).

Compound train (nB​=nC​ on one shaft):

nD​nA​​=NA​NB​​⋅NC​ND​​  (with signs: (−1)k)=train value(9.47)

Internal gearing: both gears turn the same way. Sign rule: (−1)#external meshes.

11. Epicyclic trains

Degrees of freedom (simple planetary: 4 links, j=3 hinges, h=1 higher pair)

F=3(n−1)−2j−h=9−6−1=2

→ two inputs needed (or one input → two outputs). If one gear is fixed: sun (fixed gear), planet, arm = planet carrier.

11.1 Algebraic (Willis) method

nA​−narm​nB​−narm​​=train value from A to B​(9.48)

For one external mesh the train value is −NA​/NB​; for a chain use the product of ratios with (−1)k. (The book prints (9.48) in magnitude form and says to judge the sign from the sense of rotation.)

11.2 Tabular method

Step Arm Gear 1 Gear 2 …
(i) all gears locked to arm, give arm x turns x x x
(ii) arm fixed, give gear 1 y turns 0 y y× (train ratio, with sign)
(iii) resultant x x+y x+y×(…)

Two inputs → two equations → x, y → all speeds. CCW positive. If tooth counts are missing assume equal pitch: rC​=rH​+rK​+rL​⇒NC​=NH​+NK​+NL​ (Prob. 9.9).

11.3 Differential (automobile rear axle)

nB​=2nD​+nE​​(ring gear speed = arithmetic mean of the two wheel speeds)

Straight running: planets act as keys, nB​=nD​=nE​.

12. Torques in epicyclic trains

Constant speeds (no acceleration):

M1​+M2​+M3​=0(9.49),M1​ω1​+M2​ω2​+M3​ω3​=0(9.50)  (no friction)

Sun gear held: ω3​=0 (9.51) → M3​ = holding torque; M2​=−M1​ω2​ω1​​, M3​=−(M1​+M2​). Input torque from power: M1​=2πn1​Power×60​.

14. Notes on the book's equations

Things to keep in mind
  1. (9.35) — printed with +sin; the transformation (9.22)–(9.23) gives −sin (see 6. Conjugate profile synthesis). (9.34) is fine.
  2. (9.48) — the sign of the train value is not printed; use (−1)k for k external meshes, as the worked problems do.
  3. (9.45)–(9.47) — unsigned forms hold for magnitudes only; parallel axes need (9.45a)/(9.46a).
  4. Prob. 9.10 — 7.5×103×60/(2π×1000)=71.62 N·m; the book rounds to 71.66, so M2​, M3​ differ by ≲ 0.1 %.
  5. Problem 9.3 rounds N1,min​ up (22.94 → 23). Always round up; a smaller N interferes.
  6. The text layer of the PDF is duplicated and garbles some fractions; equations here were reconstructed and then re-derived or checked numerically where possible.
