Vibration of Particles and Rigid Bodies

conceptengineering-mechanicsAM1100dynamicsvibration

The big idea

Dynamics ends where Physics I's oscillators begin: any system with a restoring force or torque proportional to displacement vibrates with q¨​+ωn2​q=0. The recipe for ωn​ is always "stiffness over inertia", ωn2​=keff​/meff​. Whether it is a block on a spring, a flywheel on a shaft or a rod pendulum, you find the effective stiffness and inertia in the coordinate you chose. This note maps the course's vibration chapter onto the full treatment already in the vault.

Energy method (Rayleigh): for a conservative system in simple harmonic motion, the maximum kinetic energy equals the maximum potential energy:

Tmax​=Vmax​ ⟹ ωn2​=Tmax​-coefficientVmax​-coefficient​.

Write T=21​meff​q˙​2 and V=21​keff​q2 for the chosen coordinate q; then ωn2​=keff​/meff​. This avoids free-body diagrams for complicated systems and is how a rolling cylinder on a spring or a pulley system is solved in one line.

flowchart TD
    A["choose one coordinate q"] --> B["kinetic energy T = 1/2 m_eff q-dot^2"]
    A --> C["potential energy V = 1/2 k_eff q^2 (small q)"]
    B --> D["omega_n = sqrt(k_eff / m_eff)"]
    C --> D
    D --> E["f = omega_n / 2 pi,  period = 2 pi / omega_n"]
🎯 Predict first: a spring of stiffness k is cut in half. By what factor does the natural frequency of the same mass change?

Each half is twice as stiff (khalf​=2k), so ωn​ rises by 2​ (to 1.41×). Stiffness is inversely proportional to length for a spring, which is also why springs in series are softer and in parallel stiffer.

Worked examples

🔬 Flywheel on a torsion rod: m=2 kg, R=0.1 m disk on a shaft with kt​=2 N·m/rad

I=21​mR2=0.01 kg·m². ωn​=kt​/I​=2/0.01​=14.1 rad/s, f=2.25 Hz, period 0.44 s. Check: quadrupling the inertia (a heavier disk) halves ωn​ ✓. For a real shaft kt​=GJ/L: a longer, thinner shaft is softer.

🔬 Energy method for a solid cylinder rolling without slipping on a spring (k=200 N/m, m=4 kg)

v=Rθ˙; T=21​mv2+21​Iω2=21​⋅23​mx˙2 so meff​=23​m=6 kg. V=21​kx2. ωn​=200/6​=5.77 rad/s (compare 7.07 rad/s for a block sliding without rotation: rolling is slower because rotation adds inertia).

⚠️ Using the static equilibrium position as origin and forgetting gravity

If gravity acts on a hanging spring system, measure the displacement from the static equilibrium: then the constant gravity term drops out and ωn​=k/m​ is unchanged (Simple Harmonic Motion).

💥 Mixing a translational k with a rotational I

ωn​=k/I​ is wrong dimensionally. Use a consistent pair: (k,m) for x or (kt​,I) for θ; or refer the spring to θ via kt​=kr2.

Quick quiz

Q1. A 2 kg mass hangs from a spring and stretches it 5 cm. Natural frequency?

k=mg/δ=392 N/m; ωn​=g/δ​=14 rad/s, f=2.2 Hz (independent of m).

Q2. Why does a loaded truck vibrate more slowly than an empty one on the same springs?

ωn​=k/m​: more mass, same k lowers the frequency. Loaded trucks also have a lower ζ if the dampers stay the same.
