T4-Q2: Parallel RLC Resonance

EE1100tutorial-solutionresonanceRLC-circuitparallel-RLC

Hub: Tutorial 4 - Resonance, Filter and Three-Phase Circuits · Theory: Theory - Resonance and Passive Filters · Previous: T4-Q1 - Series RLC Resonance · Next: T4-Q3 - Series RLC from Q and Bandwidth

A current source drives R=10 Ω, L=1 H, C=1 μF in parallel. (a) Resonant frequency. (b) Quality factor. (c) Bandwidth. (d) Lower and upper half-power frequencies. (e) For i(t)=1sin1000t the maximum voltage across the capacitor. (f) The capacitor current. [Sheet: 1000 rad/s; 0.01; 105 rad/s; 10 and 100010 rad/s; 10 V; 0.00707∠90∘ A]

Tests: parallel resonance, low-Q behaviour, the dual of Q1.

Given

R=10 Ω, L=1 H, C=1 μF; source current amplitude 1 A at ω=1000 rad/s.

Principle and why it applies

Parallel resonance is the dual of series resonance: Y=R1​+j(ωC−ωL1​) is smallest in magnitude when the susceptances cancel, so the voltage across the whole parallel block, V=I/∣Y∣, is largest at ω0​ and equals IR.

Solution

(a) ω0​=LC​1​=10−6​1​=1000 rad/s.

(b) Q=ω0​LR​=1000×110​=0.01 (also RC/L​=10×10−3): a very low Q; the 10 Ω resistor loads the tank heavily.

(c) BW=Qω0​​=RC1​=10×10−61​=105 rad/s.

(d) Exact: ω1,2​=ω0​[1+4Q21​​∓2Q1​]=1000[2501​∓50]=1000[50.01∓50]:

ω1​=10 rad/s,ω2​=100010 rad/s.

(The "bandwidth" runs from almost zero to 105: the response is a broad plateau because Q≪1, see the figure.)

(e) At resonance Y=1/R, so the voltage amplitude is Vmax​=Im​R=1×10=10 V.

(f) The capacitor sees V=10 V at ω=1000: XC​=ωC1​=1000 Ω, so

IC,max​=XC​Vmax​​=100010​=0.01 A peak=0.00707 A rms,  leading V by 90∘.

(The sheet's 0.00707∠90∘ A is the rms value.) The inductor carries the same 0.01 A peak at −90∘; they cancel and the resistor carries all 1 A.

Result

ω0​=1000, Q=0.01, BW=105 rad/s, ω1,2​=10, 100010 rad/s, Vmax​=10 V, IC​=0.01 A peak (0.00707 A rms)​

Check

IC​=QIsource​=0.01×1 A ✓ (circulating current is Q times the source). Because Q<1 there is no current magnification; the tank resonates only in the sense that the net reactive current vanishes.

Trap / alternative

Using the series formulas (Q=ω0​L/R=100, BW=R/L=10) would be exactly backwards; for a parallel circuit a smaller R gives a smaller Q. Also state whether a current is peak or rms: 0.01 A peak is 0.00707 A rms.
