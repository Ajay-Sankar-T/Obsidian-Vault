Flywheel Sizing from the Turning-Moment Diagram

conceptKDoM
In one breath

An engine's torque swings above and below the load torque. The flywheel is an energy tank: it takes the surplus and gives it back, so the speed ripples by only a few per cent. Find the largest energy swing ΔE on the torque diagram, then pick the inertia that holds the speed ripple to the allowed Cs​.

Part of KDoMs - MOC. Solved in Tutorial-10-solution.

1. The three equations

Tmean​=angle of one cycleWcycle​​,Cs​=ωmean​ωmax​−ωmin​​,ΔE=Iωmean2​Cs​

  • One cycle is 2π for a two-stroke engine and 4π for a four-stroke.
  • With ωmean​=21​(ωmax​+ωmin​) the last equation is exact: 21​I(ωmax2​−ωmin2​)=Iωmean​(ωmax​−ωmin​)=Iωmean2​Cs​.
  • Mass at radius of gyration k: m=I/k2.

2. Reading the diagram

  1. Convert each area to energy with the diagram's scale. Strokes below the zero line are negative work.
  2. Wcycle​ is the algebraic sum. Tmean​ is its average over the cycle. With a uniform load, the resisting torque is this line.
  3. Where engine torque exceeds Tmean​ the flywheel gains energy; below it, it loses. The cumulative sum E(θ)=∫(T−Tmean​)dθ is the flywheel's energy curve.
  4. ΔE=Emax​−Emin​. It is the area between consecutive crossings of the mean line that gives the biggest swing. On a one-peak diagram that is the area above the mean line on the power stroke.

For a triangular power stroke of base π and peak Tmax​=2a/π (area a), the part above Tmean​ is a similar triangle:

ΔE=21​πTmax​Tmax​−Tmean​​(Tmax​−Tmean​)=2Tmax​π(Tmax​−Tmean​)2​

3. What the numbers say

  • m∝1/Cs​: halve the allowed ripple and you double the flywheel.
  • m∝1/k2: the required I is fixed, so put the mass at the rim. That is why flywheels are rims, not discs.
  • More cylinders smooth the torque and shrink ΔE, which is why a single-cylinder engine needs the heaviest flywheel.
Traps
  • Dividing the cycle work by 2π for a four-stroke engine. It is 4π.
  • Taking the whole power-stroke area as ΔE. Only the part above the mean line counts.
  • Entering rpm in the energy formula. Convert to rad/s.
  • Using ωmax​/ωmin​ in the wrong place in Cs​: it is the difference divided by the mean.
Predict first: the flywheel mass for a ±1 % speed band against the ±0.5 % band at the same ΔE?

The band is the total spread: ±1 % gives Cs​=2%, ±0.5 % gives Cs​=1%. Mass doubles for the narrower band.

Why does the energy curve close after one cycle?

In steady running the average engine torque equals the load torque, so ∫04π​(T−Tmean​)dθ=0. If it did not, the engine would be accelerating or stalling.

4. Connections

  • Mass Moments of Inertia — I=mk2 and why the rim carries the mass.
  • Kinetics of Particles - Work-Energy and Impulse-Momentum — ΔE is just the work-energy theorem for a rotating body.
  • The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy (Impedance Analogy) — with torque ↔ current and speed ↔ voltage, inertia is a capacitor. ΔE=21​C(Vmax2​−Vmin2​) is exactly how the smoothing capacitor of a rectifier is sized for its ripple.
