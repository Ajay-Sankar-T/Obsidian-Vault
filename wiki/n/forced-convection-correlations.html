Forced Convection Correlations

conceptheat-transfer

In one breath

Choose the correlation in three steps: external or internal flow; laminar or turbulent (Re); which temperature the properties are evaluated at. Then Nu=f(Re,Pr) gives h, and q=hAΔT gives the heat.

Part of Heat Transfer - MOC. Previous: Convection Fundamentals - Boundary Layers and Scale Analysis. Next: Natural Convection.

1. External flow over a flat plate (isothermal)

Regime Local Average over 0→L
Laminar, Rex​<5×105, Pr≥0.6 Nux​=0.332Rex1/2​Pr1/3 NuL​=0.664ReL1/2​Pr1/3
Turbulent, Rex​>5×105 Nux​=0.0296Rex4/5​Pr1/3
Mixed (laminar front, turbulent rest) NuL​=(0.037ReL4/5​−871)Pr1/3

Example (mixed): air at 25 m/s over a 2 m plate, ReL​=3.2×106:

NuL​=(0.037×3.2×1060.8−871)(0.707)1/3=4460,hˉ=446020.0264​=59 W/m2K

Compare the laminar value 8.8 W/m²K at 5 m/s: higher speed and turbulence raise h about 7 times.

Cylinder and sphere in cross flow have their own correlations (Churchill–Bernstein for the cylinder, Whitaker for the sphere), with Nu based on the diameter.

2. Internal flow in a tube

Based on the bulk velocity and the diameter: ReD​=ρVD/μ; laminar for ReD​<2300.

Fully developed laminar: NuD​=3.66 (constant wall temperature) or 4.36 (constant heat flux). It is a constant: the temperature profile has become self-similar, so h=Nuk/D does not change with speed or length.

Turbulent, fully developed (long tube, L/D>10):

Dittus–Boelter: NuD​=0.023ReD0.8​Prn,n=0.4 (fluid heated), 0.3 (cooled)

Gnielinski: NuD​=1+12.7f/8​(Pr2/3−1)(f/8)(ReD​−1000)Pr​,f=(0.79lnReD​−1.64)−2

Gnielinski works down to Re≈3000 and across a wider Pr range; Dittus–Boelter is quick for Re>104.

Example. Water at 300 K (ρ=997, μ=8.5×10−4, k=0.610, Pr=5.86) at 1 m/s in a 20 mm tube:

ReD​ NuD​ h (W/m²K)
Dittus–Boelter (heating) 23 300 146 4440
Gnielinski (f=0.0251) 23 300 158 4820
laminar value, for contrast 3.66 112

Turbulence raises h about 40 times. The two correlations differ by 8 %, within their stated scatter.

3. Bulk temperature and exit state

In a tube the fluid heats up along the way. For a constant wall temperature Ts​:

Ts​−Tin​Ts​−Tout​​=exp(−m˙cp​hˉPL​)Q=m˙cp​(Tout​−Tin​)=hˉAΔTlm​

with ΔTlm​=ln(ΔTin​/ΔTout​)ΔTin​−ΔTout​​ (the same logarithmic mean as heat exchangers).

4. Method

  1. Sketch the situation: plate, tube, cylinder, bank; evaluate Re with the right length (L for a plate, D for tube/cylinder).
  2. Laminar or turbulent? Pick the row.
  3. Properties at Tf​ (external) or bulk temperature (internal).
  4. Nu→h→q; check the bulk temperature rise if the tube is long.
Traps
  • Using Dittus–Boelter at Re<104 or in a short entrance region (L/D<10): the developing flow has a higher h.
  • Using the Nusselt number of a tube for a flow in an annulus or non-circular duct without the hydraulic diameter Dh​=4A/P.
  • Applying "turbulent from the leading edge" over a short plate: laminar flow persists until Rex​=5×105.
  • Forgetting that h for the laminar tube does not depend on velocity.
Predict first: doubling the water speed in the tube. By how much does h rise?

h∝Re0.8, so 20.8=1.74: +74 %. The pressure drop rises about 4 times (fV2), so a faster flow buys heat at a steeply rising pumping cost.

Laminar flow in the tube at constant Nu=3.66: how can doubling the diameter change h?

h=3.66k/D, so doubling D halves h. In the laminar regime a thinner tube transfers heat better (the temperature profile fills the whole tube), a reason for microchannel coolers.

5. Connections

  • Pipe Flow and Losses - Formula Bank — the same f and Re; the Reynolds analogy ties friction factor to Nu (Gnielinski's f/8).
  • Viscous Flow and Exact Solutions - Formula Bank — the Poiseuille profile in a tube gives the laminar constants 3.66 and 4.36.
  • Dimensional Analysis and Similitude - Formula Bank — model testing at the same Re and Pr gives the same Nu.
  • Exponential Decay - Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation — the exponential approach of the bulk temperature to Ts​ is the same law with the thermal length m˙cp​/hˉP.
