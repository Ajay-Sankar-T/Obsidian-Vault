Why it works

Jν​(λx) solves the singular Sturm–Liouville problem (xy′)′+(λ2x−ν2/x)y=0 with y(0) bounded and y(L)=0, so Sturm-Liouville Problems gives orthogonality with weight x. Directly: multiply the equations for u=Jν​(ax) and v=Jν​(bx) and subtract,

(b2−a2)∫0c​xuvdx=c[u′v−uv′]x=c​=c[aJν−1​(ac)Jν​(bc)−bJν​(ac)Jν−1​(bc)],

which is 0 when ac,bc are zeros of Jν​; letting b→a gives the norm (Jν′​=−Jν+1​ and Jν−1​=−Jν+1​ at a zero).

Method (works for every problem)

  1. Substitute t=αn​x/L in ∫0L​xfJν​(αn​x/L)dx.
  2. Reduce ∫tmJν​(t)dt with (tνJν​)′=tνJν−1​ and (t−νJν​)′=−t−νJν+1​ (Bessel Function Properties and Recurrence Relations).
  3. Simplify with Jν​(αn​)=0: the recurrences then give Jν±1​ in terms of each other.

Pattern. f=xν+1⋅ (power): ∫0L​xν+1Jν​(αx/L)dx is exact. For ν=0, f=1: an​=αn​J1​(αn​)2​. If the integrand does not match the pattern, ∫J0​ appears and there is no closed form.

Convergence

  • In the weighted L2 norm for square-integrable f.
  • Pointwise on 0<x<L (Bessel expansion theorem) to 21​[f(x+)+f(x−)] when f,f′ are piecewise continuous; Gibbs overshoot (about 9 % of a jump) at jumps.
  • All terms vanish at x=L, so the sum is 0 there whatever f(L) is: expect slow convergence near L unless f(L)=0.
