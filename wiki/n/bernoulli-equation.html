Bernoulli Equation

conceptfluid-mechanicsinviscid-flowbernoulli

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Euler's Equations, Continuity · formulas: Formula Bank §2–§3 · problems: Solved Problems

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: along a streamline in steady, incompressible, frictionless flow, the sum p+21​ρV2+ρgz is constant.

Intuition: it is a mechanical energy bookkeeping for a parcel: pressure energy, kinetic energy and potential energy can be traded, but the total is fixed when nothing dissipates it or adds work. Where the fluid speeds up, the pressure must fall; where it climbs, it loses pressure or speed.

Governing Relation

ρp1​​+2V12​​+gz1​=ρp2​​+2V22​​+gz2​=const along the streamline​

Form Equation Units
pressure p+21​ρV2+γz= const Pa
energy per unit mass ρp​+2V2​+gz= const J/kg
head γp​+2gV2​+z= const m

Unsteady, along a streamline: ∫12​∂t∂V​ds+∫12​ρdp​+2V22​−V12​​+g(z2​−z1​)=0.

With pump, turbine, losses: see Energy Equation with Head Loss.

The six conditions (all must hold):

  1. steady flow
  2. incompressible (Ma<0.3)
  3. frictionless (no boundary layers, wakes, separation between the points)
  4. along a single streamline (or throughout, if the flow is irrotational)
  5. no shaft work between the points
  6. no heat transfer

Where: p = static pressure, V = local speed, z = height above a datum.

Visual

Figure: the energy grade line stays constant (frictionless) while the hydraulic grade line dips by V2/2g in the throat, where the pressure is lowest. The same picture with a pump and losses is in Energy Equation with Head Loss.

When To Use

  • Nozzles, jets, tanks draining, venturis, Pitot tubes, siphons: short, smooth, accelerating flows.
  • Relating pressure and speed between two points in the core flow.
  • First estimate of cavitation (lowest pressure) and forces on a bend (pressure, then momentum).

Recognition pattern: "frictionless", "speed increases so pressure ...", "find the pressure at the throat / the exit speed" → Bernoulli plus continuity.

Method

  1. Identify the streamline and choose point 1 (upstream, where most is known) and point 2.
  2. Check the six conditions; if a pump, turbine or loss intervenes, use the energy equation with head terms.
  3. Apply Bernoulli in one form and add continuity A1​V1​=A2​V2​ (two unknowns need two equations).
  4. Solve for the unknown pressure or speed; use gauge pressure unless a cavitation or compressibility check needs absolute.
  5. Check: pressure falls where speed rises, units, and no friction was assumed.

Derivation / Why It Works

Apply Newton's second law to a parcel of cross-section dA and length ds along a streamline (Euler's equations in streamline coordinates):

ρVdsdV​=−dsdp​−ρgdsdz​ ⟹ ρdp​+d(2V2​)+gdz=0

(steady, so VdV/ds is the full acceleration). Integrating with constant ρ gives the boxed result. The same equation follows from the steady-flow energy equation of thermodynamics with no work, heat or friction.

Rotational flow: the identity (V⋅∇)V=∇(V2/2)−V×ζ shows the constant differs between streamlines by the term V×ζ; for irrotational flow (ζ=0) the constant is the same everywhere.

Common Traps

  • Trap: applying Bernoulli across a pump, turbine, or through a boundary layer or wake.
  • Avoid it by: using the energy equation with hp​, ht​, hl​.
  • Trap: forgetting continuity (two unknown velocities).
  • Trap: applying it between different streamlines in a rotational flow.
  • Trap: using gauge pressure when checking for cavitation (use absolute).
  • Trap: expecting friction losses from Bernoulli: it contains none.
  • Trap: taking V1​=0 for a draining tank when A2​/A1​ is not small.

Example

Given

(a) Water (ρ=998 kg/m³) drains from a large open tank through a small hole 2 m below the free surface. (b) Water flows through a horizontal venturi: pipe 100 mm, throat 50 mm, pipe speed V1​=2 m/s.

Solution

(a) Free surface (point 1, V1​≈0, p1​=patm​) to the jet (point 2, p2​=patm​):

V2​=2gh​=2(9.81)(2)​=6.26 m/s.

(b) Continuity: V2​=V1​(A1​/A2​)=4V1​=8 m/s. Bernoulli at the same height:

p1​−p2​=21​ρ(V22​−V12​)=21​(998)(64−4)=29940 Pa=29.9 kPa,

Q=A1​V1​=7.854×10−3×2=15.7 L/s.

Answer: 6.26 m/s; pressure drop 29.9 kPa and 15.7 L/s. Key takeaway: reading the pressure drop gives the flow rate: Q=1−(A2​/A1​)2​A2​​2Δp/ρ​ for a venturi meter (×cd​ in practice).

Quick Recall

In one line: along a streamline, p+21​ρV2+ρgz is constant (steady, incompressible, frictionless, no work).

Formula / trigger:

ρp1​​+2V12​​+gz1​=ρp2​​+2V22​​+gz2​,A1​V1​=A2​V2​

Remember: speed up, pressure down; always pair it with continuity.
