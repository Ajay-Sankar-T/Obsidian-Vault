T4-Q10: Delta Load, Phase and Line Currents, and Why Their Sum Is Zero

EE1100tutorial-solutionthree-phasedelta-connectionpower

Hub: Tutorial 4 - Resonance, Filter and Three-Phase Circuits · Theory: Theory - Three-Phase Circuits · Previous: T4-Q9 - Balanced Delta Load · Next: T4-Q11 - Star Load Impedance from Power

The delta-connected load of Fig. 1 (same arrangement as Q9, 220 V line-to-line) has a phase impedance of 15+j20 Ω in each phase. (a) Find the phasor current in each line. (b) The power consumed per phase. (c) The phasor sum of the three line currents, and why. [Sheet: 8.8∠−53.13∘, 8.8∠−173.13∘, 8.8∠66.86∘ A; 1161.6 W, 1548.8 VAr; 0 A. The three currents the sheet prints are the phase currents; see below.]

Tests: delta connection, phase versus line current, the zero-sum property of balanced three-phase quantities.

Given

Zp​=15+j20=25∠53.13∘ Ω in each delta arm. The question does not restate the supply; the printed answers imply the same VL​=220 V as Q9, which is assumed here (Vˉab​=220∠0∘, abc sequence).

Principle and why it applies

In a delta, each arm is across a line voltage, so Ip​=VL​/∣Zp​∣ and the line current is 3​Ip​ at −30∘ from the phase current. Balanced three-phase quantities are three equal-magnitude phasors 120∘ apart, whose sum is zero.

Diagram

Solution

(a) Phase currents: Ip​=25220​=8.8 A, lagging Vˉab​ by 53.13∘:

Iˉab​=8.8∠−53.13∘,Iˉbc​=8.8∠−173.13∘,Iˉca​=8.8∠+66.87∘ A

(these are exactly the three phasors the sheet lists). Line currents Iˉa​=Iˉab​−Iˉca​=3​Iˉab​∠−30∘:

Iˉa​=15.24∠−83.13∘,Iˉb​=15.24∠−203.13∘ (=156.87∘),Iˉc​=15.24∠−323.13∘ (=36.87∘)  A.

(b) Power per phase:

Pph​=Ip2​R=8.82×15=1161.6 W,Qph​=Ip2​X=8.82×20=1548.8 VAr (lag),

so Sph​=1936 VA, pf=0.6 lagging. (Total: P=3485 W, Q=4646 VAr, S=5808 VA =3​VL​IL​ ✓.)

(c) Iˉa​+Iˉb​+Iˉc​=0. Why: the three line currents are equal in magnitude and 120∘ apart, 15.24[1∠θ+1∠(θ−120∘)+1∠(θ+120∘)]=0; equivalently, by KCL on the delta (no neutral) the three line currents must sum to zero. This is why a balanced three-wire system needs no neutral conductor.

Result

Ip​=8.8 A, IL​=15.24 A;  Pph​=1161.6 W, Qph​=1548.8 VAr;  Iˉa​+Iˉb​+Iˉc​=0​

Check

The sheet quotes the phase currents under the heading "line current": a delta line current must be 3​ larger (15.24 A), and it is IL​=S/(3​VL​)=5808/(3​×220)=15.24 A ✓. Powers: 3×1161.6=3485 W =3​×220×15.24×0.6 ✓.

Trap / alternative

If the question asks for line currents in a delta circuit, give 3​Ip​ with a 30∘ shift, not Ip​. The assumption VL​=220 V must be stated because the problem does not supply it here. The star equivalent confirms it: ZY​=ZΔ​/3=5+j6.67 Ω (∣ZY​∣=8.33 Ω), Van​=220/3​=127 V, so IL​=127/8.33=15.24 A ✓.
