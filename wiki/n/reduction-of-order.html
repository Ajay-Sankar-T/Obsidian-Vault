Reduction of Order

Related: General Solution Structure Theorem · 2nd Order Linear ODE with Constant Coefficients

Motivation

For y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0, the general solution needs two independent solutions. Sometimes only one solution y1​(x) is known (by inspection, series methods, etc.). Reduction of order constructs a second, independent solution y2​ from y1​.

Idea: seek y2​(x)=v(x)y1​(x) for an unknown function v(x). This reduces the 2nd order ODE for y2​ to a 1st order ODE for v′ — hence the name. The method also works for non-homogeneous equations (finding yp​ from yc​).

Derivation

Let y1​(x) be a known nontrivial solution on I, y1​(x)=0 on I. Assume y=v(x)y1​(x). Then

y′=v′y1​+vy1′​,y′′=v′′y1​+2v′y1′​+vy1′′​.

Substituting into the ODE:

y1​v′′+(2y1′​+P(x)y1​)v′+(y1′′​+P(x)y1′​+Q(x)y1​)v=0

⟹y1​v′′+(2y1′​+P(x)y1​)v′=0

since y1​ solves the homogeneous equation — no v term remains.

Let w=v′. This is first order and separable:

ww′​=−y1​2y1′​​−P(x)

Integrating: ln∣w∣=−2ln∣y1​∣−∫P(x)dx+C, so (taking constant =1)

w(x)=y12​(x)e−∫P(x)dx​.

Integrating again gives v(x)=∫y12​(x)e−∫P(x)dx​dx, hence the second solution formula:

y2​(x)=y1​(x)∫y12​(x)e−∫P(x)dx​dx​

Now W(y1​,y2​)=y12​w=e−∫P(x)dx=0, matching Abel's Formula, so y1​,y2​ are independent. General solution: y=c1​y1​(x)+c2​y2​(x).

Worked Examples

1. y′′−2ry′+r2y=0, y1​=erx. Here P(x)=−2r, e−∫Pdx=e2rx.

v(x)=∫(erx)2e2rx​dx=∫1dx=x⟹y2​=xerx

GS: y=c1​erx+c2​xerx.

2. x2y′′−xy′+y=0, x>0, y1​=x. Standard form gives P(x)=−x1​, e−∫Pdx=x.

v(x)=∫x2x​dx=∫x1​dx=lnx⟹y2​=xlnx

GS: y=c1​x+c2​xlnx.

3. (1−x2)y′′−2xy′+2y=0, y1​=x. Standard form: P(x)=−1−x22x​, e−∫Pdx=1−x21​.

v(x)=∫x2(1−x2)1​dx=∫(x21​+1−x21​)dx=−x1​+21​ln​1−x1+x​​

y2​=−1+2x​ln​1−x1+x​​

4. y′′+y=0, y1​=cosx. Here P(x)=0, e−∫Pdx=1.

v(x)=∫sec2xdx=tanx⟹y2​=tanx⋅cosx=sinx

Fundamental set: {cosx,sinx} — confirms the earlier example in Existence, Uniqueness, and Fundamental Sets.
