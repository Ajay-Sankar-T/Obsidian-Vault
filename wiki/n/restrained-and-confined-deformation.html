The pattern (Q2): a block under pressure, restrained to different degrees

A block is loaded by pressure σz​=−p0​ on its top face, with the x and y faces either free or rigidly restrained by the walls of a press. The golden rule — restrained ⇒ε=0, free ⇒σ=0 — turns each restraint case into a small linear-algebra problem on the Generalized Hooke's Law matrix.

        σz = -p0  (applied)
            ↓ ↓ ↓
      ┌───────────────┐
  x ← │               │ → x   (restrained or free?)
      │     BLOCK      │
  y ← │               │ → y   (restrained or free?)
      └───────────────┘

Case 1 — both x and y restrained

εx​=εy​=0. By symmetry (identical restraint in both directions), σx​=σy​. Substituting into Hooke's law εx​=E1​[σx​−ν(σy​+σz​)]=0 with σy​=σx​, σz​=−p0​:

σx​−ν(σx​−p0​)=0⟹σx​=σy​=−1−νν​p0​​,σz​=−p0​​

Case 2 — y restrained, x free

Free x: σx​=0​. Restrained y: εy​=0=E1​[σy​−ν(σx​+σz​)]=E1​[σy​−ν(−p0​)], so

σy​=−νp0​​,σz​=−p0​​

Case 3 — both x,y free

σx​=σy​=0​, σz​=−p0​. Strains follow directly from Hooke's law with no restraint stresses to solve for:

εx​=εy​=Eνp0​​​,εz​=−Ep0​​​

Note the block expands laterally (εx​,εy​>0) even though it's being compressed — pure Poisson effect, no lateral stress required.

The pattern (Q3): fully confined rubber, effective modulus

A rubber block confined on x and y by rigid walls, loaded axially by σz​=−p. This is exactly Case 1 above, so σx​=σy​=−1−νν​p. The axial strain is then:

εz​=E1​[σz​−ν(σx​+σy​)]=E1​[−p−2ν(−1−νν​p)]

εz​=−Ep​⋅1−ν(1+ν)(1−2ν)​​

Define an effective (constrained) modulus Eeff​=p/∣εz​∣:

Eeff​=(1+ν)(1−2ν)E(1−ν)​​

The physically striking result

For rubber, ν≈0.5 (nearly incompressible), so 1−2ν→0 and Eeff​→∞​ — a laterally confined near-incompressible material becomes enormously stiffer axially than its own (small) Young's modulus would suggest, because it can no longer relieve axial compression by bulging sideways. This is the mechanical principle behind rubber bushings/mounts confined in a rigid metal sleeve, and behind why Eeff​ is the quantity relevant for such a mount's actual axial stiffness, not the bulk E from a tensile test.

Cross-domain: this is the same ν→0.5 limit as the bulk modulus

Eeff​→∞ as ν→0.5 is the same phenomenon already noted in Generalized Hooke's Law and Poisson's Ratio for the bulk modulus K=E/[3(1−2ν)]→∞ under hydrostatic loading. Confinement in one axial direction with two lateral restraints is a partial version of full hydrostatic confinement — the same (1−2ν) denominator appears because both cases are limited by the material's resistance to volume change, not shape change, and volume change is exactly what vanishes as ν→0.5.
