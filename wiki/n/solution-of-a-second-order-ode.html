Solution of a Second Order ODE

Related: Standard Form and Homogeneity

Consider the 2nd order DE F(x,y,y′,y′′)=0 over I. y=ϕ(x) is a solution on I if:

  • ϕ is defined on I
  • ϕ is twice differentiable on I
  • the ODE becomes an identity when y,y′,y′′ are replaced by ϕ,ϕ′,ϕ′′.

Example: y=cosx solves y′′+y=0: y′=−sinx, y′′=−cosx, LHS =−cosx+cosx=0= RHS.

Similarly y=sinx is a solution. In general, y=C1​y1​+C2​y2​ is a solution of y′′+y=0 if y1​=cosx, y2​=sinx, C1​,C2​ arbitrary constants.

Superposition Principle

Theorem. For a homogeneous linear ODE y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0, x∈I (2), any linear combination of two solutions on an open interval I is again a solution of (2) on I.

  • V={y∣y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0} is a subspace of D2(I).
  • V1​={y∣y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=R(x)} is NOT a subspace when R(x)≡0.

Example: y1​=1+cosx, y2​=1+sinx are solutions of y′′+y=1. But y1​+y2​=2+cosx+sinx is not a solution: y′′+y=(−cosx−sinx)+(2+cosx+sinx)=2=1. Superposition fails for non-homogeneous equations.

Nonlinear ODE counterexample

Consider the nonlinear ODE y′′y−xy′=0.

  • y1​=x2 is a solution: (x2)′′x2−x(x2)′=2x2−2x2=0.
  • y2​=1 satisfies the DE.
  • But y1​+y2​=x2+1 is not a solution: (x2+1)′′(x2+1)−x(x2+1)′=2(x2+1)−2x2=2=0.
  • A constant multiple of x2, e.g. −x2, is also not a solution.

This shows superposition is a property special to linear homogeneous equations — it fails for nonlinear equations even without a forcing term.
