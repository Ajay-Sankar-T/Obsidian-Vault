Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits

EE1100AC-circuitsimpedance

How to attack a problem
  1. Say what is asked and what is given.
  2. Pick the principle before calculating (open the hint).
  3. Solve symbolically, then plug in numbers.
  4. Check units and a limiting case.
  5. Mark it solved only if you could redo it.

← up: Tutorial 3 · builds on: Week 6, Phasors and Complex Algebra

Definition

Impedance is the AC generalization of resistance:

Z=R+jX (Ω),∣Z∣=R2+X2​,θZ​=tan−1RX​

R is resistance (real, dissipative); X is reactance (imaginary, energy-storing — positive for inductive, negative for capacitive).

The three elements

Starting from the time-domain element laws vR​=iR, vL​=Ldi/dt, iC​=Cdv/dt, and using the phasor substitution d/dt→jω (from Phasors and Complex Algebra):

Element Impedance Reactance Phase of Z Current vs voltage
Resistor ZR​=R — 0° in phase
Inductor ZL​=jωL XL​=ωL +90° current lags ("ELI")
Capacitor ZC​=−j/(ωC)=−jXC​, XC​=1/(ωC) XC​=1/(ωC) −90° current leads ("ICE")

Memory: R→0°, L→+90°, C→−90° — matches the "ELI the ICE man" mnemonic already used in Week 5; impedance is what makes that phase statement quantitative (magnitude, not just direction).

AC Ohm's law

Vˉ=IˉZ⟺Iˉ=ZVˉ​⟺Z=IˉVˉ​

Series combination

Zeq​=Z1​+Z2​+⋯

For series RLC: Z=R+jXL​−jXC​=R+j(XL​−XC​) — the single most-used formula in this tutorial (Q3, Q5, Q7).

Three cases for series RLC

  • Inductive (XL​>XC​): Z=R+jX, current lags voltage.
  • Capacitive (XC​>XL​): Z=R−jX, current leads voltage.
  • Resonance (XL​=XC​): Z=R (purely resistive), V and I in phase — see Week 8 for the full resonance treatment (ω0​=1/LC​).

RC series (Q5 pattern)

Z=R−jXC​, ∣Z∣=R2+XC2​​, θZ​=−tan−1(XC​/R), so θI​=−θZ​>0: current leads. Worked example: Z=6−j8 Ω⇒∣Z∣=10, θZ​=−53.13°, so current leads voltage by 53.13°.

RL series (Q3 pattern)

Z=R+jXL​, ∣Z∣=R2+XL2​​, θZ​=tan−1(XL​/R), current lags.

Series RLC phase angle

ϕ=tan−1(RXL​−XC​​),θI​=θV​−ϕ

Parallel combination

Zeq​=Z1​+Z2​Z1​Z2​​ (two branches)

For more than two branches, use admittance (see below) rather than repeated product-over-sum — the same reasoning as Series-Parallel Resistance Reduction for DC resistor networks, but now with complex numbers where the "reciprocal of the sum of reciprocals" shortcut for exactly two elements still holds.

Admittance

Y=Z1​ (siemens, S),Yeq​=Y1​+Y2​+⋯ (parallel),Zeq​=Yeq​1​

Basic admittances: YR​=1/R, YL​=1/(jωL)=−j/(ωL), YC​=jωC.

Phasor voltage relationships in a series circuit

With current as reference phasor I∠0°: VR​=IR (at 0°), VL​=IXL​ (at +90°), VC​=IXC​ (at −90°), and the source voltage is the phasor sum

Vˉ=VˉR​+VˉL​+VˉC​

not a scalar sum — see Phasors and Complex Algebra.

Voltage magnification in series RLC (Q7)

Because VˉL​ and VˉC​ are 180° out of phase with each other (one at +90°, one at −90° relative to I), they can partially cancel in the phasor sum even while each individually is large:

∣VL​∣>∣Vsource​∣ and ∣VC​∣>∣Vsource​∣ are both possible

This is not a KVL violation — KVL holds for the phasor (vector) sum, not for magnitudes. This effect is the qualitative preview of resonance (Week 8): near ω0​, XL​≈XC​ so Z≈R is small, current is large, and VL​,VC​ (each =IXL,C​) can dramatically exceed the source voltage — the basis of voltage magnification in tuned circuits.
