Thevenin's theorem

Any linear two-terminal network reduces to a source VTh​ in series with ZTh​. For a load ZL​ attached to the terminals:

IL​=ZTh​+ZL​VTh​​

Finding VTh​: remove the load; VTh​=VOC​, the open-circuit terminal voltage.

Finding ZTh​: deactivate all independent sources (voltage source → short, current source → open — same as DC), then find the impedance looking into the terminals via series/parallel Z reduction (Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits).

Norton's theorem

Equivalent representation: current source IN​ in parallel with ZN​, where

IN​=ISC​ (short-circuit current),ZN​=ZTh​,VTh​=IN​ZN​

Source transformation between the two forms works exactly as in DC.

Maximum power transfer — DC baseline

For a purely resistive load: RL​=RTh​ gives Pmax​=VTh2​/(4RTh​) — see Concepts — Network Theorems and Tutorial 1.

Maximum power transfer — AC (Q12, the critical formula)

If ZTh​=RTh​+jXTh​ and the load impedance ZL​ can be any complex value, maximum average power occurs at the complex-conjugate match:

ZL​=ZTh∗​=RTh​−jXTh​​

i.e. RL​=RTh​ and XL​=−XTh​ — the load's reactance is chosen to exactly cancel the source's reactance, so the net loop reactance is zero and only the two resistances (equal) remain, which is precisely the DC-style matched-resistance condition applied to what's left after cancellation.

Memory: maximum AC power → load = conjugate of Thevenin impedance.

With the conjugate match in place, the reactive parts cancel and

Pmax​=4RTh​∣VTh​∣2​

— the same form as the DC result, now with RTh​ specifically (not ∣ZTh​∣).

Constrained case: if the load is restricted to be purely resistive (no reactance available), the optimum instead becomes RL​=∣ZTh​∣ — a weaker match than the full conjugate condition, noted already in Week 7.
