If u(z) solves Bessel's equation of order n then y(x)=xau(bxc) solves

y′′+x1−2a​y′+(b2c2x2c−2+x2a2−n2c2​)y=0(Simmons; Lecture 26).

So the general solution is y=xa[AJn​(bxc)+BJ−n​(bxc)] (replace J−n​ by Yn​ if n∈Z).

Recipe (match, don't re-derive)

Put the target in the form y′′+Py′+Qy=0 and match:

  1. P=x1−2a​⇒a.
  2. The power of x in the x-dependent part of Q is 2c−2⇒c; its coefficient is b2c2⇒b.
  3. The 1/x2 part of Q equals a2−n2c2⇒n=a2−(that)​/c.

Z denotes J or Y (use J±n​ when n∈/Z).

Equations with no power of x

If the coefficients involve e−x and so on, change the independent variable directly: y′′+e−xy=0 with z=2e−x/2 gives z2u′′+zu′+z2u=0, i.e. y=Z0​(2e−x/2). General guide: z=∫Q​dx makes the coefficient of u equal to 1, and the first-derivative coefficient then identifies the Bessel order (normal form, Lecture 26 slides 22–23).

Why the Airy equation gives order 31​

a=21​ from the missing y′ term and c=23​ from x2c−2=x; then a2=n2c2 gives n=a/c=31​. The three-thirds structure is why Ai and Bi are Bessel functions of order ±31​ in 32​x3/2.
