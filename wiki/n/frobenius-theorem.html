Frobenius Theorem

Related: Regular and Irregular Singular Points · Cauchy-Euler Equations (2nd Order DE)

Theorem (Frobenius). Let x=0 be a regular singular point of y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0. Then there exists at least one solution of the form

y=xrn=0∑∞​an​xn,a0​=0.

  • r is a constant (real or complex) to be determined — the exponent need not be a non-negative integer.
  • The series converges in some interval 0<x<R, where R is the distance to the nearest other singular point (same principle as Radius of Convergence of Series Solutions).
  • A second linearly independent solution may or may not have the same simple form; its form depends on the relationship between the two roots of the indicial equation (three cases arise — analogous to the three cases for the auxiliary/indicial equation of a Cauchy-Euler equation: distinct roots not differing by an integer, roots differing by an integer, and repeated roots).

This generalizes the ordinary power series method (Power Series Method) by allowing the leading factor xr — motivated by the standard form near a regular singular point derived in Regular and Irregular Singular Points, where the equation resembles a Cauchy-Euler equation with variable coefficients. Deriving the indicial equation that determines r is the next step: see Frobenius Method - Indicial Equation and Three Cases (derivation, the three cases, when a logarithm appears) and Frobenius Method - Worked Examples.
