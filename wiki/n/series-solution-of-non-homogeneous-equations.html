Series Solution of Non-homogeneous Equations

Related: Series Solutions - Homogeneous Examples · General Solution Structure Theorem (2nd Order DE) · Undetermined Coefficients (2nd Order DE)

The power series method extends directly to non-homogeneous equations, following the same y=yc​+yp​ structure as General Solution Structure Theorem — except here yp​ is also built as a power series, and its coefficients are driven by the power-series expansion of the right-hand side.

y′′+xy′+y=x about x=0">

Example: y′′+xy′+y=x about x=0

By Series Solutions - Homogeneous Examples Example 4, the homogeneous equation y′′+xy′+y=0 already gives yc​. Taking yp​ as the same type of series, the LHS is

n=0∑∞​[(n+2)(n+1)an+2​+(n+1)an​]xn.

Since the RHS is x, its power-series coefficients are b0​=0, b1​=1, bn​=0 (n≥2). Consequently

(n+2)(n+1)an+2​+(n+1)an​=bn​⟹an+2​=(n+2)(n+1)bn​−(n+1)an​​.

  • n=0: 2a2​+a0​=0⇒a2​=−2a0​​
  • n=1: 6a3​+2a1​=1⇒a3​=61​−3a1​​
  • n≥2: an+2​=−n+2an​​ (same as the homogeneous recurrence)

Taking a0​=a1​=0 isolates a particular solution:

yp​=6x3​−30x5​+210x7​−1890x9​+⋯

General solution: y=yc​+yp​, where yc​ is the homogeneous series from Series Solutions - Homogeneous Examples.

Key idea: whenever the RHS itself has a (finite or simple) power-series expansion, absorb its coefficients bn​ directly into the recurrence relation for an+2​ — no separate technique (like Undetermined Coefficients or Variation of Parameters) is needed, since the series method handles arbitrary analytic forcing terms automatically.
