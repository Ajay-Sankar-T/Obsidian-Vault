Sinusoidal Waveforms, RMS and Average Value

EE1100AC-circuitswaveforms

← up: Tutorial 3 · builds on: Week 5

The waveform

x(t)=Xm​sin(ωt+ϕ)

  • Xm​ — amplitude (peak value)
  • ω=2πf — angular frequency (rad/s); f=ω/2π; period T=1/f=2π/ω
  • ϕ — phase angle, position in the cycle at t=0

Quick conversion example: v(t)=100sin(400t) gives ω=400 rad/s, so f=400/2π≈63.66 Hz.

Average value

Xavg​=T1​∫0T​x(t)dt=Tsigned area in one period​

For a symmetric bipolar sine wave, Xavg​=0 — positive and negative half-cycles cancel exactly. This is why AC "average value" quoted in practice (e.g. for a rectified waveform) always refers to the average over a half-cycle or after rectification, never the raw sine.

RMS value

RMS = root mean square — the DC-equivalent value that would dissipate the same average power in a resistor:

Xrms​=T1​∫0T​x2(t)dt​

Three-step memory trick: SQUARE → AVERAGE → ROOT.

For a pure sinusoid x(t)=Xm​sinωt:

Xrms​=2​Xm​​⟹Xm​=2​Xrms​

Derivation: x2=Xm2​sin2ωt=2Xm2​​(1−cos2ωt). The cos2ωt term averages to zero over a full period, leaving mean square =Xm2​/2, hence RMS =Xm​/2​.

RMS vs peak — the classic trap

If v(t)=141sinωt, then Vm​=141 V but Vrms​=99.7 V≈100 V (this is exactly the "230 V mains" convention noted in Week 5 — quoted AC voltages are RMS, not peak). Every power formula in AC Power and Power Factor uses RMS values; using Vm​,Im​ directly in P=VIcosϕ overstates power by a factor of 2.

RMS of a stepped (piecewise-constant) waveform

If the waveform takes values X1​,X2​,… for durations t1​,t2​,… within one period T:

Xrms​=TX12​t1​+X22​t2​+⋯​​

This is the same SQUARE→AVERAGE→ROOT idea, just with a sum replacing the integral — the natural tool for Q2-style waveforms built from flat segments rather than a continuous sine.

Converting cosine to sine (and back)

Needed whenever a mixed set of cosine- and sine-form sources/currents must be combined as phasors (see Phasors and Complex Algebra):

cosθ=sin(θ+90∘)

Example: 28.3cos(ωt−60∘)=28.3sin(ωt+30∘).
