1. Ex. 5.6 Capillary rise and its scaling

Concept. Pi theorem with one angle; scaling a similar experiment.

Given. h=f(d,g,ρ,σ,θ), n=6. (b) h1​=3 cm; case 2 has d2​=d1​/2, σ2​=σ1​/2, ρ2​=2ρ1​, θ2​=θ1​.

Principle. {FLT}: h,d→L; g→LT−2; ρ→FT2L−4; σ→FL−1; θ dimensionless. j=3 ⇒ n−j=3 groups.

Steps.

  1. Π3​=θ already dimensionless.
  2. Repeating {ρ,g,d}: Π1​=h/d; Π2​=σρ−1g−1d−2=ρgd2σ​.
  3. dh​=F(ρgd2σ​,θ).
  4. For case 2: ρ2​gd22​σ2​​=2ρ1​gd12​/4σ1​/2​=ρ1​gd12​σ1​​ – the same Π, same θ ⇒ the same h/d.

h2​=h1​d1​d2​​=3×21​=1.5 cm​

Trap / alternative. Only valid because every Π matches; if σ had changed differently you would need the function F (theory gives h=4σcosθ/(ρgd), Ex. 1.9).

2. P5.20 Centrifugal pump

Given. P=f(Q,D,Ω,ρ,μ); n=6, j=3 ⇒ 3 groups. Repeating {Ω,ρ,D}.

Solution (the exponents come from pi_groups, which also verifies them symbolically):

group form name
Π1​=PΩ−3ρ−1D−5 ρΩ3D5P​ power coefficient CP​
Π2​=QΩ−1D−3 ΩD3Q​ flow coefficient CQ​
Π3​=μρ−1Ω−1D−2 ρΩD2μ​=1/Re Reynolds number

ρΩ3D5P​=g(ΩD3Q​, μρΩD2​)​

Use. Same-family pumps (Re nearly irrelevant): P∝ρΩ3D5, Q∝ΩD3. Doubling speed ⇒ power ×8, flow ×2.

3. P5.19 Surface-wave period

Given. T=f(ρ,λ,h,g,σ), n=6, j=3. Repeating {λ,ρ,g}.

Solution.

Π1​=Tg/λ​,Π2​=h/λ,Π3​=ρgλ2σ​ (inverse Bond number)

Tλg​​=F(λh​, ρgλ2σ​)​

If σ is negligible (long gravity waves): Tg/λ​=F(h/λ); deep water (h≫λ) it becomes a constant (T=2πλ/g​ from theory).

4. P5.37 Blast wave and P5.12, P5.13, P5.32 short derivations

P5.37 Δp=f(r,t,a,E); n=5, j=3 ⇒ 2 groups; repeating {r,a,E} (independent: determinant of their exponents is 1) – result

EΔpr3​=g(rat​) ⇒ Δp∝E ⇒ doubling E doubles Δp​

P5.12 a=f(p,ρ): [a]=LT−1, [p/ρ]=L2T−2 ⇒ a=Cp/ρ​ (one group, constant; theory C=k​).

P5.13 C=f(ρ,λ,σ): n=4, j=3 ⇒ one group; σ/(ρλ) has dimensions L2T−2, so C=constσ/(ρλ)​. Doubling σ at fixed ρ,λ: C→2​C (×1.41).

P5.32 weir Q=f(g,b,H), Q∝b: with b taken out, Q/b=f(g,H), [Q/b]=L2T−1 ⇒ Q=Cbg1/2H3/2.

5. P5.34 Thermal conductivity of hydrogen

Concept. Temperature adds a fourth dimension (j=4).

Given. k=f(ρ,λ,R,T) with k in W/m·K, mean free path λ, gas constant R, absolute temperature T. Air at 20 °C, 1 atm: k=0.026 W/m·K, λ=6.5×10−8 m. Hydrogen: λ=1.2×10−7 m.

Principle. n=5 variables, 4 primary dimensions ⇒ kΠ​=1. {ρ,λ,R,T} repeat; the group

ρλR3/2T1/2k​=const(checked symbolically in fm_core)

Steps (same T and p ⇒ ρ∝1/R∝M, molar masses Mair​=28.97, MH2​​=2.016):

kair​kH​​=ρair​ρH​​λair​λH​​(Rair​RH​​)3/2=14.371​×1.846×14.371.5=1.84614.37​=7.0

kH2​​≈0.182 W/m⋅K​(tabulated ≈0.18)

6. P5.4 Water-tested sphere to a weather balloon

Concept. Dynamic similarity = equal Re (no free surface, low speed).

Given. Sphere 8 cm, water 20 °C, Vw​=2 m/s, drag Fw​=5 N. Balloon D=1.5 m in sea-level standard air. ρw​=998, μw​=0.001; ρa​=1.225, μa​=1.78×10−5 SI.

Steps.

  1. νw​=1.002×10−6, νa​=1.453×10−5 m²/s.
  2. Re equal: Va​=Vw​Da​Dw​​νw​νa​​=2×1.50.08​×14.5=1.55 m/s​
  3. Same CD​ ⇒ ρV2D2F​ equal:

Fa​=Fw​ρw​ρa​​(Vw​Va​​)2(Dw​Da​​)2=5×9981.225​×0.598×351.6=1.29 N​

Trap. The speed is low because air is 14.5× more viscous kinematically. The force follows from the full ratio Fa​/Fw​=ρw​ρa​​(Va​/Vw​)2(Da​/Dw​)2=0.258, not from the speed ratio alone.

8. P5.5 Car drag in Colorado

Concept. Use dimensionless data: convert measured drag to CD​ and compare at equal Re.

Given. L=8 ft, A=60 ft². Sea-level data: 31, 115, 249 lbf at 20, 40, 60 mi/h. Colorado: 65 mi/h at 3500 m.

Steps.

  1. Sea level: ρ=0.002377 slug/ft³, μ=3.74×10−7 slug/ft·s.
V (mi/h) ReL​ CD​=21​ρV2AF​
20 1.49×106 0.505
40 2.99×106 0.469
60 4.48×106 0.451
  1. At 3500 m (ISA): ρ=0.863 kg/m³ =0.001675 slug/ft³, μ=3.50×10−7 slug/ft·s; V=95.3 ft/s ⇒ Re=3.65×106, inside the data range.
  2. Interpolate CD​ against lnRe: CD​≈0.460.

F=CD​21​ρV2A=0.460×21​(0.001675)(95.3)2(60)=≈210 lbf​,P=FV=550210×95.3​=≈36 hp​

9. P5.15 Can a 1:20 airplane model be tested?

Same pressure and temperature ⇒ same ν. Equal Re: Vm​Lm​=Vp​Lp​⇒Vm​=20×300=6000 mi/h ≈2680 m/s, i.e. Ma≈8 – compressibility would then dominate and destroy similarity. Not feasible; use a pressurised (smaller ν) or cryogenic tunnel, or test at lower Re and extrapolate.

10. P5.26 Pendulum on the moon

T=f(L,m,θ,g): n=5, j=2 (m cannot form a group with L,g; m drops out) ⇒ Tg/L​=F(θ).

  • (a) 45° vs 20° on earth: cannot be found by dimensional analysis (the group changes with θ); you need the function F (for small angles F=2π).
  • (b) Moon, L=0.3 m, 20°: same θ ⇒ same F:

T2​=T1​L1​L2​​g2​g1​​​=2.040.3×1.629.81​​=2.75 s​

(mass is irrelevant).
