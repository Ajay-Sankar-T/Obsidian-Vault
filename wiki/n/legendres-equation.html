Legendre's Equation

Related: Power Series Method · Ordinary and Singular Points · Hermite's Equation · Airy's Equation

(1−x2)y′′−2xy′+n(n+1)y=0.

Here P(x)=1−x2−2x​, Q(x)=1−x2n(n+1)​ — both analytic at x=0, so x=0 is an ordinary point. Singular points occur at x=±1 (see Regular and Irregular Singular Points — these turn out to be regular).

Assume y=∑k=0∞​ak​xk and substitute. After shifting indices, the recurrence relation is

ak+2​=(k+1)(k+2)k(k+1)−n(n+1)​ak​.

This links only alternate coefficients:

  • a0​=1,a1​=0 gives an even series solution y1​(x).
  • a0​=0,a1​=1 gives an odd series solution y2​(x).

General solution: y=a0​y1​(x)+a1​y2​(x).

If n is a non-negative integer, one of the two series terminates (since the factor k(k+1)−n(n+1) vanishes once k=n), giving the Legendre polynomial Pn​(x) as a solution — a polynomial of degree n.

Series converges for ∣x∣<1 (radius of convergence =1, matching the distance to the nearest singular point x=±1, consistent with Radius of Convergence of Series Solutions).
