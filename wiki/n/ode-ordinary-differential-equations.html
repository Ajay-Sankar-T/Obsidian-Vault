ODE (Ordinary Differential Equations)

See First Order ODEs - MOC for the full note map.

▶ An ordinary differential equation (ODE) is an equation involving an unknown function of a single independent variable and one or more of its derivatives.

###Order ###Degree
▶ The order of an ODE is determined by the highest-order
derivative appearing in the equation.		 ▶ The degree of an ordinary differential equation is the power
(exponent) of the highest-order derivative appearing in the
equation, after the equation has been made free of:
- fractional powers or roots of derivatives
- negative powers of derivatives
- derivatives appearing in denominators (fractions)
In other words, first write the ODE as a polynomial equation in derivatives; the degree is then the exponent on the highest derivative term.

Examples

Example 1

(dx2d2y​)3+(dxdy​)2+y=0

Order = 2 (highest derivative is the 2nd derivative) Degree = 3 (that highest derivative is raised to the power 3)

Example 2

dx2d2y​+dxdy​​=0

Here dxdy​ appears under a square root, thus not in polynomial form. Squaring to remove the radical:

(dx2d2y​)2=dxdy​

Order = 2, Degree = 2

Important notes

  • Order = highest derivative present (always well-defined).
  • Degree is only defined when the ODE can be expressed as a polynomial in its derivatives. If not (e.g., derivatives inside sin, log, or exponential functions), the degree is not defined.

Example where degree is undefined

dx2d2y​+sin(dxdy​)=0

Order = 2, but degree is undefined since dxdy​ sits inside a sine function.
