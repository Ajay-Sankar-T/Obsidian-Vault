System and Control Volume

fluid-mechanicscontrol-volume

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Lagrangian and Eulerian Descriptions

System

A fixed, identified quantity of matter — you follow the same fluid particles wherever they go. System=Lagrangian​. Physical laws (Newton's second law, conservation of mass, conservation of energy) are stated most fundamentally for a system.

Control volume (CV)

A chosen region in space through which fluid can freely flow in and out; its boundary is the control surface (CS). CV=Eulerian​. Engineering analysis almost always prefers a control volume — fixed in the lab frame, easy to draw around a pump, nozzle, or pipe junction — over tracking a system, whose shape distorts unrecognizably as it flows.

Why this distinction matters

Every conservation law in this vault (Conservation of Mass, Linear Momentum Equation) is a law about a system, but every equation actually solved in this vault is written for a control volume. The translation between the two is exactly the Reynolds Transport Theorem — the single most important bookkeeping tool in this folder, playing the same "translate between two natural reference frames" role that the material derivative plays one level down (for a single particle instead of an entire region).
