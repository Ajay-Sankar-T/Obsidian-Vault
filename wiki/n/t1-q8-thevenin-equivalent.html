T1-Q8: Thevenin Equivalent Seen by the 20 Ω Resistor

EE1100tutorial-solutionDC-circuitsthevenin

Hub: Tutorial 1 - Circuit Laws and Theorems · Theory: Theory - Circuit Laws and Network Theorems · Previous: T1-Q7 - Mesh Analysis with Current Sources · Next: T1-Q9 - Norton Equivalent

Find the Thevenin equivalent seen by the 20 Ω resistor between a and b in the network with sources of 45 V, 120 V and 20 V and resistors 10,5,5,15 Ω. [Sheet: Vth​=63 V, Rth​=4.67 Ω]

Tests: Thevenin's theorem: remove the load, find Voc​, kill sources for Rth​.

Given

The 45 V source (positive at the top) joins the top rail a to the left node L. The 120 V source (positive on the left) joins a to the top-right corner, then 15 Ω down to the right node R. 10 Ω from L to b and 5 Ω from b to R along the middle. Along the bottom: the 20 V source (positive on the left) and a 5 Ω from L round to R. The 20 Ω load sits between a and b.

Principle and why it applies

The load is one element in a linear network; everything else can be collapsed into Vth​ and Rth​ as seen from a-b (see theory §8). Step 1 remove the 20 Ω; step 2 compute the open-circuit voltage; step 3 switch the independent sources off and compute the resistance between a and b.

Diagram

Solution

Open-circuit voltage. Take b as the reference (0 V). Let the left node be at VL​ and the right node at VR​. The top rail is Va​=VL​+45 and the top-right corner is Va​−120=VL​−75. Currents: through the 15 Ω (downwards) 15VL​−75−VR​​; through the 10 Ω 10VL​​; through the lower branch (20 V source then 5 Ω) 5VL​−20−VR​​; through the middle 5 Ω (from R to b) 5VR​​.

KCL at R:  15VL​−75−VR​​+5VL​−20−VR​​=5VR​​ ⇒ 4VL​−7VR​=135.

KCL at L (the 45 V source delivers the same current as the 120 V source and 15 Ω):  10VL​​+5VL​−20−VR​​+15VL​−75−VR​​=0 ⇒ 11VL​−8VR​=270.

Solving: VR​=−9 V, VL​=18 V. So

Vth​=Va​−Vb​=(18+45)−0=63 V.

Thevenin resistance (all three sources shorted). The shorted 45 V source ties a to L and the shorted 120 V source ties a to the top-right corner, so a, L and that corner are one node. From it to R there are two parallel paths: the 15 Ω and the lower 5 Ω (the 20 V source is shorted): 15∥5=3.75 Ω. Looking from a to b: the 10 Ω (directly from L=a to b) is in parallel with (5 Ω from b to R in series with 3.75 Ω back to a):

Rth​=10∥(5+3.75)=18.7510×8.75​=4.67 Ω.

Result

Vth​=63 V,Rth​=4.67 Ω​

The 20 Ω load would then draw 63/(4.67+20)=2.55 A and drop 51 V.

Check

Tools/ee_t1_t4_t8.py solves the original circuit (with the 20 Ω removed) by modified nodal analysis and also gets Va​−Vb​=63.0 V. Dimensionally Rth​<10 Ω (it must be below the smallest direct path) ✓.

Trap / alternative

Don't leave the 20 Ω in when computing Voc​; and when finding Rth​, remember that a shorted ideal voltage source merges nodes, which is what makes the diagram collapse. An alternative route to Vth​ is a single mesh/nodal solve of the loaded circuit followed by Vth​=Vab​(1+Rth​/20).

Concepts used

  • Thevenin's theorem, nodal analysis, source shorting (Theory - Circuit Laws and Network Theorems)
