T7-Q1 — Turns and Core Area from the EMF Equation

EE1100transformertutorial-solutionemf-equation

Hub: Tutorial 7 on Transformer · Next: T7-Q2 - Exciting Current and Primary Current

The emf per turn of a single-phase 2310/220 V, 50 Hz transformer is approximately 13 V. Calculate (a) the number of primary and secondary turns, (b) the net core cross-section for a maximum flux density of 1.4 Wb/m². [(a) 189, 18 (b) 393 cm²]

Tests: EMF equation E=4.44fNΦm​, turns ratio.

Given

V1​/V2​=2310/220=10.5, f=50 Hz, E/N≈13 V, Bm​=1.4 T.

Principle and why it applies

Both windings link the same core flux, so every turn carries the same emf Et​=4.44fΦm​. Hence N=E/Et​ and the flux amplitude is Φm​=Et​/4.44f, with A=Φm​/Bm​.

Solution

(a) N1​=132310​=177.7, N2​=13220​=16.9. Turns must be whole numbers in the exact ratio 10.5:1; the nearest pair is N1​=189, N2​=18, which gives Et​=18220​=12.22 V per turn ("approximately 13 V" in the problem).

(b) Φm​=4.44fEt​​=4.44×5012.22​=55.05 mWb;

A=Bm​Φm​​=1.40.05505​=0.03932 m2=393 cm2.

Result

N1​=189, N2​=18,Anet​=393 cm2

Check

N1​/N2​=189/18=10.5 ✓. Using exactly 13 V/turn gives A=4.44⋅50⋅1.413​=418 cm2 and non-integer turns; the key's 393 cm² uses Et​=12.22 V. E1​=4.44×50×189×0.05505=2310 V ✓.

Trap / alternative

Rounding N1​ and N2​ independently (178 and 17) breaks the ratio. Also "net" core area excludes insulation between laminations (a stacking factor of about 0.9 would make the gross section larger).
