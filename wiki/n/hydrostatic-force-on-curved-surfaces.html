Why F=ρghc​A fails directly on a curved surface

FR​=ρghc​A assumes every elemental pressure force dF=pdA points in the same direction (perpendicular to a flat plane), so they can be summed as scalars. On a curved surface the normal direction changes from point to point, so the elemental forces must be summed as vectors — resolve into horizontal and vertical components and treat each separately.

Horizontal component

Equal to the hydrostatic force on the vertical projection of the curved surface — i.e. treat the shadow the curved surface casts on a vertical plane as an ordinary plane surface and apply Hydrostatic Force on Plane Surfaces directly to it:

FH​=γhCG​Aproj​=ρghCG​Aproj​​

Memory: horizontal force → vertical projection.

Vertical component

Equal to the weight of the (real or imaginary) fluid directly above the curved surface, up to the free surface:

FV​=ρgV=γV​

Memory: vertical force → weight of imaginary fluid. (V is imaginary when the curved surface has fluid below it rather than above, e.g. a dam's downstream curve — the "imaginary" fluid volume above the surface up to the free-surface level is used regardless of whether real fluid actually occupies it, because pressure only depends on depth, per Pressure and the Hydrostatic Equation.)

Resultant

FR​=FH2​+FV2​​​,tanα=FH​FV​​​

The line of action of FR​ passes through the point where FH​'s line of action (through the CP of the projected area) intersects FV​'s line of action (through the centroid of the pressure volume) — geometrically found, not by a single closed-form "CP of the curved surface" formula.
