Composite objects

Break the object into simple shapes with known individual centroids. For each part i, find its mass (or, for uniform density, its area/volume) mi​ and centroid coordinates xi​,yi​, then:

xCM​=∑mi​∑mi​xi​​​,yCM​=∑mi​∑mi​yi​​​

For uniform density, areas/volumes substitute directly for masses: xC​=∑Ai​∑Ai​xi​​​ (2D) or xC​=∑Vi​∑Vi​xi​​​ (3D).

Holes and cutouts

Treat a hole as a negative area/volume: Ahole​<0​, then apply the same weighted-average formula xC​=∑Ai​∑Ai​xi​​ unchanged — extremely useful for plates with circular or rectangular cutouts, since it avoids re-deriving the centroid formula for the resulting irregular shape from scratch.

Cross-domain: this is superposition, again

Composite-body centroids and hole subtraction are a direct instance of the same linear superposition principle used throughout Unsymmetric and Combined Bending (superposing stress states) and AC network superposition (superposing circuit responses): break a complicated linear problem into simple pieces whose individual answers are known, then add them with the correct sign (a hole subtracts, exactly as a "negative source" would in electrical superposition of two sources with opposite polarity). See Exponential Decay — Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation's closing point that linearity is why superposition works at all, across every one of these domains.
