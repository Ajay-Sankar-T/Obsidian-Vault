Motion of a Fluid Element

conceptfluid-mechanicskinematicsdeformation

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Differential Continuity Equation, Material Derivative · formulas: Formula Bank §4

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: a small fluid element, over a short time, can only translate, rotate, stretch (linear deformation) and shear (angular deformation), and the velocity-gradient tensor splits exactly into a part for each.

Intuition: pick a small square of fluid and watch where its corners go. A uniform velocity just carries it away. Differences in velocity from corner to corner turn it, stretch or squeeze its sides, and change its right angles. Only the last two change its shape, and only those create viscous stress.

Governing Relation

Velocity-gradient tensor, split into symmetric and antisymmetric parts:

∇V=ε˙ (strain rate)21​(∇V+∇VT)​​+rotation21​(∇V−∇VT)​​

Motion 2-D expression Meaning
Translation (u,v) element moves with the local velocity
Linear deformation ε˙xx​=∂x∂u​, ε˙yy​=∂y∂v​ rate of stretch of a line along each axis
Dilatation (volume change) ∇⋅V=ε˙xx​+ε˙yy​+ε˙zz​ zero for incompressible flow
Angular deformation ε˙xy​=21​(∂x∂v​+∂y∂u​), engineering γ˙​xy​=2ε˙xy​ rate at which a right angle closes
Rotation ωz​=21​(∂x∂v​−∂y∂u​) mean turning rate of two perpendicular lines

Vector form: ω=21​∇×V (vorticity is ζ=2ω).

Newtonian link: viscous shear stress is τxy​=μγ˙​xy​: only deformation, never rotation, produces viscous stress (see Newtonian Constitutive Relation).

Where: ε˙ij​ = rate-of-strain tensor (1/s); ωz​ = angular velocity of the element (rad/s).

Assumptions: small element, short time, smooth velocity field.

Visual

Figure: the four elementary motions (dashed = at t, filled = at t+dt). Only angular deformation closes the right angle without turning the axes; rotation turns the element without distorting it.

Figure: simple shear u=γ˙​y has straight streamlines yet is rotational: it equals a clockwise rotation ωz​=−γ˙​/2 plus a pure strain ε˙xy​=γ˙​/2.

When To Use

  • Computing strain rates, shear rates and dilatation from a velocity field.
  • Deciding whether a flow is rotational (Vorticity and Circulation).
  • Building the Newtonian stress from the velocity field.

Recognition pattern: "rate of deformation", "angular velocity of a fluid element", "rate of change of volume", "shear strain rate" → element motion.

Method

  1. Identify the velocity components and their spatial derivatives.
  2. Choose the quantity wanted: normal strain rates (ux​,vy​,wz​), shear strain rate (sum of cross derivatives), rotation (difference of cross derivatives).
  3. Apply the formulas; take the symmetric combination for deformation and the antisymmetric for rotation.
  4. Check: dilatation =ux​+vy​+wz​ must vanish for incompressible flow; a rigid-body rotation u=−Ωy, v=Ωx has zero strain rate and ωz​=Ω.

Derivation / Why It Works

Expand the velocity near the element centre: V(x+dx)=V+∇V⋅dx. Every matrix is the sum of its symmetric and antisymmetric parts; the antisymmetric part acts on dx as ω×dx (rigid rotation, no change of length), and the symmetric part changes lengths and angles. The sum of the trace gives the volume change rate ∇⋅V.

Common Traps

  • Trap: equating "straight streamlines" with "no rotation".
  • Why: shear flow is rotational.
  • Avoid it by: computing vx​−uy​ explicitly.
  • Trap: mixing the tensor shear strain rate ε˙xy​ and the engineering one γ˙​xy​=2ε˙xy​.
  • Trap: using ω where vorticity ζ=2ω is intended.
  • Trap: attributing viscous stress to rotation.

Example

Given

The planar velocity field u=3y, v=2x (in s⁻¹ units), and, separately, the stagnation flow u=2x, v=−2y.

Solution

Field 1: uy​=3, vx​=2.

ωz​=21​(vx​−uy​)=−0.5 rad/s,ζz​=−1 s−1,γ˙​xy​=uy​+vx​=5 s−1,∇⋅V=0.

Field 2: ε˙xx​=2, ε˙yy​=−2, ωz​=0, γ˙​xy​=0, ∇⋅V=0. The element is stretched in x, squeezed in y, area conserved, no rotation.

Answer: field 1 is rotational (clockwise) with shear rate 5 s⁻¹; field 2 is a pure strain with constant area. Key takeaway: one velocity gradient can contain both rotation and deformation; separate them by symmetric and antisymmetric parts.

Quick Recall

In one line: ∇V = strain rate (symmetric: stretch and shear) + rotation (antisymmetric); only the strain rate stresses a Newtonian fluid.

Formula / trigger:

ωz​=21​(vx​−uy​),γ˙​xy​=uy​+vx​,∇⋅V=ux​+vy​+wz​

Remember: shear flow rotates; rigid rotation does not deform.
