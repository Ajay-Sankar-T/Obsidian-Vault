Second Moments of Area

fluid-mechanicscentroidssecond-moment-of-area

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · used by: Hydrostatic Force on Plane Surfaces and Centre of Pressure, Stability of Floating Bodies

Definition

IG​=∫y2dA

about a centroidal axis — exactly the same quantity used throughout Flexure Formula in the Strength of Materials vault. This is not a coincidence of notation: both the flexure formula σ=−My/I and the fluid-statics center-of-pressure formula below arise from the identical mathematical operation — integrating a linearly varying quantity (y, or depth-proportional pressure) times a moment arm (y again) over an area.

Standard values (identical table to the Strength of Materials vault)

Rectangle (breadth b, height h, about centroidal axis parallel to b): IG​=12bh3​

Circle (diameter D, about a centroidal diameter): IG​=64πD4​=4πR4​

Parallel axis theorem (needed whenever the reference axis isn't through the centroid, e.g. shifted to the free surface for Centre of Pressure problems): I=IG​+Ad2, where d is the distance between the two parallel axes.

Two unrelated-looking uses of the same integral

  1. Centre of pressure (fluid statics): hCP​=hc​+Ahc​IG​​ — the "extra depth" the resultant hydrostatic force acts at, below the centroid, because pressure increases with depth so the lower part of the surface is weighted more heavily.
  2. Bending stress (solid mechanics, Flexure Formula): σ=−My/I — the stress at height y from the neutral axis, because bending strain increases linearly with y so the outer fibres are weighted more heavily.

Both formulas are, structurally, "first moment of a linearly-varying load, normalized by area and by the linear coefficient" — see Hydrostatic Force on Plane Surfaces and Centre of Pressure for the full derivation showing IG​ appears for exactly the same reason in each.

Waterplane second moment (a third use, in stability)

Stability of Floating Bodies — Metacentre uses IO​, the second moment of the waterplane area (the horizontal cross-section at the waterline), not a cross-sectional area — a different physical surface, same mathematical object. For a rectangular waterplane L×b: tilting about the longitudinal axis uses IO​=Lb3/12; tilting about the transverse axis uses IO​=bL3/12. Memory: the dimension perpendicular to the tilt axis gets cubed — identical rule to which dimension gets cubed in the beam-bending IG​=bh3/12 table.
