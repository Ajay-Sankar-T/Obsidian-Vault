Angular Momentum Principle

conceptfluid-mechanicscontrol-volumeangular-momentum

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: Reynolds Transport Theorem, Linear Momentum Equation · next: Moving and Accelerating Control Volumes

Core Idea

One-sentence definition: the net torque on the fluid in a control volume about a fixed point equals the rate of change of the angular momentum stored inside plus the net flux of angular momentum through the surface.

Intuition: linear momentum tells you the force a device must supply to turn a flow. Angular momentum tells you the torque needed to give the flow a swirl. Anything that swirls fluid (pump, turbine, sprinkler, propeller) exerts or receives a torque equal to the swirl it adds per second.

Governing Relation

Take B=r×mV and b=r×V in the Reynolds Transport Theorem, for an inertial control volume and a fixed point O:

∑MO​=∂t∂​∫CV​(r×V)ρdV+∮CS​(r×V)ρ(V⋅n)dA​

Steady, uniform at each port:

∑MO​=out∑​m˙(r×V)−in∑​m˙(r×V)

Planar swirl about a shaft axis (Vθ​ = tangential component of the absolute velocity):

Tshaft on fluid​=m˙(r2​Vθ2​−r1​Vθ1​)​,P=ωT=m˙(U2​Vθ2​−U1​Vθ1​), U=ωr

The last form is the Euler turbomachine equation (head =P/m˙g), developed with velocity triangles in Turbomachines — Euler Equation and Velocity Triangles.

Where:

  • ∑MO​ = sum of external torques (shaft, wall reactions, pressure and body-force moments) about O
  • r = position from O to the point on the surface; m˙ = mass flow through a port
  • Vθ​ = tangential velocity measured in an inertial frame

Assumptions: inertial control volume, fixed moment point, flow uniform over each port (otherwise integrate).

Visual

No new figure: use the sprinkler sketch in the example. Draw the control volume around the rotating arm, mark R, the nozzle speed Vrel​ relative to the arm, and the arm speed ωR; the absolute tangential exit velocity is Vrel​−ωR.

When To Use

  • Torque on a sprinkler, rotating arm, pump or turbine runner, fan, propeller, rocket nozzle on a swivel.
  • Torque on a bend or a vane when the line of action of the force matters (moment arm).
  • Problems that ask for rotation speed at zero or given friction torque.

Recognition pattern: "torque", "power of the turbine/pump", "rotates freely", "tangential velocity" → angular momentum.

Method

  1. Identify the axis and a fixed moment point O on it; draw the control volume around the rotor.
  2. Choose a sign convention for torque (counter-clockwise positive).
  3. Apply at each port: compute m˙, the absolute tangential velocity Vθ​, and the lever arm r; form m˙rVθ​.
  4. Solve ∑M=∑m˙rVθ​∣out​−∑m˙rVθ​∣in​ for the unknown torque or speed.
  5. Check: incoming fluid with no swirl contributes zero; pressures acting at atmospheric produce no net torque; the shaft torque on the fluid is the opposite of the torque on the shaft.

Derivation / Why It Works

For a system of fixed mass, the angular momentum about O changes only by the external torques (Newton's second law moment form, ∑M=dH/dt with H=∫r×Vdm). The RTT converts d/dt of the system quantity into a control-volume derivative plus a surface flux. Internal torques between fluid parcels cancel in pairs, so only external torques remain.

Common Traps

  • Trap: using the velocity relative to the rotating arm for Vθ​.
  • Why: angular momentum is defined with the absolute velocity.
  • Avoid it by: subtracting the wall speed: Vθ​=Vrel​−ωR for a jet leaving backwards.
  • Trap: dropping the sign of the torque (a sprinkler turns opposite to its jets).
  • Trap: taking moments about a moving point or including atmospheric pressure forces that cancel.
  • Trap: treating the fluid torque on the rotor as the torque on the fluid (Newton's third law again).

Example

Given

A two-armed sprinkler. Arms of radius R=0.15 m, two nozzles of diameter 5 mm, total flow Q=3×10−4 m³/s of water (ρ=998 kg/m³), discharging tangentially to atmosphere. Find (a) the torque needed to hold it at rest, (b) the free-running speed with no friction, (c) the speed with a 0.05 N·m bearing friction.

Approach

Water enters on the axis with no angular momentum. Each nozzle passes Q/2 with relative speed Vrel​.

Solution

Vrel​=πd2/4Q/2​=1.9635×10−51.5×10−4​=7.639 m/s,m˙=ρQ=0.2994 kg/s.

(a) At rest: T=m˙RVrel​=0.2994×0.15×7.639=0.343 N·m.

(b) Free spin: no external torque, so m˙R(Vrel​−ωR)=0 and ω=Vrel​/R=50.9 rad/s =486 rpm.

(c) With friction torque Tf​: m˙R(Vrel​−ωR)=Tf​, so Vrel​−ωR=0.05/0.04491=1.113 m/s, giving ω=43.5 rad/s =415 rpm.

Answer: 0.343 N·m, 486 rpm, 415 rpm. Key takeaway: the water leaves with zero absolute swirl when the arm runs freely; friction forces the water to keep some swirl, which is what takes torque out of the stream.
