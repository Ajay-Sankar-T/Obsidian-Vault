Existence, Uniqueness, and Fundamental Sets

Related: Abel's Formula · Existence and Uniqueness (Picard-Lindelof Theorem) (first-order analogue)

Existence and Uniqueness Theorem

Consider y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=R(x), x∈I, y(x0​)=y0​, y′(x0​)=y0′​. If P,Q,R are continuous on an interval I containing x0​, then this IVP has a unique solution y(x) valid on all of I. (Proof not required.)

This underlies all subsequent theorems about the structure of solutions.

Solution Space is a Vector Space

The set S of all solutions of L[y]=y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0 on I forms a vector space over R, under usual addition and scalar multiplication — this follows from the superposition principle and L[0]=0.

  • If P,Q are continuous on I, then dim(S)=2.
  • Any two linearly independent solutions y1​,y2​ form a basis of S, called a fundamental set of solutions.

Worked Example: largest interval of a unique solution

Find the largest interval guaranteeing a unique solution of (x−1)y′′+3y′+(tanx)y=ex, y(2)=1, y′(2)=0.

Standard form: y′′+x−13y′​+x−1(tanx)y​=x−1ex​.

Coefficients undefined at x=1 and at x=2π​+kπ. Nearest singularities to x0​=2: x=2π​≈1.57 (left), x=23π​≈4.71 (right).

Largest interval =(2π​,23π​).

Fundamental Set of Solutions

A pair {y1​,y2​} of solutions of the homogeneous equation is a fundamental set if W(y1​,y2​)(x)=0 for some (equivalently every) x∈I.

Existence of a fundamental set is guaranteed by the existence-uniqueness theorem: take y1​ solving y(x0​)=1,y′(x0​)=0, and y2​ solving y(x0​)=0,y′(x0​)=1. Then W(y1​,y2​)(x0​)=1=0, so {y1​,y2​} is fundamental — this proves existence of a basis for S.

Example: Construct a fundamental set for y′′+y=0 at x0​=0.

  • y1​: y1​(0)=1,y1′​(0)=0⇒y1​=cosx
  • y2​: y2​(0)=0,y2′​(0)=1⇒y2​=sinx
  • W(y1​,y2​)(0)=1=0, so {cosx,sinx} is a fundamental set: y=c1​cosx+c2​sinx.
