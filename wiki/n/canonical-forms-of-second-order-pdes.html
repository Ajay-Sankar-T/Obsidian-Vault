Canonical Forms of Second Order PDEs

differential-equationspdesconceptcanonical-formcharacteristics

Related: PDEs - MOC · Classification of Second Order PDEs · D'Alembert's Solution of the Wave Equation · Lagrange's Method for Quasi-linear PDEs · solved in Assignment 8 - First Order PDEs and Canonical Forms - Solutions Q5, Q6

What it is

A change of independent variables ξ=ξ(x,y), η=η(x,y) that reduces auxx​+buxy​+cuyy​+⋯=g to its simplest form:

Type Canonical form
hyperbolic uξη​=Φ(ξ,η,u,uξ​,uη​) (or equivalently uξξ​−uηη​=…)
parabolic uηη​=Φ(ξ,η,u,uξ​,uη​)
elliptic uξξ​+uηη​=Φ(ξ,η,u,uξ​,uη​)

The goal: integrate the reduced equation, or recognise it as a known one (e.g. uξη​=0 gives u=f(ξ)+g(η)).

Transformation rules

With u(x,y)=w(ξ,η) the chain rule gives uxx​=wξξ​ξx2​+2wξη​ξx​ηx​+wηη​ηx2​+wξ​ξxx​+wη​ηxx​ and similarly for uxy​,uyy​. The new coefficients are

A=aξx2​+bξx​ξy​+cξy2​,B=2aξx​ηx​+b(ξx​ηy​+ξy​ηx​)+2cξy​ηy​,C=aηx2​+bηx​ηy​+cηy2​,

D=aξxx​+bξxy​+cξyy​+dξx​+eξy​ (coefficient of wξ​),E likewise with η.

To kill wξξ​ take ξ with A=0, i.e. aξx2​+bξx​ξy​+cξy2​=0: ξ= const is an integral curve of the characteristic equation ady2−bdxdy+cdx2=0 (see Classification of Second Order PDEs).

The three recipes

Solve dxdy​=2ab±b2−4ac​​ (if a=0 swap the roles of x,y).

Type Characteristics New variables Result
hyperbolic two real families φ1​=c1​, φ2​=c2​ ξ=φ1​, η=φ2​ A=C=0, divide by B: uξη​+…
parabolic one family φ=c (double root dy/dx=b/2a) ξ=φ, η= any function independent of ξ (e.g. x or y) A=B=0, divide by C: uηη​+…
elliptic complex pair φ=α±iβ=c ξ=α, η=β (real and imaginary parts) A=C, B=0: uξξ​+uηη​+…

Method

  1. Classify; solve the characteristic ODE; build ξ,η.
  2. Compute A,B,C,D,E (the formulas above) and express them in ξ,η (invert x=x(ξ,η), y=y(ξ,η)).
  3. Divide by the leading coefficient; write the canonical form.
  4. Integrate it if possible: uξη​=0⇒f(ξ)+g(η); uηη​=0⇒ηf(ξ)+g(ξ); uηη​+κuη​=0⇒f(ξ)+g(ξ)e−κη.
  5. Substitute back ξ(x,y), η(x,y).

Examples

  • Constant coefficients, hyperbolic 3uxx​+10uxy​+3uyy​=0: dy/dx=3 or 31​; ξ=y−3x, η=3y−x: uξη​=0, u=f(y−3x)+g(3y−x).
  • Parabolic uxx​+2uxy​+uyy​=0: ξ=x−y, η=x: uηη​=0, u=f(x−y)+xg(x−y).
  • Elliptic uxx​+2uxy​+17uyy​=0: dy/dx=1±4i, ξ=y−x, η=4x: uξξ​+uηη​=0 (harmonic in (ξ,η)), so u=F(y+(−1+4i)x)+G(y+(−1−4i)x).
  • Variable coefficients, hyperbolic uxx​−y4uyy​=2y3uy​ (y=0): dy/dx=±y2, ξ=x+y1​, η=x−y1​: uξη​=0 (the first-order term is exactly what makes this integrable), u=f(x+y1​)+g(x−y1​).
  • Euler operator x2uxx​+2xyuxy​+y2uyy​+xux​+yuy​=0 is (x∂x​+y∂y​)2u=0: ξ=y/x, η=lnx, uηη​=0, u=f(y/x)+g(y/x)lnx.
  • Hyperbolic with non-integrable first-order terms: uxx​+yuyy​=0, y<0 gives uξη​+2(ξ−η)uξ​−uη​​=0 (Euler–Poisson–Darboux form), not solvable by elementary means; yuxx​+uyy​=0 (Tricomi) gives uξη​−6(ξ−η)uξ​−uη​​=0 for y<0 and uξξ​+uηη​+3ηuη​​=0 for y>0.

Removing first-order terms (constant coefficients): u=veλξ+μη in uξη​−4uξ​+uη​+3u=0 gives vξη​+(μ−4)vξ​+(λ+1)vη​+(λμ−4λ+μ+3)v=0; choosing μ=4, λ=−1 leaves vξη​+7v=0.

Traps
  • Dividing by B before substituting x,y in terms of ξ,η: the coefficients must be functions of (ξ,η) in the answer.
  • Choosing η not independent of ξ in the parabolic case (the Jacobian must be non-zero).
  • Branches: −y​, y3/2, x3/2 need a sign convention. Solve in each region separately.

Cross-domain links

  • D'Alembert's Solution of the Wave Equation — ξ=x+ct, η=x−ct turns utt​−c2uxx​=0 into uξη​=0: this is the canonical-form method.
  • Eigenvalues, Rotation and Diagonalization Everywhere — a constant-coefficient canonical form is a diagonalisation of the principal-part matrix by a (generally non-orthogonal) change of coordinates.
