Reynolds Transport Theorem

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · builds on: System and Control Volume

The theorem

For a fixed control volume, relating the rate of change of any extensive system property B to the equivalent control-volume bookkeeping:

dtdBsystem​​=∂t∂​∫CV​ρbdV+∮CS​ρb(V⋅n)dA​

where b=B/m is the intensive (per-unit-mass) version of B.

Interpretation

System change=Accumulation in CV+Net outflow through CS​

This is the control-volume generalization of the material derivative: instead of asking "how does a point property change following one particle," RTT asks "how does an extensive property of an entire system change," expressed using only quantities defined on a fixed region in space. Setting B=m,b=1 gives conservation of mass; setting B=mV, b=V gives the linear momentum equation — every conservation law used in this vault is a specific instance of this one theorem.

Cross-domain: RTT is the fluid-mechanics KCL

Compare the "Accumulation = Inflow − Outflow" structure with Nodal and Mesh Analysis's Kirchhoff's Current Law, ∑Ileaving​=0 at a node — KCL is exactly RTT's mass-conservation special case applied to a control volume shrunk down to a single circuit node, with charge playing the role of the conserved extensive quantity B and current playing the role of the flux term ∮ρb(V⋅n)dA. Both are, at bottom, the statement that a conserved quantity cannot accumulate or vanish inside a region without a matching flux crossing its boundary.
