The pattern (Q7)

A uniform bar of length l, cross-section A, modulus E, hangs vertically under its own weight and also carries an end load P. Total elongation:

δ=∫0l​EAN(x)​dx​

— the general varying-N(x) form already established in Axial Deformation.

   P (end load, at bottom, pulling down)
   │
   ▼
  ═══   x = l  (free end)
   │
   │   bar hangs from top,
   │   x measured from bottom
   │
  ═══   x = 0  (fixed support, top)

End-load contribution

The end load creates constant internal force throughout: N(x)=P⇒δP​=EAPl​​ — ordinary uniform-bar elongation.

Self-weight contribution

With γ = weight density (force per volume), the internal force at a section a distance x from the free end must support the weight of everything below it: N(x)=γA(l−x) (maximum at the fixed support, zero at the free end — a bar doesn't need to support its own weight below its very tip).

δW​=∫0l​EAγA(l−x)​dx=Eγ​∫0l​(l−x)dx=Eγ​[lx−2x2​]0l​=Eγ​⋅2l2​

δW​=2Eγl2​​

Combined result

δtotal​=EAPl​+2Eγl2​​

Alternative using total weight W=γAl: δW​=2EAWl​, so δtotal​=EAPl​+2EAWl​​ — note the factor of 21​: a bar under self-weight elongates as much as it would under a concentrated end load equal to half its total weight, because the internal force decreases linearly from full weight at the support to zero at the tip, and the average of a linear ramp is half the maximum. (If γ given is mass density ρ rather than weight density, substitute γ→ρg.)

Cross-domain: this is the "distributed vs concentrated load" idea from beam theory

The 21​ factor here is the axial-loading twin of the pattern used throughout Double Integration and Macaulay's Method and Relations Between Load, Shear, and Moment: integrating a linearly-varying internal force/moment over the member's length always produces a coefficient smaller than the naive "peak value × length" estimate, because the load tapers rather than staying at its maximum throughout — the same reasoning gives the wL2/8 (not wL2/2) maximum moment for a uniformly loaded simply-supported beam.
