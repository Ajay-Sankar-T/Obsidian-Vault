Resonance and Buckling — Instability as an Eigenvalue Problem

← up: Cross-Domain Synthesis MOC

Resonance explorer

Two "critical values" that are the same kind of object

  • Euler Buckling of Columns: below Pcr​=π2EI/L2 the column is stable; at Pcr​ a nontrivial deflected shape becomes possible "at no further load increase."
  • Week 8 / Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits: at ω0​=1/LC​, Z collapses to pure R and current becomes maximal — a "trivial" tiny driving voltage produces a large circulating current/voltage (see the voltage-magnification section of Impedance and Series-Parallel AC Circuits's Q7 discussion).

Both are the point where a linear operator's homogeneous problem stops having only the trivial solution. Formally:

Buckling: (EIdx2d2​+P)v=0, v(0)=v(L)=0⟺eigenvalue problem, eigenvalue P

Resonance: Z(ω)=R+j(ωL−ωC1​)=0 (reactive part)⟺root-finding on Im(Z)

The buckling case is a genuine Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue problem (infinitely many eigenvalues Pn​=n2π2EI/L2, only the smallest, n=1, is physically reached first as load increases). The resonance case is a single algebraic root in ω for a fixed L,C — a lower-dimensional cousin of the same idea, because a simple series RLC circuit has only one reactive balance point, whereas a continuous beam has infinitely many buckling/vibration modes.

The genuinely new idea: a beam is a mechanical resonant circuit with a continuum of modes

If you replace the static buckling load P with a time-varying one and add mass (distributed inertia) to the column, the governing equation becomes a wave/vibration equation whose spatial eigenvalues Pn​=n2π2EI/L2 become, mode-for-mode, a beam's natural (resonant) vibration frequencies ωn​ — the same n2π2EI/L2 expression (via ωn2​∝EI/(mL4)⋅n4 for a beam, or the simpler ωn​∝nπ/L⋅EI/m​ for the equivalent string-like case) reappears as a resonant frequency rather than a critical load. This means: Euler Buckling of Columns's Pcr​ formula and a beam's fundamental resonant frequency are computed from the exact same boundary-value eigenvalue problem, just read along different axes (load-axis vs frequency-axis) — a fact your Strength of Materials vault doesn't currently state because it treats buckling as purely static, but which is the reason real slender structures (bridges, tall columns, transmission lines) must be checked against both static buckling and dynamic resonance (wind-induced vibration, e.g. Tacoma Narrows) using literally the same EI-based eigenvalue machinery.

Practical crossover: why a "stiffer" fix works for both problems

Euler Buckling of Columns's design note says raising Pcr​ requires increasing EI or reducing L/K. The resonance analogue: raising a circuit's ω0​ requires reducing L or C (its "electrical compliance"), which by The Mechanical-Electrical Analogy corresponds to reducing mechanical compliance, i.e. making a mechanical system stiffer, exactly matching the buckling fix. The universal design rule that falls out: in any second-order system prone to instability or unwanted resonance, the fix is always the same — increase the operator's own stiffness term relative to its mass/inertia term; adding damping (R, or structural damping) helps resonance amplitude but does nothing to move Pcr​ or ω0​ themselves, a point already made in The Universal Second-Order Linear System.
