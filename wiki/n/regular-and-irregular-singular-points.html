Regular and Irregular Singular Points

Related: Ordinary and Singular Points · Radius of Convergence of Series Solutions · Frobenius Theorem

We now study y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0 near points where P,Q are not analytic — singular points of the equation. A simple power series y=∑an​xn may fail to exist at a singular point.

Frobenius (1849–1917) developed a generalized series method for such points, applicable when the singularity is of a mild type called regular. It extends the power series method by allowing a fractional or negative leading power — essential for solving Bessel's equation, Legendre's equation (at x=±1), and Euler (Cauchy-Euler) equations. See Cauchy-Euler Equations in the 2nd Order DE folder for the constant-power-law special case that motivates the Frobenius ansatz. Frobenius solutions appear widely in physics: heat flow, waves, quantum mechanics.

Recap: Ordinary vs Singular

  • x0​ is ordinary if P(x),Q(x) are both analytic at x0​ — a power series y=∑an​(x−x0​)n always solves the ODE there.
  • x0​ is singular if P(x) or Q(x) fails to be analytic there. Singular points arise where the leading coefficient a0​(x) of the ODE vanishes.

Not every singular point behaves the same way: some still permit a generalized series solution; others are too severe for any series method to work. The distinction is based on the growth rate of P(x),Q(x) near x0​.

Regular Singular Point

x0​ is a regular singular point of y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0 if (i) (x−x0​)P(x) is analytic at x0​, and (ii) (x−x0​)2Q(x) is analytic at x0​.

Equivalently: P(x) has at most a simple pole at x0​, and Q(x) has at most a pole of order two there. This mild singularity still permits a Frobenius-type series solution.

Examples:

  • x2y′′+xy′+(x2−n2)y=0 (Bessel's equation) has x0​=0 regular.
  • Legendre's Equation has regular singular points at x=±1.

Irregular Singular Point

If x0​ is singular but not regular, it is an irregular singular point — either (x−x0​)P(x) or (x−x0​)2Q(x) fails to be analytic at x0​.

Example: x3y′′+y′+xy=0 has P(x)=1/x3, so (x−0)P(x)=1/x2 is not analytic at x=0 — hence x=0 is an irregular singular point.

Near an irregular singular point, the Frobenius method generally fails; solutions may involve essential singularities or asymptotic (non-convergent) series, requiring more advanced techniques. Fortunately, most classical ODEs of physics have only regular singular points, making the Frobenius method broadly applicable.

Standard Form Near a Regular Singular Point

Let x=0 be a regular singular point (shift x0​ to the origin if needed). Then xP(x) and x2Q(x) are analytic at x=0:

xP(x)=p0​+p1​x+p2​x2+⋯,x2Q(x)=q0​+q1​x+q2​x2+⋯

Multiplying the ODE by x2:

x2y′′+x[xP(x)]y′+[x2Q(x)]y=0.

This makes the regular singular structure explicit — the equation resembles a Cauchy-Euler equation (recall ax2y′′+bxy′+cy=0 from Cauchy-Euler Equations) but with variable coefficients. This motivates seeking solutions of Euler-type (xr) times a power series — exactly the trial solution Frobenius proposed.
