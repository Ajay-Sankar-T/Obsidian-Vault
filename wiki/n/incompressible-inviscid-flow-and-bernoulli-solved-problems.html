1. Ex. 3.24 Fire hose nozzle: Bernoulli + momentum

Concept. Bernoulli finds the pressure; the control-volume momentum equation turns it into a force.

Given. D1​=10 cm hose, D2​=3 cm nozzle, Q=1.5 m³/min =0.025 m³/s, water to atmosphere (p2​=0 gage), frictionless.

Steps.

  1. V2​=πD22​/4Q​=35.4 m/s, V1​=πD12​/4Q​=3.18 m/s.
  2. Bernoulli: p1​=21​ρ(V22​−V12​)=21​(1000)(35.42−3.22)=6.2×105 Pa gage.
  3. Momentum (flange bolts FB​ pull the nozzle back, p2​=0): FB​=p1​A1​−m˙(V2​−V1​), m˙=25 kg/s, A1​=7.85×10−3 m².

FB​=4872−805=4067 N (≈915 lbf)​

Trap. p1​ is gauge; atmospheric pressure acts on both sides and cancels. This is why a charged hose is hard to hold.

2. FE3.9 Cavitation test in a venturi

Given. 6 cm pipe → 3 cm throat, water, p1​=120 kPa, Q=155 gal/min =9.78×10−3 m³/s; cavitation begins at the throat. Find pv​.

Steps. V1​=3.46 m/s, V2​=13.83 m/s.

p2​=p1​−21​ρ(V22​−V12​)=120000−21​(998)(191.4−11.96)=30500 Pa

Cavitation begins when p2​=pv​: pv​≈31 kPa​ (option (d)). The throat EGL/HGL picture is in the formula bank.

3. FE3.10 Venturi flow rate

Given. 6 cm → 4 cm, p1​=120 kPa, p2​=50 kPa, water, frictionless.

Q=1−β4​A2​​ρ2Δp​​,  β=64​,  β4=0.1975

V2​=998(1−0.1975)2(70000)​​=13.22 m/s,  Q=(1.257×10−3)(13.22)=0.01661 m3/s=263 gal/min​

(option (c)).

4. P3.156 Blimp: stagnation and static pressure

Concept. Stagnation pressure at the nose; velocities are relative to the blimp.

Given. Cruise 75 mi/h =33.5 m/s through sea-level air; a transducer between the nose and the side reads pnose​−pside​=950 Pa.

Steps.

  1. Nose is a stagnation point: pnose​=p∞​+21​ρV2=101350+21​(1.225)(33.5)2=102040 Pa​ (a).
  2. Side: pnose​−pside​=21​ρVside2​⇒Vside​=1.2252(950)​​=39.4 m/s relative to the blimp, directed backward.
  3. In the ground frame the air near the side moves at 39.4−33.5=5.9 m/s​ backward (b).

Trap. The Bernoulli velocity is relative to the body (steady frame); "absolute" velocity requires subtracting the blimp speed.

5. P3.166 Wind-tunnel test section

Given. Open-circuit tunnel draws sea-level air through a contraction; a wall transducer reads 45 mm of water difference between the inside and outside.

Steps.

  1. Δp=ρw​gh=998(9.81)(0.045)=440.6 Pa =patm​−ptest​ (air at rest outside has p0​=patm​).
  2. V=ρa​2Δp​​=1.2252(440.6)​​=26.8 m/s =60 mi/h​ (a).
  3. A model's nose is a stagnation point: pnose​=p0​=patm​=101350 Pa​ (b).

6. Pitot-static tube readings

Air (aircraft probe): p0​−ps​=550 Pa, ρ=1.225:

V=2(550)/1.225​=30.0 m/s(Ma=0.088; compressibility error Ma2/4=0.19%)

Water pipe with mercury U-tube (h=5 cm):

V=2gh(ρρm​​−1)​=2(9.81)(0.05)(13.63−1)​=3.52 m/s

(using ρm​=13600, ρ=998). Forgetting the "−1" gives 3.66 m/s, an 4 % error.

Compressibility at Ma=0.3: (p0​−p)/(21​ρV2)=1.023 ✓: reading 2.3 % high in dynamic pressure, 1.1 % in speed.

7. P3.159 Garden hose pressure

Given. 0.625-in hose, 0.375-in nozzle, the garden is 125 ft from the nozzle exit; losses neglected. Minimum gauge pressure in the hose?

Steps.

  1. Minimum speed to reach 125 ft is for a 45° launch: R=V22​/g⇒V2​=32.2(125)​=63.4 ft/s.
  2. V1​/V2​=(0.375/0.625)2=0.36.
  3. p1​=21​ρ(V22​−V12​)=21​(1.94)(63.4)2(1−0.362)=3398 lbf/ft2=23.6 psig​

8. P3.179 Draining tank

Given. Cylinder diameter D, depth h0​, plug of diameter d removed. Quasi-steady Bernoulli between the surface (speed V1​=−dh/dt) and the jet:

V22​−V12​=2gh,A1​V1​=A2​V2​ ⇒ dtdh​=−(Dd​)21−(d/D)42gh​​​

Separate and integrate (d≪D):

t0​=(dD​)2g2h0​​​​

Example: D=1 m, d=5 cm, h0​=2 m ⇒ t0​=400(0.639)=255 s ≈4.3 min. The exact factor 1−(d/D)4​ differs from 1 by 2×10−5.

9. P3.180 Unsteady Bernoulli: a pipe suddenly opened

Given. Large tank (depth h constant), pipe of length L and area A opened at t=0 to the atmosphere.

Steps. Bernoulli (3.76) from the tank surface (V≈0) to the exit: the unsteady integral is ∫12​∂V/∂tds=LdV/dt (all the acceleration is in the pipe):

LdtdV​+2V2​=gh

Solution with V(0)=0 and V∞​=2gh​:

V(t)=V∞​tanh(2LV∞​​t)​

Check: tanh satisfies the ODE exactly (sympy ✓). Example h=3 m, L=12 m: V∞​=7.67 m/s, time constant 2L/V∞​=3.13 s; at t=3.13 s V=0.76V∞​.

Interpretation. Steady Bernoulli would give V∞​ instantly – the unsteady term explains the inertia of the water column. A closed tank at gage pressure p0​ (P3.181): replace gh by gh+p0​/ρ.

10. Channel flow dropping a step (P3.177 style)

Geometry assumed

The figures for P3.177–3.178 are not available as text; I assumed a rectangular channel whose floor drops by H, with uniform flow and hydrostatic pressure at sections 1 and 2, using the data h1​=1.5 m, H=4 m, V1​=3 m/s. For a rise (bump) change the sign of H.

Steps.

  1. Unit flow q=V1​h1​=4.5 m²/s.
  2. Surface Bernoulli (pressure atmospheric): h1​+2gV12​​+H=h2​+2gh22​q2​, so E=1.5+0.459+4.0=5.959 m.
  3. h23​−Eh22​+2gq2​=0 (cubic) has two positive roots:
root h2​ (m) V2​ (m/s) Fr=V/gh​ regime
shallow 0.432 10.4 5.06 supercritical (fast)
deep 5.93 0.76 0.10 subcritical (slow)

critical depth hc​=(q2/g)1/3=1.27 m. Both satisfy Bernoulli and continuity; which one occurs is set by the downstream conditions (the two are connected by a hydraulic jump, which dissipates energy, so Bernoulli cannot cross it). See the figure in the formula bank (§6).

11. P4.60 / P4.26 Vortex flows and pressure

Free vortex (drain), vθ​=ΩR2/r: irrotational ⇒ same Bernoulli constant everywhere: pressure falls toward the axis as p=p∞​−21​ρvθ2​ at fixed z. Free-surface depth at r=R: zC​=H−Ω2R2/2g (see kinematics problem 12).

Forced vortex, vθ​=Ωr: rotational, Bernoulli does not connect different radii. Use Euler normal to the streamlines: RV2​=Ω2r=ρ1​∂r∂p​ ⇒ p=p0​+21​ρΩ2r2; free surface z=Ω2r2/2g (paraboloid).

Comparison. Same speed at r=R but opposite behaviour inside: free vortex surface plunges toward the axis, forced vortex surface rises away from it. Real vortices (Rankine) have a forced core and a free-vortex outer region.
