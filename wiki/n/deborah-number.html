Deborah Number

fluid-mechanicsdimensionless-numbersviscoelasticity

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · classifies: Newtonian and Non-Newtonian Fluids

Definition

De=tflow​λ​​

  • λ — the material's own relaxation time (how long internal microstructure — polymer chains, suspended particles — takes to relax back toward equilibrium after being disturbed)
  • tflow​ — characteristic timescale of the flow/observation (e.g. 1/γ˙​ for a shear rate γ˙​, or the duration of the experiment)

Memory: De=flow timerelaxation time​ — the direct temporal analogue of Knudsen Number's spatial ratio λ/L.

Regimes

De Behaviour
De≪1 liquid-like — the material fully relaxes long before the flow changes; ordinary viscous (Newtonian) behavior
De∼1 viscoelastic effects important — the material "remembers" its recent deformation history; neither purely viscous nor purely elastic models are accurate
De≫1 elastic/solid-like — the flow changes faster than the material can relax, so it responds like an elastic solid rather than a fluid

The physical punchline: "everything flows, given enough time"

Deborah number is named for the Biblical verse "the mountains flowed before the Lord" — the point being that any material can be made to look either fluid or solid depending purely on the ratio of observation time to its own relaxation time. Silly putty behaves elastically (bounces) under a fast impact (De≫1) but flows like a viscous liquid if left to sit under its own weight for hours (De≪1) — same substance, same λ, different tflow​.

Cross-domain: relaxation time as a universal τ

λ here plays exactly the role of a circuit or structural time constant: compare directly with First-Order AC Transients's τ=RC (how fast a capacitor's voltage relaxes toward its new equilibrium) and Exponential Decay — Time Constants, Damping and Spatial Relaxation's general principle that any linear relaxation process is governed by a ratio of its own internal rate constant to the observer's timescale, never by the size of the disturbance. The Deborah number is the fluid-mechanics member of that same family: De≪1 (fluid-like) is the mechanical-relaxation analogue of "steady state reached" (t≫τ) in an RC circuit, and De≫1 (solid-like) is the analogue of "still mid-transient" (t≪τ).
