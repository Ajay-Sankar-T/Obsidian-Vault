A-Q7 — Ripple, Ripple Factor and Form Factor

EE1100diodestutorial-solutionrectifier

Hub: Tutorial 5 on Diode Circuits and Applications · Previous: A-Q6 - Rectifier Circuits and Waveforms · Next: A-Q8 - Half-Wave Rectifier Filter Capacitor

Define the ripple, ripple factor (RF) and form factor (FF) of a rectifier output. Calculate and compare their values for half-wave and full-wave rectifiers without and with a capacitor filter. Input vs​=Vm​sin314t.

1. Definitions

For the output vo​(t) with period To​:

Vdc​=To​1​∫vo​dt,Vrms​=To​1​∫vo2​dt​

  • Ripple: the ac component left in the dc output, vr​(t)=vo​(t)−Vdc​. Its peak-to-peak value is Vr​ and its rms value is

Vac​=Vrms2​−Vdc2​​.

  • Form factor: FF=Vdc​Vrms​​ (shape of the waveform; FF=1 for pure dc).
  • Ripple factor: RF=Vdc​Vac​​=FF2−1​ (smaller is better; RF=0 for pure dc).

Frequency: ω=314 rad/s so f=50 Hz.

2. Without filter

Half-wave (vo​=Vm​sinωt for 0<ωt<π, else 0):

Vdc​=2π1​∫0π​Vm​sinθdθ=πVm​​,Vrms2​=2π1​∫0π​Vm2​sin2θdθ=4Vm2​​⇒Vrms​=2Vm​​

FF=Vm​/πVm​/2​=2π​=1.571,RF=4π2​−1​=1.211

Full-wave (vo​=∣Vm​sinωt∣):

Vdc​=π2Vm​​,Vrms​=2​Vm​​

FF=22​π​=1.111,RF=8π2​−1​=0.483

3. With a capacitor filter (triangular-ripple approximation)

The capacitor discharges roughly linearly through RL​ for (almost) one output period To​ (=1/f for half-wave, 1/2f for full-wave), losing

Vr​=CIdc​To​​≈fRL​CVm​​ (HW),Vr​≈2fRL​CVm​​ (FW).

The output is then a sawtooth of peak-to-peak Vr​ about Vdc​≈Vm​−Vr​/2, whose rms value is Vr​/(23​):

Vac​≈23​Vr​​,RF=Vdc​Vac​​≈23​(Vm​−Vr​/2)Vr​​ Vr​≪Vm​​ ⎩⎨⎧​23​fRL​C1​43​fRL​C1​​(HW)(FW)​

FF=1+RF2​ ≈1.

Conclusions
  • Without a filter, full-wave has lower RF (0.483 vs 1.211) and higher Vdc​ (twice).
  • A capacitor reduces RF by one to two orders of magnitude; FF → 1.
  • For the same C, full-wave ripple is half that of half-wave (the capacitor discharges for half as long), so RF is also halved.
  • The linear-discharge formulas are slightly pessimistic (they assume discharge for the whole period, but the capacitor recharges earlier).
