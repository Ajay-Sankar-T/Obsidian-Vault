T2-Q9: Switch Closes in an RC Network

EE1100tutorial-solutionDC-circuitstransientsRC-circuit

Hub: Tutorial 2 - Transient Analysis of DC Circuits · Theory: Theory - Transients in RL, RC and RLC Circuits · Previous: T2-Q8 - Switch Opens in an RC Network · Next: T2-Q10 - Series RLC Step Response

Same network as Q8, but the switch is closed at t=0 after having been open for a long time. Determine vC​ and vo​ at (a) t=0−, (b) t=0+, (c) t=1.3 ms. [Sheet: 0,0 V; 0,38.4 V; 70.0,38.4 V. The vC​ at 1.3 ms is revised below.]

Tests: first-order charging, Thevenin resistance seen by the capacitor with an ideal source attached.

Given

Same circuit as T2-Q8 - Switch Opens in an RC Network: the 120 V source in a switched vertical branch at the node between 250 Ω and 600 Ω, with 4 μF ∥ 1250 Ω on the left and 2 kΩ, 100 Ω, 400 Ω on the right.

Principle and why it applies

For t<0 the source is disconnected, so with nothing driving it the capacitor is uncharged: vC​(0−)=0. At t=0+ the voltage vC​ stays 0 but the ideal source clamps the middle node at 120 V instantly, so the right-hand network sees a fixed 120 V and vo​ jumps to its steady value at once. The capacitor then charges through the Thevenin resistance of the left-hand network.

Diagram

Solution

(a) t=0−: the source is disconnected and the circuit has no stored energy: vC​=0, vo​=0.

(b) t=0+. vC​=0. The middle node is at 120 V, so vo​ is exactly the DC value found in Q8:

vo​(0+)=120⋅600+400400​⋅500400​=38.4 V.

The source clamps the node, so vo​ is independent of the capacitor for all t≥0+ (the left and right halves are decoupled by the ideal source): vo​=38.4 V for all t>0.

(c) t=1.3 ms. Seen from the capacitor, the left-hand network is the 1250 Ω in parallel with the 250 Ω to the 120 V node (clamped, so it acts like a 250 Ω to a 120 V source):

Vth​=120⋅1250+2501250​=100 V,Rth​=1250∥250=208.3 Ω,τ=208.3×4×10−6=0.833 ms.

vC​(t)=100(1−e−t/0.833ms) ⇒ vC​(1.3 ms)=100(1−e−1.56)=78.99 V.

Result

vC​ vo​
0− 0 0
0+ 0 38.4 V
1.3 ms 78.99 V 38.4 V

The sheet's vC​(1.3 ms)=70.0 V

With the time constant τ=0.833 ms that follows from the circuit, 100(1−e−1.3/0.833)=78.99 V; 70.0 V would need τ=1.08 ms (R=270 Ω), which no combination of the given resistors produces. The Q8 value 59.45 V matches its time constant exactly, so the circuit reading is right and the printed 70.0 is taken as a slip. The final value 100 V and vo​=38.4 V agree with the sheet.

Check

The final value 100 V equals vC​(0−) of Q8 (same DC steady state with the source connected) ✓. At 5τ = 4.2 ms the capacitor is at 99.3 V. The charging current at 0+ is Vth​/Rth​=100/208.3=0.48 A.

Trap / alternative

  • With an ideal voltage source across the middle node, vo​ cannot depend on C for t>0; students often make vo​ vary.
  • The initial vC​=0 is only valid because the circuit has been open "for a long time": a long-open switch means all stored energy has leaked away.
  • The time constant uses the Thevenin resistance with the source killed (short), which is why the 250 Ω ends up in parallel with the 1250 Ω here (in Q8 it was in series).

Concepts used

  • Thevenin resistance, first-order charging, ideal source clamping (Theory - Transients in RL, RC and RLC Circuits)
