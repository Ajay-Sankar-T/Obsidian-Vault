Operational Amplifiers: models and circuit analysis

EE1100op-amptheory

Part of Tutorial 6 on OP-AMP. Course map: EE1100 MOC.

1. What an op-amp is

A high-gain differential amplifier with two inputs, inverting (−) and non-inverting (+), and one output. The output is vo​=A(v+​−v−​)=Avd​ with A∼105 or more, limited only by the supply rails (±Vsat​). With no feedback, any millivolt input saturates it, so every useful circuit feeds the output back to the inverting input (negative feedback).

Ideal op-amp (Lecture 32): infinite open-loop gain, infinite input impedance (zero input current), zero output impedance, infinite bandwidth and slew rate, zero offset, infinite CMRR.

2. The two analysis rules (negative feedback, linear region)

  1. v+​=v−​ (the virtual short): A→∞ and vo​ is finite, so vd​=vo​/A→0.
  2. i+​=i−​=0: no current flows into either input.

Everything in this tutorial follows from KCL at the inverting node plus these two rules. Note that the rules apply only with negative feedback. A positive-feedback or open-loop op-amp saturates.

4. Real op-amp

Finite A, input resistance Ri​, output resistance Ro​: the model is Ri​ between the inputs and a source Avd​ in series with Ro​ (T6-Q7 - Follower with Finite Gain). The closed-loop gain is only slightly below its ideal value when A≫ the loop requirement.

CMRR (Lecture 33). The output responds to the differential input vd​=v1​−v2​ with gain Ad​ and to the common-mode input vc​=2v1​+v2​​ with gain Ac​: vo​=Ad​vd​(1+ρ1​vd​vc​​), ρ=∣Ad​/Ac​∣ (CMRR; in dB, 20log10​ρ; 100 dB means ρ=105). Example from the lecture: inputs 50 and −50 μV give vd​=100 μV, vc​=0 and vo​=100Ad​ μV. Inputs 1050 and 950 μV have the same vd​ but vc​=1000 μV and vo​=100Ad​(1+10/ρ) μV: an error of 10 %, 1 %, 0.1 % for ρ=102,103,104. High CMRR needs matched transistors and costs more.

5. Choosing resistors

Use standard values (E12/E24 series: 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.2, 2.7, 3.3, 3.9, 4.7, 5.6, 6.8, 8.2 × decade). Keep resistances between about 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ (lower loads the source and the op-amp output, higher lets input bias current and noise matter). Ratios matter, not absolute values, so scale a design to suit.
