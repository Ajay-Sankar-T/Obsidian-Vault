Knudsen Number

fluid-mechanicsdimensionless-numbers

← up: Fluid Mechanics MOC · governs: Continuum Concept

Definition

Kn=Lλ​​

  • λ — mean free path: the average distance a molecule travels between successive collisions
  • L — characteristic length of the flow/problem

Memory: Kn=flow scalemolecular scale​ — it directly measures how "grainy" the fluid looks relative to the size of the thing you're studying.

Flow regimes

Kn Regime Meaning
<0.01 Continuum molecular graininess totally negligible — continuum hypothesis fully valid
0.01–0.1 Slip flow continuum equations mostly valid, but need modified (slip) boundary conditions at walls
0.1–10 Transitional neither continuum PDEs nor free-molecular kinetic theory alone is accurate
>10 Free molecular molecules travel so far between collisions that they interact with boundaries more than with each other — must use kinetic theory, not continuum PDEs
L, not just λ, controls the regime">

Why L, not just λ, controls the regime

The mean free path λ for a given gas at fixed temperature and pressure is essentially fixed (roughly 68 nm for air at sea level). What changes Kn in practice is L — a full-scale aircraft has Kn≪0.01 (continuum, ordinary aerodynamics applies), while a MEMS micro-channel or a spacecraft in the upper atmosphere can have L small/large enough to push Kn into the slip or free-molecular regime for the same air. This is the same reclassification logic as Deborah Number's regime table: the substance's own microscopic property (λ, or λ the relaxation time) stays fixed, and it is the problem's own scale (L, or tflow​) that determines which physical model applies.
