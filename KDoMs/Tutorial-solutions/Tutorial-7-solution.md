## 1. Theory

### 1.1 Fundamental law of gearing
For constant angular-velocity ratio, the common normal at the point of contact must always pass through a fixed point on the line of centres, the **pitch point P**:

$$
\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_3}=\frac{O_3P}{O_2P}=\text{constant}
$$

### 1.2 The involute profile
The involute of a circle is the path of a point on a taut string unwrapped from the **base circle**:

$$
r=\frac{r_b}{\cos\alpha},\qquad \operatorname{inv}\alpha=\tan\alpha-\alpha
$$

Two involute gears satisfy the fundamental law at **any** centre distance because the common normal is always the common tangent to the two base circles, and that line cuts the line of centres at a fixed point.

### 1.3 Line of action and pressure angle
- The **pressure (action) line** is the common internal tangent to the two base circles.
- The pressure angle $\phi$ is the angle between this line and the common tangent of the pitch circles.
- Contact occurs only along the segment between the points where the **addendum circles** cut the line.

$$
r_b=r\cos\phi,\qquad \cos\phi=\frac{r_{b2}+r_{b3}}{C}
$$

### 1.4 Standard tooth geometry (20° full-depth, module $m$)

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Pitch radius | $r=mN/2$ |
| Addendum | $a=m$ |
| Dedendum | $b=1.25\,m$ |
| Clearance | $c=b-a=0.25\,m$ |
| Circular pitch | $p=\pi m$ |
| Base pitch | $p_b=p\cos\phi=\pi m\cos\phi$ |
| Base radius | $r_b=r\cos\phi$ |
| Centre distance | $C=m(N_2+N_3)/2$ |
| Velocity ratio | $\omega_2/\omega_3=N_3/N_2=r_{b3}/r_{b2}$ |

### 1.5 Path of contact and contact ratio

$$
Z=\sqrt{r_{a2}^2-r_{b2}^2}+\sqrt{r_{a3}^2-r_{b3}^2}-C\sin\phi,\qquad
m_c=\frac{Z}{p_b}
$$

$m_c$ is the average number of tooth pairs in contact; it must exceed 1, and $\gtrsim1.4$ is comfortable. The path splits at the pitch point into an **approach** part and a **recess** part.

### 1.6 Interference and undercutting

The involute exists only **outside** the base circle. The pressure line touches the base circles at $T_2$ and $T_3$, the **interference points**, so involute contact is possible only on the segment $T_2T_3$.

> [!important] Interference criterion
> **Interference occurs if an addendum circle cuts the pressure line beyond a tangent point.** The tooth tip then digs into the non-involute part of the mating flank (below its base circle).
> For gear 2 driving gear 3, check both: $\sqrt{r_{a2}^2-r_{b2}^2}\le C\sin\phi$ and $\sqrt{r_{a3}^2-r_{b3}^2}\le C\sin\phi$.

**Minimum teeth (pinion against a rack, full depth, addendum $km$):**

$$
N_{\min}=\frac{2k}{\sin^2\phi}\quad\Rightarrow\quad 17.1\ (20^\circ),\ \ 31.9\ (14.5^\circ),\ \ 11.2\ (25^\circ)
$$

**Pinion against a gear of ratio $m_g=N_3/N_2$:**

$$
N_{2,\min}=\frac{2k}{(1+2m_g)\sin^2\phi}\Big[m_g+\sqrt{m_g^2+(1+2m_g)\sin^2\phi}\Big]
$$

For equal gears this gives 12.3 → 13 teeth, and for $m_g=2.5$ it gives 14.6 → 15 teeth.

When a tooth is generated by a rack cutter or hob and $N<17$, the cutter removes the root of the tooth. This is **undercutting**. It weakens the tooth, but it also removes the interfering material.

## Q1 – Is interference a problem?

> [!question] Question
> For the figure shown, will interference be a problem or not? Justify.

**Reading the figure.** The pressure line touches base circle 2 at the point labelled **A** and base circle 3 at **B**. These are the interference points (the radial lines $O_2A$ and $O_3B$ are drawn perpendicular to the pressure line). The addendum circles cut the pressure line at **C** (addendum circle 3) and **D** (addendum circle 2).

Along the pressure line the order is

$$
C\ \longrightarrow\ A\ \longrightarrow\ \cdots\ \longrightarrow\ B\ \longrightarrow\ D
$$

so $C$ lies **beyond** $A$ and $D$ lies **beyond** $B$.

> [!success] Answer: **Yes – interference is a problem**
> - The addendum circle of gear 3 crosses the line at C, outside the tangent point A on base circle 2. The tip of gear 3 therefore touches profile 2 **below its base circle**, where profile 2 is not an involute (the figure labels this: "this portion of profile 2 is not an involute").
> - The addendum circle of gear 2 crosses at D, outside B. The tip of gear 2 touches the non-involute part of profile 3.
> - Contact outside $AB$ violates the conjugate action: the common normal no longer passes through P, so the velocity ratio fluctuates and the tips gouge the flanks, or the gears jam.

**Why it happens.** The pressure line is short (its length between tangent points is $C\sin\phi$) while the addendum circles are large. Small tooth numbers make the base circles small, so the tangent points $A$, $B$ come close to P.

**Remedies** are in Q2.

## Q2 – Ways to prevent interference

> [!question] Question
> State if true or false. If true, list disadvantages.

|     | Statement                                   | T/F      | Why it works                                                                                   | Disadvantages                                                                                                                                            |
| --- | ------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a   | Undercut the tooth                          | **True** | The cutter removes the interfering root material, so the mating tip has room.                  | Weaker tooth root; shorter involute and **lower contact ratio**; stress concentration; fatigue life drops                                                |
| b   | More teeth on pinion and gear               | **True** | $N_{\min}$ is reached (≥ 17 vs rack); bigger base circle moves $T_2,T_3$ outward               | Larger pitch diameters at fixed module; **heavier, costlier**; higher pitch-line speed and **noise**; smaller module (weaker teeth) if the size is fixed |
| c   | Larger pressure angle (smaller base circle) | **True** | $r_b=r\cos\phi$ falls, so the involute starts closer to the root; $N_{\min}$ drops (11 at 25°) | **Larger radial and bearing loads** ($F\tan\phi$); lower contact ratio; more noise and friction; non-standard hobs; shafts must be stiffer               |
| d   | Shorter (stub) addendum                     | **True** | The addendum circle is smaller, so it crosses the line before the tangent point                | **Lower contact ratio** (rougher, noisier); non-standard cutters; gears not interchangeable; shorter active flank                                        |
``
> [!tip] Extra option (not listed)
> **Profile shifting** (addendum modification): shift the cutter outward on the pinion and inward on the gear. This avoids undercut without changing the module, but it requires a non-standard centre distance.

## Q3 – Increasing the centre distance

> [!question] Question
> The centre distance is increased so there is clearance (backlash). Answer a–g.

Governing relations:

$$
r_b=\tfrac12 mN\cos\phi_0\ (\text{fixed by the cut}),\qquad
\cos\phi'=\frac{r_{b2}+r_{b3}}{C'},\qquad
r'=\frac{r_b}{\cos\phi'}
$$

| | Question | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | Base circle diameters increase? | **No** | They are fixed by the number of teeth, module and the cutting pressure angle ($r_b=\tfrac12 mN\cos\phi_0$). |
| b | Pressure angle increases? | **Yes** | $\cos\phi'=(r_{b2}+r_{b3})/C'$, so a bigger $C'$ lowers $\cos\phi'$ and raises $\phi'$ (the figure shows $\phi'>20^\circ$). |
| c | Pitch circle diameters increase? | **Yes** | The operating pitch circles are $r'=r_b/\cos\phi'$; they grow, but their ratio stays $N_3/N_2$. (The *standard cutting* pitch circles are unchanged.) |
| d | Velocity ratio changes? | **No** | $\omega_2/\omega_3=r_{b3}/r_{b2}=N_3/N_2$, which only depends on the base circles. |
| e | Contact ratio? | **Decreases** | $Z$ falls because $C'\sin\phi'$ rises while the addendum circles are fixed, and $p_b$ is unchanged. |
| f | Helps interference? | **Yes** | The tangent-to-tangent length $C'\sin\phi'$ increases, so the addendum circle cuts the line *inside* $T_2T_3$ sooner. Cost: less overlap, backlash, higher bearing load. |
| g | Fundamental law satisfied? | **Yes** | Involutes stay conjugate at any centre distance; the common normal is still the common tangent to the base circles, so P is fixed. |

### Numerical illustration (the 24/36 gears of Q7, $m=6$, $C=180$ mm)

![[T7-fig3-centre-distance.png]]

Example at $C'=184$ mm (+4 mm): $\phi'=23.2^\circ$, $d_2'=147.2$ mm (was 144), $d_3'=220.8$ mm (was 216), contact ratio $1.65\to1.03$, and the circular backlash is about **3.2 mm**. At $C'\approx184.2$ mm the contact ratio reaches 1, so continuous transmission is lost beyond it.

![[T7-fig4-backlash.png]]

### (f) in numbers – a 12/36 pair that interferes at the standard distance

For $N_2=12$, $N_3=36$, $m=6$ ($C_0=144$ mm) the interference margin is $-2.68$ mm. It becomes zero at only **$C'=144.94$ mm** (+0.94 mm), and the contact ratio there is still 1.41.

![[T7-fig5-interference-vs-C.png]]

> [!note] Takeaway
> A small increase in centre distance removes interference cheaply, which is the basis of profile-shifted ("long-short addendum") gears. A large increase destroys the contact ratio.

## Q4 – Gear pair for C = 84 mm, speed ratio 0.4

> [!question] Question
> Parallel shafts 84 mm apart. Output speed = 40 % of input. Choose a module from the table (non-unique). Check undercutting.

**Teeth relation.** Output slower, so $N_3>N_2$:

$$
\frac{\omega_3}{\omega_2}=\frac{N_2}{N_3}=0.4\ \Rightarrow\ N_3=2.5\,N_2
$$

$$
C=\frac{m(N_2+N_3)}{2}=\frac{m\cdot3.5N_2}{2}=1.75\,m\,N_2=84\ \Rightarrow\ \boxed{m\,N_2=48\ \text{mm}}\quad N_3=\frac{120}{m}
$$

Both $N_2=48/m$ and $N_3=120/m$ must be integers. Scanning the table (preferred and next choice) gives:

| m (mm) | $N_2$ | $N_3$ | Contact ratio | Undercut check (rack limit 17.1 → 18; pair limit 14.6 → 15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 48 | 120 | 1.81 | OK |
| **1.5** | 32 | 80 | 1.75 | OK |
| **2** | 24 | 60 | 1.69 | OK ✔ |
| 3 | 16 | 40 | 1.61 | Interference-free against this gear (16 ≥ 15), but **undercut if hobbed** (16 < 17) – marginal |
| 4 | 12 | 30 | 1.54 | **Undercut / interference** |
| 6 | 8 | 20 | 1.43 | **Undercut / interference** |
| 8 | 6 | 15 | 1.35 | **Undercut / interference** |
| 12 | 4 | 10 | 1.25 | **Undercut / interference** |

Other table values (1.125, 1.25, 1.375, 1.75, 2.25, 2.5, 2.75, 3.5, 4.5, 5.5, …) give a non-integer $N_2$, so they are rejected.

![[T7-fig7-q4.png]]

> [!success] Answer
> Any of **m = 1, 1.5, 2 mm** works. A good choice is **m = 2 mm, $N_2=24$, $N_3=60$**, with $C=2(24+60)/2=84$ mm ✔, $\omega_3/\omega_2=24/60=0.4$ ✔, contact ratio 1.69, and no undercutting because $24>17.1$.
> Modules 4, 6, 8 and 12 are rejected because their pinions have fewer than 15–17 teeth.

## Q5 – Module from centre distance

$$
C=\frac{m(N_2+N_3)}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ m=\frac{2C}{N_2+N_3}=\frac{2(92)}{32+84}=\frac{184}{116}=\mathbf{1.586\ mm}
$$

![[T7-fig8-q5.png]]

> [!note] Remarks
> - 1.586 mm is not a standard metric module; it equals 25.4/16 = 1.5875 mm, i.e. a **16 diametral-pitch** gear, so these are inch-series gears (check: $C=58\times1.5875=92.08\approx92$ mm).
> - If instead standard $m=1.5$ gears were used, the standard centre distance would be 87 mm. Mounting them at 92 mm would need $\cos\phi'=87\cos20^\circ/92$, i.e. $\phi'=27.3^\circ$, with a much lower contact ratio and large backlash.

## Q7 – Full tooth data for a 24/36 pair, m = 6

> [!question] Question
> $m=6$ mm, 24-tooth pinion drives a 36-tooth gear, 20° full-depth involute. Tabulate addendum, dedendum, clearance, circular pitch, base pitch, base circle radii, length of path of contact and contact ratio.

**Step 1 – pitch circles.**

$$
r_2=\frac{6\times24}{2}=72\ \text{mm},\quad r_3=\frac{6\times36}{2}=108\ \text{mm},\quad C=180\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 2 – tooth proportions.**

$$
a=m=6,\qquad b=1.25m=7.5,\qquad c=b-a=1.5\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 3 – pitches.**

$$
p=\pi m=18.850\ \text{mm},\qquad p_b=p\cos20^\circ=18.850\times0.93969=17.713\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 4 – base and addendum radii.**

$$
r_{b2}=72\cos20^\circ=67.658\ \text{mm},\qquad r_{b3}=108\cos20^\circ=101.487\ \text{mm}
$$

$$
r_{a2}=72+6=78\ \text{mm},\qquad r_{a3}=108+6=114\ \text{mm}
$$

**Step 5 – path of contact.**

$$
\sqrt{r_{a2}^2-r_{b2}^2}=\sqrt{78^2-67.658^2}=38.813,\qquad
\sqrt{r_{a3}^2-r_{b3}^2}=\sqrt{114^2-101.487^2}=51.927
$$

$$
C\sin20^\circ=180\times0.34202=61.564
$$

$$
Z=38.813+51.927-61.564=\mathbf{29.176\ mm}
$$

Approach part $=51.927-r_{b3}\tan20^\circ=14.99$ mm, recess part $=38.813-r_{b2}\tan20^\circ=14.19$ mm.

**Step 6 – contact ratio.**

$$
m_c=\frac{Z}{p_b}=\frac{29.176}{17.713}=\mathbf{1.647}
$$

### Results table

| Quantity | Pinion (24) | Gear (36) |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch radius (mm) | 72 | 108 |
| Addendum $a$ (mm) | 6 | 6 |
| Dedendum $b$ (mm) | 7.5 | 7.5 |
| Clearance $c$ (mm) | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| Addendum radius (mm) | 78 | 114 |
| Root radius (mm) | 64.5 | 100.5 |
| Base-circle radius (mm) | **67.658** | **101.487** |
| Circular pitch $p$ (mm) | 18.850 | 18.850 |
| Base pitch $p_b$ (mm) | 17.713 | 17.713 |
| **Length of path of contact $Z$** | **29.18 mm** (approach 14.99 + recess 14.19) | |
| **Contact ratio** | **1.647** | |

**Checks.** Interference: $38.813<61.564$ and $51.927<61.564$, so both addendum circles meet the line inside $T_2T_3$ and there is **no interference** (also $24>17.1$ teeth). The root radius of the pinion (64.5) lies below its base radius (67.66), which is normal for 24 teeth.

## Appendix

### Core code (Python)

```python
import numpy as np
phi = np.radians(20)

def gear(N, m):
    r = m*N/2
    return dict(r=r, rb=r*np.cos(phi), ra=r+m, rf=r-1.25*m)

g2, g3 = gear(24, 6), gear(36, 6)
C  = g2['r'] + g3['r']
Z  = np.sqrt(g2['ra']**2 - g2['rb']**2) + np.sqrt(g3['ra']**2 - g3['rb']**2) - C*np.sin(phi)
pb = np.pi*6*np.cos(phi)
print(Z, Z/pb)            # 29.176  1.647

# minimum pinion teeth against a gear of ratio mg
def nmin(mg, k=1.0):
    s2 = np.sin(phi)**2
    return 2*k/((1+2*mg)*s2)*(mg + np.sqrt(mg**2 + (1+2*mg)*s2))
print(nmin(2.5))          # 14.64
```
