Related: [[Series Solutions About x0 not equal to 0]] · [[Ordinary and Singular Points]] · [[Legendre's Equation]] · [[Airy's Equation]]

The series solution about $x_0$ converges at least for $|x-x_0|<R$, where
$$R = \text{distance from } x_0 \text{ to the nearest singular point (real or complex)}.$$

This estimate is obtained **without solving the equation explicitly** — just from locating the zeros of the leading coefficient $a_0(x)$ (equivalently, the poles of $P(x)$ or $Q(x)$).

**Examples:**
- [[Legendre's Equation]] $(1-x^2)y''-2xy'+n(n+1)y=0$ has singular points $x=\pm1$. Series about $x_0=0$ converges for $|x|<1$.
- [[Airy's Equation]] $y''-xy=0$ has **no** singular points at all. So the series solution converges for every real $x$ — matching [[Hermite's Equation]], which similarly has no singular points.
