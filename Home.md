## How To Use This Vault

1. Use the subject MOCs as the navigation spine.
2. Keep each concept note atomic: one idea, one definition, one method, or one solved pattern.
3. Add links where ideas repeat across subjects, especially through [[Cross-Domain Synthesis/Cross-Domain Synthesis - MOC|Cross-Domain Synthesis]].
4. Prefer precise technical connections over vague similarity.
5. When adding notes with an LLM, update the nearest MOC in the same pass.

## Active Learning Areas

- Differential equations: first-order ODEs, second-order ODEs, series solutions, special functions, partial differential equations (first-order PDEs, wave, heat and Laplace equations), and solved assignments 1–10.
- Physics I: vectors and frames, Newton's laws in polar coordinates, SHM, damped and driven oscillators, phase portraits and the nonlinear pendulum, conservative forces, Euler–Lagrange equations, Kepler orbits and Hohmann transfers, divergence theorem, Stokes' theorem and Bernoulli.
- Engineering mechanics, drawing and the two labs: free-body diagrams, trusses and frames, friction (belts, screws), virtual work, projectile and rigid-body dynamics, vibration; drawing standards; strain-gauge, grating, Stefan's-law and other physics experiments; beam, strut, tensile and torsion experiments.
- Electrical engineering: DC circuits, AC phasors, transients, RLC response, transformers, and machines.
- Strength of materials: stress-strain, axial loading, torsion, beams, deflection, buckling, energy methods, and pressure vessels.
- Fluid mechanics: fundamentals, dimensionless numbers, statics, kinematics, control volume analysis, centroids, dimensional analysis, differential kinematics, Bernoulli, and pipe flow with losses (GPS 7 and 8).
- Kinematics and dynamics of machinery: velocity/acceleration analysis, mechanism synthesis, gears.
- Diodes and rectifiers: EE1100 Tutorial 5. Operational amplifiers: Tutorial 6. Transformers, voltage regulation and efficiency: Tutorial 7, with concept notes on induction machines (slip, rotating field, power flow).
